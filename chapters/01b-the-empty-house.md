# The Empty House {#the-empty-house}

Jesus once told a story about a house that was cleaned out and then left empty.

An unclean spirit goes out of a man. It wanders about, finds nowhere to rest, and says, "I will return into my house from which I came out". It comes back and finds the house "empty, swept, and put in order". So it goes and fetches seven other spirits, each one worse than itself, and they all move in. Jesus finishes the story in one sentence: "The last state of that man becomes worse than the first."

You don't have to believe in spirits to know that house, and the story isn't here to tell you that you have one. It is here because nobody has ever described quitting by force more exactly.

You have swept that house many times.

You swore off porn, and for three weeks you were clean. Then you noticed you were spending an hour a night scrolling past women in swimsuits and calling it nothing. Or you deleted every app and blocked every site, and the reel in your memory started up in their place. Or you stopped the whole business by force, and poured the same hunger into chasing a woman who didn't want you. You threw one thing out, and it came back with company. That is how it goes with a squatter. Keep his room ready, and he brings his friends. They are as small as he is, and they smell just as bad.

No wonder it feels unbeatable. No wonder you decided the fault was in you.

It can feel like being possessed. Something is running you that you never invited, and you want it out. That is an honest way to say how it feels, and there is no shame in it. But keep two things straight. He has never once made you do it. Every time, he asked, and you were the one who answered. And he is not ten feet tall. He is a pipsqueak, squatting in a back room.

Now look at the one detail in the story that explains everything. Why could it come back? Because the house was empty.

The man did all the right things, as far as he knew. He put the tenant out. He swept. He tidied. And he left the room standing ready, with the tenant's name still on the door.

That is what quitting by force is. A man stops the act and goes on believing that it gave him something. It relaxed him. It helped him sleep. It made him feel wanted. So there is a gap in his life where it used to be, and a gap gets filled.

This book works the other way around. Before anybody is put out, we look at what this tenant has really done for you all these years. What rent has he paid? What has he ever mended? You will find that he has paid nothing and broken a great deal, and that the room was never short of him. It was never his room. You have been treating him as a tenant, and a tenant who pays nothing is only a squatter.

So why is he still there? A man keeps a squatter for one of two reasons. He believes the squatter gives him something. Or he has grown so used to him that he thinks he has a right to stay. This one has given you nothing, and he has no right. He is a freeloader, and it is your house. He leaves his dishes in your sink, and he smells. And look who has been paying his bills and carrying his meals up the stairs. You have. Nobody else can put him out for you. And you have never truly tried, because some part of you still thought he was worth keeping.

Once a man has seen that, there is no vacancy. There is nothing for the tenant to come back to.

Jesus gave the order of things in one line: "You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free." He was speaking of his own teaching, and a Christian will take it that way. But notice the order, because it holds for any man. Knowing comes first, and freedom follows. He didn't say that effort will make you free.

A Christian will say more. Jesus's story is about a house left empty, and a house isn't meant to stand empty. It has an owner, and he means to live in it. He doesn't come as a substitute for the tenant. He was the owner all along.

Now look at how the squatter lives. He has no body of his own. He can't look at anything, or touch anything, or go anywhere. Everything he gets, he gets with what you lend him: your eyes, your mind, your hand, your feet and your bed. That makes five ways he is fed, and you can count them on one hand.

**Look.** He borrows your eyes. Porn is the big one, in every form it comes in. But the feed full of half-dressed strangers belongs here too. So does the second glance at a woman in the street, the one you take on purpose.

**Think.** He borrows your mind. The private film. The fantasy, the memory played back, the woman from work undressed in your head.

**Touch.** He borrows your hand. For most men this is the way he has been fed most often. It is on the cover of this book.

**Chase.** He borrows your feet. This is lust doing your thinking around real women. The flirting you shouldn't be doing. The message you shouldn't be sending. The woman on a pedestal, and the money spent to make her notice you.

**Take.** He borrows your bed. Sex outside marriage. The one-night stand, the affair, the sex a man pays for. And one case is not like the others, and it gets a fair hearing in this book. It is the bed a man shares with the woman he loves and hasn't married yet.

Look, think, touch, chase, take. It is a ladder, and each rung leads to the next. It is also the oldest story there is. He saw her. He couldn't stop thinking about her. He went after her. He took her.

You may be feeding all five, or only two. It makes no difference. He is fed five ways, and there is one small belly behind them all. That is why you are not going to fight five battles. There is only one squatter, with whatever grubby friends he has brought home. They all eat from the same plate.

It is also why this book asks you not to throw anything out yet. The day will come, near the end, when you show him the door. You'll do it once, and it will be easy, because by then you will know that he has nothing you want. You won't have to drag anyone out. You stop paying his bills and you stop bringing his food, and his friends leave with him.

But before we look at what he has paid, one question has to be settled. Five ways make a long list. Is all of that really lust? Where exactly is the line?
