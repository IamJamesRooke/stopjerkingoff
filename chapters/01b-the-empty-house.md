# The Empty House {#the-empty-house}

> "You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free."
>
> — John 8:32

Jesus once told a story about a house that was cleaned out and then left empty.

An unclean spirit goes out of a man. It wanders about, finds nowhere to rest, and says, "I will return into my house from which I came out". It comes back and finds the house "empty, swept, and put in order". So it goes and fetches seven other spirits, each one worse than itself, and they all move in. Jesus finishes the story in one sentence: "The last state of that man becomes worse than the first."

You don't have to believe in spirits to know that house, and the story isn't here to tell you that you have one. It is here because nobody has ever described quitting by force more exactly.

You have swept that house many times.

You swore off porn, and for three weeks you were clean. Then you noticed you were spending an hour a night scrolling past women in swimsuits and calling it nothing. Or you left the phone in the kitchen at bedtime, and the reel in your memory started up in its place. Or you stopped the whole business by force, and poured the same hunger into chasing a woman who didn't want you. You threw one thing out, and it came back with company.

No wonder it feels unbeatable. No wonder you decided the fault was in you.

Now look at the one detail in the story that explains everything. Why could it come back? Because the house was empty.

The man did all the right things, as far as he knew. He put the tenant out. He swept. He tidied. And he left the room standing ready, with the tenant's name still on the door.

That is what quitting by force is. A man stops the act and goes on believing that it gave him something. It relaxed him. It helped him sleep. It made him feel wanted. So there is a gap in his life where it used to be, and a gap gets filled.

This book works the other way around. Before anybody is put out, we look at what this tenant has really done for you all these years. What rent has he paid? What has he ever mended? You will find that he has paid nothing and broken a great deal, and that the room was never short of him. It was never his room.

Once a man has seen that, there is no vacancy. There is nothing for the tenant to come back to.

Jesus gave the order of things in one line: "You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free." He was speaking of his own teaching, and a Christian will take it that way. But notice the order, because it holds for any man. Knowing comes first, and freedom follows. He didn't say that effort will make you free.

A Christian will say more. Jesus's story is about a house left empty, and a house isn't meant to stand empty. It has an owner, and he means to live in it. He doesn't come as a substitute for the tenant. He was the owner all along.

In Jesus's story the spirit came back with seven others. Count the ways your own tenant is fed, and you will find five. Call them his five mouths. You can count them on one hand.

**Look.** Porn is the big one, in every form it comes in. But the feed full of half-dressed strangers belongs here too. So does the second glance at a woman in the street, the one you take on purpose.

**Think.** The private film. The fantasy, the memory played back, the woman from work undressed in your head.

**Touch.** The hand. For most men this is the mouth that has been fed most often. It is on the cover of this book.

**Chase.** This is lust doing your thinking around real women. The flirting you shouldn't be doing. The message you shouldn't be sending. The woman on a pedestal, and the money spent to make her notice you.

**Take.** Sex outside marriage. The one-night stand, the affair, the sex a man pays for. And the one nobody likes to hear: the woman he loves and means to marry someday.

Look, think, touch, chase, take. It is a ladder, and each rung leads to the next. It is also the oldest story there is. He saw her. He couldn't stop thinking about her. He went after her. He took her.

You may be feeding all five, or only two. It makes no difference. There are five mouths and one small belly behind them. That is why you are not going to fight five battles. There is only one tenant.

It is also why this book asks you not to throw anything out yet. The day will come, near the end, when you show him the door. You'll do it once, and it will be easy, because by then you will know that he has nothing you want.

But before we look at what he has paid, one question has to be settled. Five mouths make a long list. Is all of that really lust? Where exactly is the line?
