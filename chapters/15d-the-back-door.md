# The Back Door {#the-back-door}

> "All things are lawful for me," but I will not be brought under the power of anything.
>
> — 1 Corinthians 6:12

The third mouth needs no screen, no woman and no money. All it needs is a man by himself for five minutes.

For most men it was the first of the five to arrive, and they expect it to be the last to go. This is the hand.

Its lie is different from the lies of the other four. The others say, "This is exciting." This one says, "This is nothing." It's natural. It's free. Nobody gets hurt. It's just maintenance, like trimming your nails.

Some men in Corinth had a slogan like that: "All things are lawful for me." Paul let them have it, and then answered it: "but I will not be brought under the power of anything."

Because the lie is "nothing", this is the one men keep. A man quits porn and is proud of it, and he should be. But he keeps the hand, the way a man gives up whiskey and keeps the beer. He tells himself it is a different thing now.

This chapter is called the back door for a reason. One man quit porn twice, and went back twice. Both times, he said, masturbation was what led him back.

It isn't hard to see how. The hand never works alone. Ask what is playing while it happens, because something always is. It's a memory, or a face from the feed, or a woman from work. The hand is where the look and the thought turn into the act.

Then the memory wears thin. Every scratch works a little less well than the one before, so the hand goes looking for a stronger film, and it knows exactly where the stronger film is kept. The man bolted the front door and left the back door standing open. Then he was surprised to find the same old visitor in his kitchen.

So what has the hand really been paying you all these years?

It was never pleasure. It was relief from an itch, and the last time is what set the itch going. For ten minutes it gets a man back to how the man who never does it feels all day. That is the entire gift.

Then there is what it teaches. A body learns that desire is something to be dealt with quickly, alone, and with nobody to answer to. A man practices that for fifteen years. Then he marries, and wonders why the lesson came with him.

The world calls this self-love. It is a strange kind of love that needs the door locked and leaves you feeling worse than before.

So the hand is not the harmless one. It holds the door open for the other four.

The three so far have something in common. Each has kept to a room with nobody else in it. The next one walks out into company.
