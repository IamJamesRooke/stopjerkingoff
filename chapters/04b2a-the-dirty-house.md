# The Dirty House {#the-dirty-house}

> "They, having become callous, gave themselves up to lust, to work all uncleanness with greediness."
>
> — Ephesians 4:19

In the 1990s Procter & Gamble, the company behind Tide and Pampers, made a spray that wiped out bad smells. It was built to destroy smells, not just cover them up. The company was sure it would sell like crazy.

It didn't sell.

So the company sent people into homes to find out why. One woman's house stank of her cats. The visitors could barely breathe in the living room. She couldn't smell a thing. She'd lived in it so long that her nose had quit reporting it.

That's the whole story of the spray, called Febreze. The people who needed it most couldn't smell what they needed it for. The company learned that people get used to the smells in their own homes. They stop noticing them, even when the smell would make a guest gag.

## The house you live in {-}

Now sniff your own house. Not the one with your name on the mailbox. The one in your head, where the Little Monster lives. Here's what the world carries into it on a normal evening:

- A music video where a singer dances on a church altar.
- A halftime show the whole family watches, and you can't tell if it's a concert or a strip club.
- An ad for burgers sold by a woman in her underwear.
- A video game where the sex scenes come with the price of the game.
- A teen drama where high-school characters get sex scenes.
- A feed that serves you a stranger in a bikini between your cousin's wedding and the weather.

Your grandfather would have walked out of that house holding his nose. You eat dinner in it.

You don't smell it. Most of us don't. That's not because the house is clean. It's because you've lived in it your whole life.

## Past feeling {-}

The Bible has a word for this. Paul, the first-century apostle who wrote much of the New Testament, describes people who "having become callous, gave themselves up to lust." The old King James Bible puts it more sharply: they were "past feeling."

A callus is hard, thick skin that's been rubbed so often it can't feel anything. That's handy on a carpenter's palm. It's a disaster on a conscience.

The prophet Jeremiah, writing about six hundred years before Jesus, said the same of his own people. They did disgusting things, and "neither could they blush."

You don't need the Bible to believe this one. Anyone who's worked in a slaughterhouse, an emergency room or a sewer knows it. The first day turns your stomach. By the third month you eat lunch there. Your senses didn't decide the place got cleaner. They just gave up telling you.

## The man who lingered {-}

There's an old story about a man named Lot. In Genesis, the first book of the Bible, he moved his family to Sodom, a city famous for its filth. Peter, one of Jesus's closest friends, wrote that Lot was "tormented in his righteous soul from day to day with seeing and hearing lawless deeds."

Tormented. Day after day. He hated what he saw.

He stayed anyway.

Then the morning came when the city was going to burn. Two angels told him to grab his family and run. Here's what Genesis says Lot did next:

> "But he lingered."

The angels had to grab him by the hand and drag him out.

![](images/illustrations/dirty-house-lot-lingers.png){.illustration width=80%}

That's what living in a dirty house does to a man. First it disgusts him. Then it bores him. Then he can't see why anyone would leave.

## How your nose got switched off {-}

You weren't born numb. You were trained, one step at a time, by a world that does better when you're numb, and every step was sold as no big deal.

Here's how the training works. A stage puts on something shocking. People gasp. Ten years later it's wallpaper, so the next stage has to go further to get the same gasp.

One example says it all. In 1989 the pop superstar Madonna released a music video called "Like a Prayer." It had burning crosses, and a church scene with bleeding wounds on her hands like Jesus's on the cross. The Vatican, the Pope's own government, condemned it. Pepsi had paid her five million dollars for a commercial. After the protests, it pulled the commercial off the air.

In 2023 Pepsi aired that same commercial again, at the MTV Video Music Awards, a big TV night for pop music. This time nobody blinked.

The commercial hadn't changed in thirty-four years. The audience had.

The world trained you, yes. But nobody else carried those plates up the stairs. You did that yourself, every time. Which means you can stop.

![](images/illustrations/dirty-house-the-shock-ladder.png){.illustration width=80%}

## Fresh air {-}

Here's the good news, and it's very good.

Walk out of a smelly house for a weekend, and come back. The smell hits you at the door. Your nose wasn't broken. It was only asleep.

The same thing happens to a man who stops feeding the Little Monster. He's the pathetic little freeloader in the back room upstairs, who bangs on the floor until you carry up his dinner. Stop the meals, and something strange happens downstairs too. Your nose comes back.

Many men who quit say it surprised them. The billboard they never noticed now looks loud and strange. The music video looks desperate. The show they used to have on in the background makes them reach for the remote. One woman who gave up porn wrote that she hadn't realized how much of it was all around her, until she stopped.

Nobody had to scrub the world for them. They just stopped breathing it in on purpose.

You don't have to fight anything, and you don't have to hide from the world. Just do one thing as you go about your week. Sniff. Notice what's on the screen in the restaurant, the ad before the video, the song in the store. Ask yourself whether your grandfather would have called it normal.

![](images/illustrations/dirty-house-door-open-fresh-air.png){.illustration width=80%}

You'll start to smell it. And once you smell it, you'll want to know who keeps tracking it in.

Some of them are proud of it.
