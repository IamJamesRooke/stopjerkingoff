# The Collector and the Customer {#the-collector-and-the-customer}

The last thing lust borrows is a man's bed. The chase was heading there, and so was everything before it. One night he stops chasing and takes.

Most men who have done it are neither collectors nor customers. They simply went home with someone when the chance came. Ask one of them about it the next morning. He doesn't look like a conqueror. He is looking for his shoes.

There was also a woman in that bed. Perhaps she hoped it meant something, and it didn't. Or perhaps she was using him exactly as he was using her. Then two people took a hit, and nobody was given a gift.

This chapter is for the man who has gone home with a woman he didn't mean to keep. If that has never been you, it is a short one, and you know a man it fits.

Two men have gone further up this road than the rest. Look at them closely, because each has an "at least", and each believes it.

## The collector {-}

The collector's "at least" is that he needs no screen. Real women want him. And he keeps a number. He wouldn't tell his mother what it is, but he knows it exactly. He compares it with other men's, the way boys compare scores.

Ask him about the women and something odd comes out. He didn't like half of them. Some he wasn't even attracted to. He slept with them anyway, because each one moved the number up. And the number told him something he badly needed to hear: women want you, so you must be somebody.

That isn't confidence. It's a man so unsure of his worth that he needs a new woman every few days to vote on it.

Picture a man at a buffet for the first time. He stacks plate after plate. He paid a lot to get in and he may never be back, so he takes everything. Now watch the wealthy man at the same buffet. He takes what he wants and leaves the rest. He isn't afraid it will run out. The man who takes every woman who offers is not the rich man in that room. He's the hungry one.

Now try something. Suppose every night like that came with what sex was built to carry. If you sleep with her, you stay with her. You marry her. If a child comes, that child is a human life, and yours to raise. And count the years. You'll spend far more of them talking with her, eating with her and sitting through her bad days than in bed with her.

Would you still have gone home with her?

Most men answer at once. "No. Not her." Then she was never someone you wanted. She was a plate.

One question for any man, believer or not. Suppose you had lived by that rule: sex only with the woman you have promised to keep. Would you have made better decisions, or worse?

## The lies that go with it {-}

**"A real man gets as many women as he can."** That is the oldest lie of all. It makes a man's worth depend on being wanted, which is exactly what makes him easy to lead. A real man is one a woman can trust. Of the men who live by the other rule, how many would you trust with your sister?

**"It's only a little fun. She knew what it was."** Then why did you have to tell yourself so? Using a person is still using her when she agrees to it. Paul's rule, in his first letter to the Christians in Thessalonica, is short: "that no one should take advantage of and wrong a brother or sister in this matter". You already knew that. That is why it never felt like fun the next morning.

She has been lied to as well. She was told that sleeping with a man and expecting nothing from him is freedom. She was told that a pill makes it safe. She was told that if a child comes anyway, the child's life can be ended, as though that would cost her nothing. Nobody told her the price, and nobody asked what she was worth. If you were part of a choice like that, it is forgiven the same way as everything else in this book. The grief is real, and there is help for it.

You don't have to take that from a church. In 2022 a British journalist named Louise Perry wrote a book about it. She had worked at a rape crisis center. She argued that the sexual revolution mostly served men who want sex without ties. One of her chapters is called "Consent Is Not Enough". Studies are mixed, and many people say they felt fine at the time. But the regret shows when researchers ask. A 2012 study questioned Canadian students who'd had sex with no commitment. About three in four said they had regretted it at some time. That was true of the men as well as the women.

So "she agreed" settles nothing. Men like to complain about women who have been with too many men. They never ask who those men were. If you slept with her and left, you were one of them.

## The customer {-}

The customer comes in two kinds. The first would never go to a prostitute. But he pays a monthly fee to see one woman's private pictures, and he tips her so that she will write back. Or he sends money to a woman who plays video games on a live stream, so that she will say his name. Or it's the strip club.

It is a big business. In 2024, men spent more than seven billion dollars on the largest of these subscription sites. Here is what that money often buys. Reporters found that many of the women there never write the messages at all. Agencies hire workers to do it, called chatters. Many of them are men, sitting in offices on the other side of the world, paid to type like her and to keep him spending.

He thinks he has a girlfriend. He is a customer, and even when she is kind to him, it is her job. If this is you, add up what you have paid this year and look at the number. Then ask what it bought you.

The second kind buys it in person, and his "at least" is that it's honest. For him it may be an escort, a back room, or a cheap flight to a place where nobody knows him. He pays a fair price, he says, and they are two adults. But he can't know whether she chose this. In every country police keep finding women in this trade who are held there. A debt holds them, or a threat, or a passport locked in someone else's drawer. And he can't know her age.

And for all that, he buys the same ten minutes and the same flat feeling, at a higher price. It is the porn habit with the screen taken away. The man who watches porn is already a customer. This man has only walked up to the counter.

## The married man who strays {-}

One more man belongs here. He has a wife, and he took a woman who wasn't her. He has an "at least" too, and it is usually "it only happened once".

Once is not a small number here. His wife was promised all of him, so once is the whole promise broken. He knows it, and that is why he has told nobody.

He is not a different kind of man from the others in this book. He looked, he thought, he chased, and one night the chance came. If that is you, and it is still going on, end it today. That one can't wait for the last page, because someone else is paying for it. Then keep reading. The way out is open to you too, and it goes through the truth.

So that is the collector, the customer and the stray. Each of them took a woman he never meant to keep.
