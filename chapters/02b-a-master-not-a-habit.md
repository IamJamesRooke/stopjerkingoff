# A Master, Not a Habit {#a-master-not-a-habit}

"It's only a habit. And habits are hard to break."

Are they?

You move to a new house, and for two days your hand slaps the wrong wall for the light switch. By the weekend it doesn't.

![](images/illustrations/master-the-light-switch.png){.illustration width=80%}

You rent a car in a country that drives on the other side. One sweaty mile, and you're fine. Men drop thirty-year habits without a backward glance, even ones they loved.

So why not this one? You don't even like it. Skip it and you're restless. Do it and you're ashamed. And stopping asks nothing of you. You only have to not do one thing.

Why can't you?

Because it isn't a habit.

## The right word {-}

Jesus had the word for it. He was arguing with men who bragged they'd never been anybody's slaves. He told them, "Everyone who commits sin is the bondservant of sin."

A bondservant is a slave.

A man with a habit is in charge of it. This thing is in charge of you. Call it sin, or addiction, or a trap. Men call it the habit, and so will this book. Just don't forget what it is.

**IT'S A MASTER, NOT A HABIT.**

Men who've gotten out reach for the same word without being told. One of them, eight months clean, wrote that he'd been a slave to it since he was twelve. He was thirty.

And there's only one master. The thing that sends you to the bathroom is the same thing that opens the porn site. Two habits, one boss.

Here's his whole grip. The slave thinks his master is also his comforter.

## Some master {-}

Now look at who's been giving the orders.

A pipsqueak in your own back room. He bangs, he wails, he kicks his little feet, and you come running with his plate.

Why do you come running? The lies. They told you he was paying his way, and they made a pipsqueak look like a king.

![](images/illustrations/master-the-pipsqueak-king.png){.illustration width=80%}

Take the lies away, and your master is a pest in a back room.

## The quiet trap {-}

How did a pest like that ever get hold of you?

Quietly.

![](images/illustrations/master-the-quiet-trap.png){.illustration width=80%}

A boy gets no warning. Cigarettes at least bring a cough and a label on the box. His own hand brings no dealer, no price, no hangover and no smell on his clothes. Nothing flashes red.

Then comes the first guilt, or the first prayer he doesn't quite want to finish. By then the routine is laid down.

So whose fault is it? One man who got out said it well: "I would say it is our fault and not our fault at the same time". Nobody chooses to walk into the trap. A man does have to choose to walk out.

## Why he never gets out {-}

Watch when a man tries. It's never on a good day. He tries after something goes wrong.

- He's nearly caught.
- A friend says something that lands.
- He looks at his wife and feels like a fraud.
- He's with a real woman, and his body lets him down.

So he quits on the day he feels worst. That's the very day he most wants his old comforter. He's stacked a second misery on the first.

![](images/illustrations/master-two-boxes-in-the-rain.png){.illustration width=80%}

A few days of that, and his resolve melts. "I picked a bad week. I'll do it after finals. When I've got a girlfriend. Once I'm married."

Then things calm down. The pressure's off, and the reason to stop goes with it. He drifts until the next scare.

![](images/illustrations/master-calendar-pages-fly.png){.illustration width=80%}

Every year the calm weeks get fewer. Job, rent, wife, kids. Life, he decides, just gets more stressful as you go.

Not that much. He wasn't carefree at fourteen. He was a bundle of nerves. What's grown is the habit's share of the load. It doesn't calm him down. It winds him up, one guilty night at a time. Another secret to keep. Another promise broken. Another morning started behind.

He blames the job.

![](images/illustrations/master-wind-up-key.png){.illustration width=80%}

You're not quitting yet, and nobody's asking you to cut down. So the next time a day flattens you, go ahead and carry his plate up. Just ask one thing on the way back down the stairs. Did you take the edge off? Or did you give in to a tantrum?

## Two things hold you {-}

Only two things keep the trap shut.

1. **The flesh.**
2. **The world's lies.**

Take those two away, and nothing's left to hold you.

The men who've walked out aren't clenching their jaws or staring back with longing. If anything, they look faintly puzzled that they ever bothered.

![](images/illustrations/master-walked-out-puzzled.png){.illustration width=80%}

Before long you'll be one of them.

Take the flesh first. It's the smaller of the two, and it's not what you think it is.
