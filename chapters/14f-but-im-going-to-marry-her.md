# But I'm Going to Marry Her {#but-im-going-to-marry-her}

"But this is different. I love her."

This chapter is for the man with a girlfriend or a fiancée. If that isn't you, read it for the friend it fits.

There's a man you may recognize. He is not a collector, and he has never paid for anything. He has found the right woman, and he is thinking about a ring.

One afternoon she sends him a photograph meant for his eyes alone. He does with it what he has done with a thousand other pictures. Afterward he asks himself an honest question. Was that lust? She's the woman I am going to spend my life with.

His "at least" is the best one in this book. At least it's her.

So take it seriously, and look at what happened. She sent him something of herself. He took it into a room alone, used it for ten minutes, and felt flat afterward. It was the old habit with her face on it. For those ten minutes the woman he loves was material.

That is the trouble with lust. It doesn't care who she is. It will use a stranger or your future wife, and it uses them the same way.

The photograph is half of it. The other half is the bed. "We're as good as married", he says. Listen to the words. Everything that makes a marriage is in the part he hasn't done yet. That part is the promise, said aloud in front of witnesses, with the door closed behind him. If they truly are as good as married, the wedding is good news, and it can be small and soon. And if there is a reason he can't marry her yet, he already knows the two are not the same.

You may be thinking that this is a great deal of fuss over a few months. Look at what the months are for.

She is about to trust one man with the rest of her life. There will be a year when she is ill, or eight months pregnant, or three thousand miles away. In that year she will need a man whose desire for her doesn't turn into a demand. He can show her now that she is getting one. No ring says it half as well.

So he tells her. He doesn't make a speech about sin. He says something closer to this: "I want you more than I can say, and I'm glad to keep it for our wedding." She may laugh. She may be hurt, and hear it as a no to her. So he says the other half plainly: he doesn't want her less. She may need an evening to think about it. And she will remember it on her wedding day. Read this chapter with her, if you like. Waiting is something the two of you give each other, and she should have a say in how you keep it.

One fair point. She sent the picture, and she isn't a child. Her part in this is real, and it is hers to answer for. But you are the one reading this book, so you are the one who moves first.

And if you were the one who asked for the picture, hear it plainly. That is making your own porn, with a woman you know. A picture, once sent, belongs to whoever holds the phone. A free man doesn't ask for it. If she offers, he says no, and she will remember that he did.

If you're not a believer, use the plain test. Is this a gift given inside a promise already made? The gift is real. The promise isn't made yet. Make it, and the bed is yours to share. One caution. A ring doesn't turn using her into giving. The photograph, alone in a room, is still a hit after the wedding.

## If you already share a bed {-}

Perhaps you are long past this line with the woman you love. Perhaps you live together, and have for years. You haven't ruined anything. You start from the same place as every other man in these pages.

But you can't settle this one alone in your head, because she is in it. So talk to her, in daylight, and not at bedtime. Tell her what you have seen about yourself. Tell her it is no judgment on her, and that you aren't leaving. Then tell her what you want: to stop taking from her, and to make her a promise.

After that, the two of you have real choices. You can bring the wedding forward. Plenty of couples have married within a month, with ten guests. Or you can keep the bed for the wedding from here on. That isn't simple under one roof, so some men move out for the months between. If she doesn't share your conviction, listen to her. Don't preach, and don't make her the problem. The line doesn't move. How the two of you get behind it is yours to work out together.

Only one choice is closed. Don't tell yourself you'll sort it out later and go on as before. "Later" is the Little Monster's favorite word.

That is every room in the house. One more thing has kept the Little Monster alive in all of them for years, and it isn't desire. It is a locked door.
