# But I'm Going to Marry Her {#but-im-going-to-marry-her}

There's a man you may recognize. He is not a collector, and he has never paid for anything. He has found the right woman, and he is thinking about a ring.

One afternoon she sends him a photograph that was meant for his eyes alone. He does with it what he has done with a thousand other pictures. Afterward he asks himself an honest question. Was that lust? She's the woman I am going to spend my life with.

His "at least" is the best one in this book. At least it's her.

So take it seriously, and look at what happened. She sent him something of herself. He took it into a room alone, used it for ten minutes, and felt flat afterward. It was the old loop with her face on it. For those ten minutes the woman he loves was material.

That is the trouble with lust. It doesn't care who she is. It will use a stranger or it will use your future wife, and it uses them in the same way.

The other half of this is the bed. "We're as good as married", he says. Listen to the words. Everything that makes a marriage is in the part he hasn't done yet: the promise, said aloud in front of witnesses, with the door closed behind him. If it truly is as good as married, then marry. And if there is a reason he can't yet, he already knows the two things are not the same.

You may be thinking that this is a great deal of fuss over a few months. Look at what the months are for.

She is about to trust one man with the rest of her life. There will be a year when she is ill, or eight months pregnant, or three thousand miles away. In that year she will need a man who can want her and wait. He can show her now that she is getting one. No ring says it half as well.

So he tells her. He doesn't make a speech about sin. He says something closer to this: "I want you more than I can say, and I want to be a man who can wait for you." She may laugh at him. She may be hurt for an evening. And she will remember it on her wedding day.

One fair point. She sent the picture, and she isn't a child. A woman can feed lust in a man as easily as a man can feed it in himself. But you are the one reading this book, so you are the one who moves first.

If you're not a believer, use the plain test. Is this a gift given inside a promise already made? The gift is real. The promise isn't made yet. Make it, and nothing in this chapter applies to you ever again.

And if you're long past this line with the woman you love, you haven't ruined anything. You start from today, like every other man in these pages.

That is all five heads. Most men who read a list like that do the same thing with it. They look for the head they don't have.
