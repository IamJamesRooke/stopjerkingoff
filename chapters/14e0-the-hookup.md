# The Hookup {#the-hookup}

The last thing lust borrows is a man's bed. The chase was heading there, and so was everything before it. One night he stops chasing and takes.

Most men who've done it are no great seducers. They went home with someone when the chance came. Ask one the next morning. He doesn't look like a conqueror. He's hunting for his car keys.

![](images/illustrations/hookup-hunting-for-keys.png){.illustration width=80%}

Maybe that's never been you. Read on anyway. You know a man it fits.

## The old word for it {-}

There's an old word for this, and it's fornication. It means sex between two people who aren't married to each other.

The word has gone out of use. The thing hasn't. It has friendlier names now: hooking up, a one-night stand, friends with benefits. A young man is told he's missing out unless he joins in.

Forget the word for a minute. Ask what you ask of every room. What did the night give you?

The same hit as the screen and the hand.

The hookup has an "at least" of its own. "I'm not jerking off. At least I'm with a real woman."

She is real, and that's the trouble. The screen used a picture. The hand used a memory. This uses her. He hasn't stopped jerking off. He's doing it with her body, and she's a person.

"But she wanted it too." Maybe she did. Then where were the two of you going? If nowhere, each of you gave one night and kept the rest back. Two people each took a hit off the other, and nobody got a gift.

Same for the girlfriend of two years he has no plan to marry. It's one long night. She's no stranger, and you stay. But ask her what she thinks the two years are for. If her answer isn't yours, one of you is being used. A plan for someday is not a promise.

"But there might be a future with this one." A maybe isn't a promise. If there's a future, it'll still be there when the promise is made. Sleeping with her first doesn't help you find out. You'll say a careful man tests before he signs. What has the bed ever tested? It tells you she's warm. It can't tell you whether she keeps her word. And she pays tonight for something you may never deliver.

## Take the night apart {-}

"The same hit" is easy to say. A woman in your bed feels like far more than a banging on the floor. So take the night apart, hour by hour.

**Before.** The match, the messages, the drinks. Most of what you feel is one question. Does she want me? When the answer's yes, an alarm shuts off. That's the high point of the evening, and it comes before anyone has touched anyone.

**During.** Be honest about where your head was. Half the time you were watching yourself. Was it going well? Was she comparing? You were with a stranger, and you were performing for her.

**The minute after.** That's the test of any pleasure. A husband who's made love to a wife he loves is glad a minute later. He stays put. Most nights you wanted her gone, or you wanted to be gone. She reached for you, and being held was the part you couldn't stand. Count the nights you stayed and were glad. Then count the others. Then count how many of those women are still in your life. Two psychologists put the question to American students in 2001, in two small studies. Men with many past partners said a woman looked less attractive to them after the first time. Men with few partners didn't. There's an old saying among men who pay for it. You don't pay her for the sex. You pay her to leave.

**The morning.** One of you is hunting for a missing sock. Nobody mentions next week. Then comes the drive home, radio off, in a flat gray quiet. The phone's out again before lunch.

![](images/illustrations/hookup-gray-drive-home.png){.illustration width=80%}

Add it up. You were told this is the best thing in a man's life. For one night you had her body and nothing else of her, and she had yours. Within days you wanted the next one. That's no meal. It's the hit, with a witness.

"But she was warm, and I wasn't alone." You were never more alone than in that minute after. A man can be lonelier beside a stranger than by himself.

Maybe one of them was different. You liked her. A minute later you were glad, and you stayed till morning. Grant all of it. What made that night good? Not the few seconds. It was that she knew you, and for a while you both acted as if you were staying. The good part was the part that looked like the promise. Now where is she? If you're reading this single, it ended like the others, only slower, and it cost you both more. Leaving the promise out added nothing. It just kept a door open, and one of you used it.

So that night is no exception. It's the hardest bed in the house to leave. A stranger's bed is empty by morning, and a man leaves it. The warm bed is good enough to stay in, and never good enough to decide in. A man can spend two years there, and then two more with the next woman. It looks so much like the real thing he stops looking for the real thing. Count the years the warm beds cost you. Then count what they cost her.

## Would you have kept her? {-}

Try something. Suppose every night like that came with what sex was built to carry. If you sleep with her, you stay with her. You marry her. If a child comes, that child is a human life, and yours to raise. And count the years. You'll spend far more of them talking with her, eating with her and sitting through her bad days than in bed with her.

Would you still have gone home with her?

Listen for how fast your answer comes. "No. Not her." Then she was never someone you wanted. You wanted the hit.

## The lies that go with it {-}

**"A real man gets as many women as he can."** That's the oldest lie of all. It makes a man's worth depend on being wanted, which is exactly what makes him easy to lead. A real man is one a woman can trust. Of the men who live by the other rule, how many would you trust with your sister?

**"It's only a little fun. She knew what it was."** Then why did you have to tell yourself so? Using a person is still using her when she agrees to it. Paul's rule, in his first letter to the Christians in Thessalonica, is short: "that no one should take advantage of and wrong a brother or sister in this matter". You already knew that. That's why it so seldom felt like fun the next morning.

She's been lied to too. She was told that sleeping with a man and expecting nothing from him is freedom. She was told a pill makes it safe. She was told that if a child comes anyway, the child's life can be ended, as though that would cost her nothing. Often nobody told her the price, and nobody asked what she was worth. If you were part of a choice like that, there's forgiveness for it, the same as for everything else in this book. The grief is real, and there's help for it.

You don't have to take that from a church. In 2022 a British journalist named Louise Perry wrote a book about it. She had worked at a rape crisis center. She argued that the sexual revolution mostly served men who want sex without ties. One of her chapters is called "Consent Is Not Enough". Studies are mixed, and many people say they felt fine at the time. The regret shows when researchers ask. A 2012 study questioned Canadian students who'd had sex with no commitment. About three in four said they'd regretted it at some time. That was true of the men as well as the women. It counts one regret in a lifetime, so it proves less than it sounds.

"She agreed" settles nothing. Men like to complain about women who've been with too many men. They never ask who those men were. If you slept with her and left, you were one of them.

## The door {-}

The terms at this door are the usual ones, with one difference. Nobody's asking for a vow, and nobody's counting. But this room has another person in it. You make a choice about her each time, and you can stop making it tonight.

Nobody ends hookup culture by complaining about it. A man ends it one bed at a time, starting with his own.

Some men have gone further up this road than the rest.
