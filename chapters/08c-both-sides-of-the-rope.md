# Both Sides of the Rope {#both-sides-of-the-rope}

We tend to picture a tug-of-war. One team is fear: "It's a sin, it's draining me, I hate the secrecy." The other team we think of as pleasure: "It's my comfort and my release, the one thing that's mine."

Look at that second team again, because it's fear as well. It isn't that you enjoy the habit so much. It's that you're uneasy without it. The man who never started isn't uneasy without it, any more than you're uneasy without a drug you've never taken. The habit doesn't soothe that unease. It manufactures it.

This is also why a list of costs has never made you stop. A frightened man reaches for his comfort, and you know what you've trained yourself to reach for. So instead of stopping, men develop a set of excuses:

**"It's my life. Better to enjoy it than to live like a monk."** Fine. Enjoy it. Now tell me honestly that the life you have just been shown is the enjoyable one. Tell me the man hiding in the bushes is having a better time than the man walking in the garden.

**"I'm single. Who exactly am I hurting?"** The man you're turning into. Your future wife will marry whatever comes out the other end of this. And apart from all that, there's you now. You're a person too.

## The shadow {-}

There's one more cost, and it's the one you'll be gladdest to lose.

Every man with this habit carries a shadow at the back of his mind. Most of the time he doesn't look at it. He jokes and works and plays with his kids. But it's there, just under the surface, and it goes wherever he goes. He knows he's doing something he despises, and he has arranged his life so as not to think about it.

It's the small jolt when the conversation turns to porn, or the half-second of dread when someone borrows his phone. It's also that vague uneasiness late at night after a bad day, which he's always blamed on the day.

The day didn't cause it. Men without the habit don't carry that shadow. King David, who wrote many of the Psalms, knew it well. In Psalm 32 he wrote: "When I kept silence, my bones wasted away through my groaning all day long."

Then he tells you what lifted it. "I acknowledged my sin to you. I didn't hide my iniquity," he wrote, and God "forgave the iniquity of my sin." The shadow goes the same way for you. You tell the truth, and you are forgiven.

Some of the gains of stopping are ones you'll notice and enjoy on purpose. You'll have the time and the energy, and you'll be done with the whole silly business of making love to nobody. But the best of them is a thing you'll notice by its absence. One ordinary afternoon you'll realize that the shadow isn't there, and hasn't been for days. You'll have nothing to hide and nothing to dread.

That day is closer than you think.

One cost is left. It is the one a man least likes to hear said aloud.
