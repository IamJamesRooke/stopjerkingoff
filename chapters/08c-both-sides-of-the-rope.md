# Both Sides of the Rope {#both-sides-of-the-rope}

Why has no list of costs ever made you stop? Because we picture the wrong fight.

We picture a tug-of-war. One team is fear: "It's a sin, it's draining me, I hate the secrecy." The other team, we think, is pleasure: "It's my comfort and my release, the one thing that's mine."

Look at that second team again. A few seconds of it is pleasure. The rest is fear too. You don't enjoy the habit so much as feel uneasy without it. The man who never started feels no such unease, any more than you miss a drug you've never taken. The habit doesn't soothe the unease. It manufactures it.

![](images/illustrations/rope-fear-on-both-teams.png){.illustration width=80%}

That's why the list never worked. It frightens you, and a frightened man reaches for his comfort. You know what yours is. So instead of stopping, men build excuses. Here are the two favorites.

**"It's my life. Better to enjoy it than to live like a monk."** Fine. Enjoy it. Now tell me honestly that the life you've just been shown is the enjoyable one. Tell me the man hiding in the bushes is having a better time than the man walking in the garden.

**"I'm single. Who exactly am I hurting?"** The man you're turning into, for one. Anyone who ever relies on you, for another: they get whatever comes out the other end of this. And you, now. You're a person too.

## The shadow {-}

There's one more cost, and it's the one you'll be gladdest to lose.

Every man with this habit carries a shadow at the back of his mind. Most days he doesn't look at it. He jokes and works and plays with his kids. But it goes wherever he goes. He knows he's doing something he despises, and he's arranged his life so he doesn't have to think about it.

![](images/illustrations/rope-the-shadow.png){.illustration width=80%}

It's the jolt when the talk turns to porn. The half-second of dread when someone borrows his phone. The low hum of unease at bedtime, which he's always blamed on the day.

The day didn't cause it. A man without the habit has bad days too, and no shadow like this. King David knew it well. He had a secret of his own once, and in Psalm 32 he wrote: "When I kept silence, my bones wasted away through my groaning all day long."

Then he tells you what lifted it. "I acknowledged my sin to you. I didn't hide my iniquity," he wrote, and God "forgave the iniquity of my sin." The shadow goes the same way for you. You tell the truth, and you're forgiven.

If you don't pray, half of that still holds. A shadow needs something hidden. Nothing left to hide, nowhere to stand.

Some gains you'll notice and enjoy on purpose. The time and the energy come back, and you're done with the whole silly business of making love to nobody.

The best gain is the one you won't see arrive. One ordinary afternoon you'll realize the shadow's gone, and has been for days. Nothing to hide. Nothing to dread.

![](images/illustrations/rope-shadow-gone.png){.illustration width=80%}

That day is closer than you think.

So much for how the habit makes you feel. There's also what it makes you worth to the people who count on you.
