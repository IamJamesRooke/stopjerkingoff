# The Lonely Room {#the-lonely-room}

The last occasion is the night a man feels alone. It comes in a small size and a large one.

## After the date {-}

The date went badly, or nowhere. Or you had a fight with your wife and she's gone to bed with her back to you. You've got the sour ache of a man who's been turned down. Tonight of all nights, you tell yourself, you're owed something. You aren't. Her no is never your excuse.

What the habit offers is company that can't say no, because nobody is there. And each night you take that offer makes a real woman a little harder to look in the eye.

These are the nights you think you'd miss it most. The trick is the same. The habit quieted the Little Monster on a night when he was loud, which guaranteed he'd be loud on the next one.

## Loneliness {-}

This is the large size, and it lies under most of the others.

You're alone in an apartment or a hotel room, or lying beside a wife who feels a long way off. Nobody to touch, nobody who knows you. And the habit, at least, feels like somebody holding you.

The ache is real, and it's a good ache. You were made for company. "It is not good for the man to be alone" is the first thing God calls not good in the whole Bible. A Catholic speaker passes on a friend's line: "What you really long for is authentic love, but you settled for the counterfeit."

A counterfeit doesn't just fail to pay. It makes you poorer. C.S. Lewis, the Christian writer, answered a letter about this very habit. He said it "sends the man back into the prison of himself, there to keep a harem of imaginary brides." Desire was meant to get you out of yourself. The habit locks you further in. Every time, you finish more alone than you began, in the same empty room with one more thing nobody knows about you. A secret is a wall, and you're building it yourself, one evening at a time, between you and everyone who might love you.

![](images/illustrations/lonely-the-wall.png){.illustration width=80%}

Loneliness is also what breaks the men who quit by gritting their teeth. One man set himself a goal of five years and reached day 280. He had, in his own words, "even lost interest in porn". Then: "I ended up relapsing because I was overwhelmed by extreme loneliness and the feeling that nothing in my life was going right." The porn had gone, and the belief was still standing: that when a man is lonely enough, this is where he goes. That belief is the Big Monster, and no number of days will make it go away. It has to be seen through.

Loneliness is a hunger for people, and the habit feeds it a photograph.

![](images/illustrations/lonely-a-photograph.png){.illustration width=80%}

## It's the occasion, not the habit {-}

Here's the list: the bad day, the good day, the empty hour, the stuck page, the quiet evening, the sleepless night, the bad date and the lonely room. In every one the habit claimed to help. In every one it only quieted, for a few minutes, a restlessness it had caused. Then it handed your trouble back with interest.

It was never the habit that made those moments bearable. The evening off, the warm shower and the clean bed are good in themselves, and the habit was a tax on them. Take away the need for it, and the good times get better and the hard times get easier.

A man who believes he needs the habit to cope will reach for it every time life leans on him. A man who knows he doesn't need it has nothing left to reach for.

So the next time life leans on you, remember you're allowed. No rule in this book says you can't. Ask one small thing first. Do I need this, or does he?

Come to me, Jesus says, and I will give you rest. That's better than relief, and you know the difference. Relief is what you've been buying every night for years, and it has never lasted until morning.
