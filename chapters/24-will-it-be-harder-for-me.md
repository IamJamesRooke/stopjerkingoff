# Will It Be Harder for Me?

> "In the day of prosperity be joyful, and in the day of adversity consider; yes, God has made the one side by side with the other"
>
> — Ecclesiastes 7:14

No two men come to this book from the same place. One's nineteen and one's sixty. One's married, one's never been kissed. Some situations do look harder than others. They don't have to be, once the Big Monster is gone.

## The man who leads the church {-}

You'd think it would be easiest for the pastor, the elder or the worship leader. It isn't. He's got more reasons to stop than anyone, and reasons were never the problem. He fears being found out, and fear sends a man reaching for comfort. He believes he has to set an example, so he carries a second helping of guilt: "I, of all people." Often he has no pastor of his own. If you lead in a church, you're in an ordinary trap, and it opens the ordinary way.

If that's you, tell someone outside your own church: a pastor you trust in another town, your bishop or superintendent, a counselor. If you're a priest, tell your confessor or spiritual director. You've spent years carrying other men's secrets. You're allowed to put this one down.

## The man who has been at it for decades {-}

He's sure it must be harder for him. Thirty years in the rut can't be the same as three.

It's the same. The Little Monster is no bigger at fifty than he was at fifteen, and the lies are the same lies. Under one man's testimony a commenter did the math on his own life: from thirteen to forty-four, more than thirty years of it. A longer sentence doesn't change how the door opens. All it changes is the size of the relief.

## The man who thinks he's built this way {-}

Plenty of men believe they're a special case. "I've got an unusually high drive." "I've got an addictive personality." Christians have their own version: "This is my besetting sin, my thorn. I'll carry it to the grave." That man is borrowing from Paul, who wrote of a "thorn in the flesh". But Paul's thorn was a suffering God allowed. It was never a sin he was told to keep. Usually a man picks up the belief from too many failed streaks and too many alarming videos.

Nobody's born needing to do this to get to sleep. You weren't lying in your crib plotting it. The trap catches a man. His character has nothing to do with it, though the trap is clever enough to make him blame it. Drop that belief. A man who thinks he's in for life will behave like it long after the Little Monster is dead.

## The student and the single man {-}

One more hard-looking case is boredom plus stress. Think of the student in finals week, or the single man alone with a microwave dinner. Long empty hours, nobody around. On the Willpower Method those hours are deadly, because he's got all evening to brood over what he's "lost".

With the right frame of mind those same hours are no trouble. Don't worry that being alone keeps reminding you that you've stopped. Let it. Every reminder is a reminder that you're getting out.

The single man has one more thought. The married man keeps a bed, and he keeps none. But unless a woman shares his bed now, he's not giving up a bed. He's giving up an itch.

## So, will it be harder? {-}

No.

Young or old, married or single, pastor or plumber, believer or not: any man can find this easy and even enjoyable. He only has to follow the two instructions.

Most men find it easier than they feared. Some carry more than the habit: an old wound, a depression, something that was done to them. If that's you, the method still works, and you may need a person's help as well as a book. Getting that help is no failure.

## The bad day {-}

One thing trips men in every one of these situations, and it's a bad day.

Get this straight before you start: you'll have good days and bad days whether you go back to the habit or not. Ecclesiastes says God made the good day and the bad day side by side. They come to the free man and the man in the trap alike.

The willpower man spends his bad day pacing the kitchen, pining for his comfort, so now he's got two problems. The free man has one. He meets it with a clear head, and he isn't dragging a guilty secret through it.

If a bad day lands on you in the first weeks, shrug and take the punch. You had bad days before, plenty of them, or you wouldn't be reading this book. Then say it plainly: "All right, today is rotten. Jerking off won't fix it. Nor will a video, or a message to a woman I shouldn't be writing to. Tomorrow will be better, and in the meantime I've got one enormous thing going for me. I'm free."

Stay glad. It matters on the good days, and it matters most on the bad ones.

And on a bad day you'll be tempted to reach for something else in the habit's place.
