# The Rigged Game {#the-rigged-game}

One man is still in the hall, with his arms crossed. He has heard every word about the promise, and he has an answer.

"Marriage is a scam, and it is rigged against men. She can leave whenever she likes. She takes the children, the house and half my pay. I watched it happen to my friends."

He is no fool, and much of what he says is true. So take it one piece at a time, with the numbers. All of them are American.

## What is true {-}

**Wives do most of the leaving.** One national study followed couples from 2009 to 2015. In about two of every three divorces, the wife was the one who wanted out. Court records from four states in 1995 show the same. Wives filed about two thirds of the time.

**Either one can leave, for no reason.** Every American state now grants a divorce without proof that anyone did wrong. The one who breaks the vow pays no penalty for breaking it. As a contract, the state's paper binds nobody.

**The children mostly live with her.** In 2018, four of every five parents with custody were mothers.

**The money mostly comes from him.** In 2013, nine of every ten parents paying child support were fathers.

**Judges lean.** In a study published in 2018, 372 sitting judges read the same custody case. Only the sex of the parent was changed. They gave the mother about half a day more each week.

**A man can be jailed for the debt.** In 2011 the Supreme Court heard the case of a father who was about $5,700 behind. He had served twelve months. He'd had no lawyer, and the judge never found that he was able to pay. The Court ruled that he had been jailed unlawfully.

**A man can pay for a child who is not his.** The law presumes that a husband is the father of his wife's child.

**A man can be put out of his house on a sworn statement.** A court can order it before he has been heard. Most such orders protect someone who needs protecting. Nobody has an honest count of how often one is misused in a custody fight.

**Divorce is hard on men.** One large study followed Americans through the 1980s. Divorced men were more than twice as likely as married men to die by suicide. And a 2011 report looked at fathers living apart from their children. About one in four had not seen them in a year.

None of that is in your head. A man who has watched it happen is right to be careful.

## What is exaggerated {-}

Some of what men tell each other is wrong, and you should know which parts.

**"Half of all marriages fail."** That was close to true for couples who married in the 1970s. The divorce rate has fallen by about a third since 1980. Take male college graduates born around 1960. By age forty-six, fewer than one in four of their first marriages had ended in divorce. That is the best-placed group. For men who stopped at high school, it was nearly half.

**"The court takes the kids."** In the best study of it, a judge decided 14 custody cases out of 933. The parents settled the rest between them. Be fair to the skeptic here. A father who expects to lose may never ask. The law is moving, though. By 2026, seven states had made equal time with each parent the starting point.

**"Alimony for life."** It is rare. The counts are rough, and they find a few hundred thousand people receiving it in the whole country. Florida ended permanent alimony in 2023. Where it is paid, it is nearly always a man who pays it.

**"She walks away rich."** Mostly she doesn't. About a quarter of mothers with custody live in poverty, though many of those never married. Fewer than half of the parents who are owed support get all of it.

**"No-fault divorce did this."** Wives were already filing about six divorces in ten in the nineteenth century. The best study of the new laws found that they raised divorce for about ten years. Then the effect faded.

So the table is tilted against a husband. It is less tilted than men tell each other, and in some states it is tilting back.

That is the charge, heard in full. "Fine", he says. "Tilted is enough for me. I'll have the woman and skip the paper."

That is the last easier line in the house, and it has a price list of its own.
