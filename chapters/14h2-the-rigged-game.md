# The Rigged Game {#the-rigged-game}

He's heard every word about the promise, and he has his answer loaded.

"Marriage is a scam, and it's rigged against men. She can leave whenever she likes. She takes the children, the house and half my pay. I watched it happen to my friends."

He's no fool, and much of what he says is true. So take it one piece at a time, with the numbers. All of them are American.

## What is true {-}

**Wives do most of the leaving.** One national study followed couples from 2009 to 2015. In about two of every three divorces, the wife wanted out. Court records from four states in 1995 show the same. Wives filed about two thirds of the time.

**Either one can leave, for no reason.** Every American state now grants a divorce without proof that anyone did wrong. Usually the one who breaks the vow pays no penalty for breaking it. The state's paper doesn't make anyone stay.

**The children mostly live with her.** In 2018, four of every five parents with custody were mothers.

**The money mostly comes from him.** In 2013, nine of every ten parents paying child support were fathers.

**Judges lean.** In a study published in 2018, 372 sitting judges read the same custody case. Only the sex of the parent was changed. They gave the mother about half a day more each week.

**A man can be jailed for the debt.** In 2011 the Supreme Court heard the case of a father about $5,700 behind. He'd served twelve months. He'd had no lawyer, and the judge never found that he was able to pay. The Court ruled he'd been jailed unlawfully.

**A man can pay for a child who isn't his.** The law presumes a husband is the father of his wife's child.

**A man can be put out of his house on a sworn statement.** A court can order it before he's been heard. Most such orders protect someone who needs protecting. Nobody has an honest count of how often one is misused in a custody fight.

**Divorce hits men hard.** One large study followed Americans through the 1980s. Divorced men were more than twice as likely as married men to die by suicide. And a 2011 report looked at fathers living apart from their children, divorced or never married. About one in four hadn't seen them in a year.

None of that is in your head. A man who's watched it happen is right to check the ice before he steps on it.

## A man who lost the bet {-}

One man lost that bet, and the habit helped him lose it.

The divorce made him hate women. He went red pill. He saw women in the state of nature as amoral, and ready to trade up. The instant he was no longer useful, he was gone.

He saw women like his stomach. He has fed it every day of his life. It doesn't care. It asks what he fed it lately.

He won't tell you that was all wrong. A lot of it is still true. Being with modern women was a nightmare, and he lived it.

But hating them fixes nothing, he says, and he's no angel himself.

So what holds a vow? Two people alone don't, he says. "Till death do us part" needs a church and a community behind it. No leaving. Ever.

Even then there's a risk. Plenty of Christian wives find that the Holy Spirit wants them divorced and happy.

He hasn't figured it all out. He's making the same bet again, knowing all of this.

## What is exaggerated {-}

Some of what men tell each other over beers is wrong. You should know which parts.

**"Half of all marriages fail."** That was close to true for couples who married in the 1970s. The divorce rate has fallen by about a third since 1980. Take male college graduates born around 1960. By age forty-six, fewer than one in four of their first marriages had ended in divorce. That's the best-placed group. For men who stopped at high school, it was nearly half.

**"The court takes the kids."** In the best study of it, a judge decided 14 custody cases out of 933. The parents settled the rest between them. Be fair to the skeptic here. A father who expects to lose may never ask. The law is moving, though. By 2026, seven states had made equal time with each parent the starting point.

**"Alimony for life."** It's rare. The counts are rough, and they find a few hundred thousand people receiving it in the whole country. Florida ended permanent alimony in 2023. Where it's paid, it's nearly always a man who pays it.

**"She walks away rich."** Mostly she doesn't. About a quarter of mothers with custody live in poverty, though many of those never married. Fewer than half of the parents owed support get all of it. That goes for fathers with custody too. In 2022 they were owed $4.8 billion, and they received $3.0 billion.

**"No-fault divorce did this."** Wives were already filing about six divorces in ten in the nineteenth century. The best study of the new laws found they raised divorce for about ten years. Then the effect faded.

So the table is tilted against a husband. It's less tilted than men tell each other, and in some states it's tilting back.

![](images/illustrations/rigged-tilted-table.png){.illustration width=80%}

That's the charge, heard in full. "Fine", he says. "Tilted is enough for me. I'll have the woman and skip the paper."

That's the last easier line in the house, and it has a price list of its own.
