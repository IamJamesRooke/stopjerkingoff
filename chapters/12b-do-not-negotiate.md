# Do Not Negotiate {#do-not-negotiate}

A woman has a pursuer who will not stop calling. She ignores thirty messages. Then, worn out, she calls him back, only to tell him to leave her alone.

She thinks she has ended it. Now listen to what he hears.

He doesn't hear her words. He doesn't think, "She isn't interested in me." He thinks, "She called. Twenty-nine messages got me nothing, and the thirtieth got me her voice. So the price of her voice is thirty messages."

It makes no difference what she said, or how angrily she said it. She answered, and to him an answer is a prize. She meant to shut the door. She has shown him exactly how many times he has to knock.

That picture comes from Gavin de Becker, an American security specialist who spent his career guarding public figures from stalkers. He put it in a book called *The Gift of Fear*, in 1997. He has one rule for every kind of unwanted pursuit, and it is three words long.

**Do not negotiate.**

He gives the woman four instructions.

- **Say it once, and say it outright.** She tells him that she has no interest in him and is certain she never will.
- **Give no reason.** A reason is something he can argue with. If she says she doesn't want a relationship "right now", the only words he hears are "right now". A refusal with a condition attached is a discussion.
- **Don't let him down gently.** A kindness that leaves the door open an inch is heard as hope.
- **Then never answer again.** She doesn't answer the angry call, or the apology, or the "one last goodbye". She doesn't send a friend to warn him off. Any reply at all buys her weeks more of the same. "The way to stop contact", de Becker wrote, "is to stop contact." And if she is ever afraid of him, she goes to the police.

De Becker himself reaches for the comparison this book needs. The pursuer, he says, is like an addict, and she is the drug. Small doses don't wean him. They keep him hooked. The only cure is none at all.

## Your own stalker {-}

You have a stalker of your own. De Becker's clients were famous people, the kind a stranger fixes on and will not leave alone. In this story you are the famous one, and you have a fan.

**The Little Monster is the stalker.** He is the caller who can't take no for an answer, and every urge is one more message from him.

Every "just once" is you calling him back. It makes no difference that you only meant to quiet him. He thinks exactly as the stalker thinks. He doesn't think, "This man is finished with me." He thinks, "If I nag long enough, I get fed." Hold out for ten days and then give in, and you have taught him the price: ten days of nagging. Next time he will nag for eleven, quite cheerfully.

Her four instructions are yours, word for word.

- **Say it once, and say it outright.** This book will bring you to that moment. It is one plain No, with no hope left in it.
- **Give no reason.** Tell him you're too tired tonight, and he will be back when you're rested. You don't owe an itch an explanation.
- **Don't let him down gently.** "Only this week" and "only on Saturdays" are a door left open an inch, and he hears them as hope.
- **Then never answer again.** Arguing with him is contact. Bargaining with him is contact. Even telling him to go away is contact. When he calls, let it ring.

There is one difference, and it is all in your favor. Her pursuer may be dangerous. Yours is not. The Little Monster is not ten feet tall. He is a pipsqueak. He has to stand on a chair to reach the telephone. His only weapon is to annoy you.

He is an itch, about as strong as a tag scratching at the back of your collar. He can't hurt you, and he can't make you do anything. Men have crossed oceans and built cities, and then taken orders from this.

If he has ever felt enormous to you, that was the fear of him, and fear can flatten a strong man.

Nobody ever went back because the itch was unbearable. It isn't. If stopping has ever felt like torture, the itch wasn't doing it. The wanting was, and the belief that you were being denied something. Men go back because they believe "just once" will buy some peace. It buys another round of calls.

So don't pick up. The cure is de Becker's: none at all. A caller who is never answered has nothing to learn. The Little Monster gives up once he knows that you won't answer, however many times he calls. He rings a little less each day, and then he stops.

So how does a caller like that ever get through?
