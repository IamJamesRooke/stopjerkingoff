# Do Not Negotiate {#do-not-negotiate}

A woman has a pursuer who will not stop calling. She ignores his messages, thirty of them. Then, worn out, she calls him back, only to tell him to leave her alone.

She thinks she has ended it. Now listen to what he hears.

He doesn't hear her words. He doesn't think, "She isn't interested in me." He thinks, "She called. Twenty-nine messages got me nothing, and the thirtieth got me her voice. So persistence is the key. If I want to talk to her once, I have to call thirty times."

It makes no difference what she said, or how angrily she said it. She answered, and to him an answer is a prize. She meant to shut the door. She has shown him exactly how many times he has to knock.

That picture comes from Gavin de Becker, an American security specialist who spent his career guarding public figures from stalkers. He put it in a book called *The Gift of Fear*, in 1997. He has one rule for every kind of unwanted pursuit, and it is three words long.

**Do not negotiate.**

His instructions to the woman are worth knowing for their own sake. There are four.

- **Say it once, and say it outright.** She tells him that she has no interest in him and is certain she never will, whatever he may have assumed until now.
- **Give no reason.** A reason is something he can argue with. If she says she doesn't want a relationship "right now", the only words he hears are "right now". A refusal with a condition attached is a discussion.
- **Don't let him down gently.** A kindness that leaves the door open an inch is heard as hope.
- **Then never answer again.** She doesn't answer the angry call, or the apology, or the "one last goodbye". She doesn't send a friend to warn him off. Every reply of any kind buys her weeks more of the same. "The way to stop contact", de Becker wrote, "is to stop contact." And if she is ever afraid of him, she goes to the police.

De Becker himself reaches for the comparison this book needs. The pursuer, he says, is like an addict, and she is the drug. Small doses don't wean him. They keep him hooked. The only cure is none at all.

## If you find yourself obsessed with a woman {-}

This part is not for every reader. But there may be a woman who has told you no, and you are still writing to her. If so, hear that list from the other side.

She may never have said the word. Many women don't. They want to be kind, or polite, or they are a little afraid of how you'll take it. So her no comes as a slow reply, then "I'm busy this week", then silence. Don't wait to hear it said outright. A woman who wants to hear from you makes that easy to tell. If you have to wonder, she has answered.

You wouldn't call yourself a stalker. You would say that you're persistent, or in love. From where she stands, she said no and she meant it, and every message since then has frightened her a little more. That isn't love. Love wants her good, and she has told you what her good is.

The best thing you can do is also the only thing. Respect her decision, and stop all contact today. Send no last message, no apology and no explanation. Stay away from her house and from her pictures.

- **It is the only way to leave with your dignity.** Every further message spends a little more of it.
- **Expect nothing from it.** If she were ever to think differently, it would be her doing and on her terms. It would never be because you pushed.
- **You are thinking like a starving man.** She is not the only woman in the world. There are women who would be glad of you, and you could be with one who wants you back.

It will feel hard for a few days. That is only the itch, and it passes. If you find you can't stop, tell a doctor or a counselor today. In many places this is also a crime.

## Your own stalker {-}

Now for every reader. You have a stalker of your own. De Becker's clients were famous people, the kind a stranger fixes on and will not leave alone. In this story you are the famous one, and you have a fan. De Becker was writing about dangerous men, and without knowing it he described your habit.

**The Little Monster is the stalker.** You are the one he won't leave alone. He is the caller who can't take no for an answer, and every urge is one more message from him.

Follow it through. Every "just once" is you calling him back. It makes no difference that you only meant to quiet him. The Little Monster thinks exactly as the stalker thinks. He doesn't think, "This man is finished with me." He thinks, "If I nag long enough, I get fed." Hold out for ten days and then give in, and you have taught him that ten days of nagging is the price. Next time he will nag for eleven, quite cheerfully.

Her four instructions are yours, word for word.

- **Say it once, and say it outright.** This book will bring you to that moment. It is one plain No, with no hope left in it.
- **Give no reason.** Tell him you're too tired tonight, and he will be back when you're rested. You don't owe an itch an explanation.
- **Don't let him down gently.** "Only this week" and "only on Saturdays" are a door left open an inch, and he hears them as hope.
- **Then never answer again.** Arguing with him is contact. Bargaining with him is contact. Even telling him to go away is contact. When he calls, let it ring.

There is one difference, and it is all in your favor. Her pursuer may be dangerous. Yours is not. Keep his size in view. The Little Monster is not ten feet tall. He is a pipsqueak you could punt the length of a football field. His only weapon is to annoy you.

He is an itch, about as strong as a mosquito bite or a tag scratching at the back of your collar. He can't hurt you, and he can't make you do anything. Men have crossed oceans and built cities, and then taken orders from this.

If he has ever felt enormous to you, that was not his size. He is small. The fear of him is what was big, and fear can flatten a strong man.

Nobody ever went back because the itch was unbearable. It isn't. If stopping has ever felt like torture to you, the itch wasn't doing it. The wanting was, and the belief that you were being denied something. Men go back because they believe "just once" will buy them some peace. It buys another round of calls.

So don't pick up. The cure is the one de Becker gave, which is none at all. A caller who is never answered has nothing to learn. The Little Monster gives up once he knows that you won't answer, however many times he calls. He rings a little less each day, and then he stops.

So how does a caller like that ever get through?
