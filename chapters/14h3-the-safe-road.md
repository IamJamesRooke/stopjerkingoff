# The Safe Road {#the-safe-road}

"I'll have the woman and skip the paper." It sounds like prudence. So put the same weight on it that every other line had to bear.

## What follows the child {-}

Look at where the danger sits. Custody and child support don't follow the marriage license. They follow the child. An unmarried father owes support the same as a divorced one.

So skip the wedding, and you have skipped none of that. You have only made a child with a woman who promised you nothing.

Skipping it doesn't keep her, either. In the study of who leaves, the gap was found only among the married. Unmarried couples split at his wish about as often as at hers. Read that both ways. Marriage is where more women want out, and no arrangement stops a woman leaving. Living together doesn't stop the leaving. It makes leaving cheap, for both of you.

Then what does the paper add to your risk? Mostly it adds property, and sometimes alimony. In most states a prenuptial agreement can cover both. It can't touch custody or child support. Signing one is no insult to her. Each of you hires a lawyer, and you sign it well before the wedding.

## The paper and the promise {-}

The state's paper is weak, and this book never meant the paper. It meant the promise. That is what you say to her, out loud, in front of the people who will hold you both to it.

No law can make either of you keep it. Only the two of you can. So the woman you say it to matters far more than anything a court does afterward.

Then why sign the state's paper at all? Because a vow that costs nothing to break is the hand on the door again. Refusing the paper keeps the cheap exit, for you and for her. Signing it puts your name and your money where your vow is. It also says so in the one form that everybody else recognizes.

## What the safe road keeps {-}

Now ask what "the woman without the paper" keeps for you. It keeps the exit. That is its whole purpose.

A man with one hand on the door is not all in, and she knows it. So she keeps a hand on her own door. Two people can live that way for years. Each holds something back, and each calls it being careful. Neither is ever fully known, because nobody shows everything to someone who may be leaving.

You have met this before. It is a room kept ready. The habit kept every room for the same reason, so that you would never have to choose.

Follow the safe road to sixty. A study of 2009 counted American men then aged forty-five to sixty-three. Of the never-married men, about three in four lived alone, and one in five was poor. Of married people that age, about one in twenty-five was poor.

Those numbers can't say the ring made the difference. Poorer men marry less, and some of those men had a woman and no paper. But the road is real, and those men are on it.

## Who should not sign {-}

Some men should not marry, and nobody here will push you.

If you can't find a woman you would trust with that lever, don't sign. If the bed is the reason, or a pregnancy, don't sign. A bad marriage is worse than none.

Then the line means the single road, and you have seen that it is not an empty one. The line never said "marry or else". It says no bed without the promise.

## How the odds move {-}

The risk is not spread evenly, and a man can move it. These are odds and not guarantees. Most come from a large government survey of 2006 to 2010.

Of the men who married at twenty-five or older, three in four were still married ten years on. Of the men who married in their teens, fewer than half were.

Some men had their first child well after the wedding. About three in four of them were still married at twenty years. Where the child came first, it was about four in ten.

Men who moved in with a woman before they were engaged did worse than men who didn't.

Steady work counts. A long study of American couples found that a husband without full-time work ran a higher risk of divorce each year. What she earned made no difference. That sits badly beside "for richer, for poorer", and it is a fact all the same. So find out how she behaved the last time money was short.

And her record counts, the same as yours. Has she kept her word when it cost her? Does she own her part when she tells you about the men before you?

Now think again about the friends who were cleaned out, and ask how each of them chose. Some chose well and were wronged anyway. That happens, and it is a real injustice. How many chose with the bed? Beauty can blind a man, and a blind man signs anything.

A careful man can't bring the risk to zero. He can do what he does with any large bet. He learns the odds, he picks the table, and he doesn't play drunk.

Lust is the drink.

That is the last man in the hall. So far this walk has only shut doors. One door is left, and it opens.
