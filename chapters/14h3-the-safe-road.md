# The Safe Road {#the-safe-road}

"I'll have the woman and skip the paper." It sounds like prudence. So put the same weight on it that every other line had to bear.

## What follows the child {-}

Look at where the danger sits. Custody and child support don't follow the marriage license. They follow the child. An unmarried father owes support the same as a divorced one.

So skip the wedding, and you've skipped none of that. You've only made a child with a woman who promised you nothing.

It doesn't keep her, either. In the study of who leaves, the gap showed up only among the married. Unmarried couples split at his wish about as often as at hers. Read that both ways. Marriage is where more women want out, and no arrangement stops a woman leaving. Living together doesn't stop the leaving. It makes leaving cheap, for both of you, as easy as sliding a box into a car trunk.

Then what does the paper add to your risk? Mostly property, and sometimes alimony. In most states a prenuptial agreement can cover both. It can't touch custody or child support. Signing one is no insult to her. Each of you hires a lawyer, and you sign it months before the wedding, not on the morning of.

## The paper and the promise {-}

The state's paper is flimsy, and this book never meant the paper. It meant the promise. That's what you say to her, out loud, in front of the people who'll hold you both to it.

No law can make either of you keep it. Only the two of you can. So the woman you say it to matters far more than anything a court does afterward.

Then why sign the state's paper at all? Because a vow that costs nothing to break is the hand on the door again. Refusing the paper keeps the cheap exit, for you and for her. Signing it puts your name and your money where your vow is. It also says so in the one form everybody else recognizes. You can only take your own hand off the door. Hers is hers, which is why you choose her with such care.

## What the safe road keeps {-}

Now ask what "the woman without the paper" keeps for you. The exit. That's its whole purpose.

A man with one hand on the door isn't all in, and she knows it. So she keeps a hand on her own door. Two people can live that way for years. Each keeps a packed bag in the closet, and each calls it being careful. Neither is ever fully known, because nobody shows everything to someone who may be leaving.

You've met this before. It's a room kept ready. The habit kept every room for the same reason, so you'd never have to choose.

Follow the safe road to sixty. A study of 2009 counted American men then aged forty-five to sixty-three. Of the never-married men, about three in four lived alone, and one in five was poor. Of married people that age, about one in twenty-five was poor.

Those numbers can't say the ring made the difference. Poorer men marry less, and some of those men had a woman and no paper. But the road is real, and those men are on it.

## Who should not sign {-}

Some men shouldn't marry, and nobody here will push you.

If you can't find a woman you'd trust with that lever, don't sign. If the bed is the reason, or a pregnancy, don't sign. A bad marriage is worse than none.

Then the line means the single road. It's not an empty one, and it gets its own answer. The line never said "marry or else". It says no bed without the promise.

## How the odds move {-}

The risk isn't spread evenly, and a man can move it. These are odds, not guarantees. Most come from a large government survey of 2006 to 2010.

Of the men who married at twenty-five or older, three in four were still married ten years on. Of the men who married in their teens, fewer than half were.

Some men had their first child well after the wedding. About three in four of them were still married at twenty years. Where the child came first, it was about four in ten.

Men who moved in with a woman before they were engaged did worse than men who didn't.

A steady paycheck counts. A long study of American couples found that a husband without full-time work ran a higher risk of divorce each year. That sits badly beside "for richer, for poorer", and it's a fact all the same. So find out how she acted the last time money ran short.

And when she earns more than he does? Men say the risk skyrockets. The studies say it rises some, and mostly in one kind of couple.

An American survey from around 1990 found divorce about half again as likely when the wife earned more. A later study found the link had faded for couples who married in the 1990s. In Sweden, women promoted to a top job were about twice as likely to divorce within three years. That held in traditional couples. It didn't hold in couples who'd shared the load from the start.

So her paycheck isn't the danger. The danger is two people who signed up for one kind of marriage and got another. Settle which kind before the wedding.

Her record counts, the same as yours. Has she kept her word when it cost her? Does she own her part when she tells you about the men before you?

Now think again about the friends who were cleaned out, and ask how each of them chose. Some chose well and were wronged anyway. That happens, and it's a real injustice. How many chose with the bed? Beauty can blind a man, and a blind man signs anything.

A careful man can't bring the risk to zero. He can do what he does with any large bet. He learns the odds, he picks his table, and he doesn't play drunk.

Lust is the drink.

Every door on this walk so far has been one to shut. The next one opens.
