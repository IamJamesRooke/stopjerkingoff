# The Best Liar of All {#the-best-liar-of-all}

The most persuasive voice in all this is your own.

The world pitches the lies, and the one behind the world wants them believed. Neither one can close the sale. You do that.

Once you're in the trap, the lies get help from inside. Some part of you knows the Little Monster expects feeding, and it will guard his dinner plate against any argument. That's fear: the fear of that empty, fidgety feeling.

Men in the trap aren't weak-willed. It takes real stamina to keep a secret for twenty years. The trouble is where the strength goes. We pour it into defending the habit, and when we try to quit and fail, we turn it on ourselves. Failing is one thing. Hating yourself for it is another, and that seeps into everything. You skip the gym. The diet lasts until Thursday. Your kids get the worst of your temper.

Nobody argues with himself about whether to give up golf. About the habit we argue every night, and we think that's normal.

From now on, treat every word in favor of the habit like a salesman with his foot in your door, your own words included. Smile, nod, and don't sign.

![](images/illustrations/best-liar-foot-in-the-door.png){.illustration width=80%} That goes for "you need it" and "it's who you are". It goes for the safe version too. "Only in the shower", "only without a screen" and "only on weekends" are all the Big Monster, bargaining for the little one's dinner. Keep asking yourself two questions. "Why am I doing this? Do I actually need to?"

Of course you don't.

The trap closes slowly. Most men don't know they're in it until the day they try to leave, and plenty won't admit it even then. Anyone who's raised the subject with a young man knows how it goes:

You ask him, "You know the only reason you keep doing it is that you can't stop?"

"Nonsense," he says. "I like it. If I didn't, I'd stop."

"Then stop for a month, just to show me."

"Why would I? I like it."

"Stop for a month to show yourself."

"There's no point," he says. "I told you, I like it."

He likes it so much that he won't risk finding out.

Don't take that dare yourself. You know how a month by force goes.

## "Giving up" {-}

One more thing keeps the lies standing: we never question them. We believe what we've always heard, most of all from the people we trust.

The world says the man who stops is missing out. Wrong man. It's the man who carries on. He hands over his energy, his time, his confidence and his peace of mind. And what does he get for it?

A few good seconds. Then a few minutes of the calm the free man has all day long.

"Giving up" is the lie in two words. It smuggles in a sacrifice, and there's nothing to give up. You'll be evicting a freeloader and gaining a great deal. So the phrase is gone, here and now. In this book you don't "give up" the habit. You stop. You quit. Best of all, **YOU ESCAPE.**

The world's lies are general. Your own are specific. The habit helps me with this trouble, at this hour.

So let's go through your day and find them.
