# The Chase {#the-chase}

Here lust borrows a man's feet. This is the chase. If that's never been you, read this for the man you know who's living it. Its excuse is that the women are real. Ask what he's talking to them for.

You text an old girlfriend from the parking lot after work. You stay in something you know is wrong, because leaving means going without. You'd tell a friend he was being a fool. The reasons you gave yourself were made up afterward, because the decision was already made, lower down. That's thinking with the wrong head.

It's the same loop as the screen: cue, urge, relief. A woman pays you attention, the itch stirs, and you do whatever makes it stop. That's not love and it's not courage. A man who loves a woman can think. A man led by the itch can't.

You've seen the man at the party. He walks in and goes straight to the women. He doesn't say hello to the men. He buys a round, and every glass goes to a girl. Ask him why, and he says, "I'm only being friendly. I'm a generous guy."

Try a test. Does he buy the men a drink? Does he talk to the men as long? A generous man is generous with everybody. This man isn't giving, he's shopping, and he knows what's on the list. It's a hit dressed up as a gift.

## The man who waits {-}

The man at the party is the loud kind. There's a quiet kind, and there are far more of him.

He has a friend who's a woman, and he's been in love with her for three years. He drives her to the airport. He fixes her computer. He listens for an hour while she talks about the man she's actually seeing. He calls it friendship.

But a friend wants nothing back, and this man is keeping count. Every favor is a deposit in an account he's sure will pay out one day. She may know it and enjoy the service, or have no idea. Either way, he isn't being kind. He's waiting to be paid, and under the good manners he's angry the check hasn't come.

![](images/illustrations/chase-favors-in-the-bank.png){.illustration width=80%}

Put the same test to him. Would he do all this for a man? Would he do it for her if he knew for certain that nothing would ever come of it? If the answer is no, it was never a gift.

If that's you, the way out is plain. Stop the favors that are really payments. Tell her once, outright, what you want, or step back and say nothing. Then see what's left. Either the friendship stands on its own or it never was one.

## The man who swipes {-}

The chase has a pocket version, and it looks respectable. The dating app.

Wanting a wife is good, and so is meeting her through a phone. Plenty of good marriages began that way. But be honest about why you open it. A man looking for a wife reads slowly and writes to one woman. A hungry man swipes. Fifty faces in five minutes, each sorted in half a second, all for the little jolt when one matches.

One man had been on an app for eight years. Then he met a woman at a party, and they decided to get married. He wrote on a forum that he was still swiping, though he never spoke to a single match. He had what the app was supposed to be for, and he couldn't put it down. Somebody answered him, "Don't you get it? You already won. Close the app."

Same itch, better clothes. The swipe is the scroll, with a chance attached.

And look who the machine pays. In 2022 the Pew Research Center asked Americans who'd used a dating app in the past year how it went. More than half the women said they'd felt swamped by messages. About two in three men said they'd felt insecure because so few came.

Same app, two different evenings. Three in ten of those men said casual sex was a major reason they were on it. Among the women it was about one in eight. So most men on the thing are fishing for something most of the women aren't offering. They sit waiting for a buzz that doesn't come. A slot machine at least takes your money fast. This one takes your evenings, and pays in maybes.

Now ask how the app makes its money. You pay by the month for more swipes and better odds. A man who finds his wife cancels. A man who almost finds her pays again. Nobody has to cheat you for that to matter. The app's best customer is the man who never quite gets there.

In 2024 six users took the owner of two of the biggest apps to court in San Francisco. Their complaint said the apps were built like games, to lock users into paying, and that users "will chase the elusive high of matching". The company called the suit ridiculous. Leave that to the lawyers. You don't need a verdict to know how a slot machine works. It pays a little, now and then, on no schedule you can learn. That's what keeps a man in the chair.

![](images/illustrations/chase-slot-machine.png){.illustration width=80%}

A match is the same little jolt. It lands just often enough to keep your thumb moving.

So the test is simple. Are you looking for a wife, or looking for a hit? If it's a wife, you won't need fifty at a time. If it's a hit, you know what to call it.

## The man who wins {-}

So far the men in this chapter lose. One man doesn't. He matches, he meets her, and he takes her home. He'll tell you this is when he feels most alive. Without it, life goes flat.

Take the thrill apart. It has a before and an after.

Before, a question hangs over him. Does she want me? He checks his phone every ten minutes for the answer. That's not excitement. That's a man waiting for a test result.

Then she says yes, and the question goes quiet. The joy is an alarm shutting off. It lasts about as long as the walk to her door.

Then the question comes back, because her yes settled nothing about him. So he needs another woman to ask. What did the fortieth yes tell him that the fourth didn't?

A man who knows he's worth having doesn't need a vote every weekend.

Where does a man learn that he's worth having? Not from a yes. A yes wears off by Monday. He learns it from promises kept and work finished. He learns it from people who'd call him at three in the morning. None of that can be won in a bar. The winner of the chase is the most anxious man in it.

Life without it doesn't go flat. Flat is what he feels now, between women.

## The one girl {-}

The app is fifty faces. The opposite case is one.

You check your phone forty times an hour. You know her last message by heart. You write your answer five times before you send it. You know when she was last online. It feels like love. It even feels like the opposite of the harem, because she's one woman and she's real.

Look closer. You aren't thinking about her. You're thinking about what you want from her, and how you look to her. You love the longing. She's become a picture in your head, and you're texting the picture. It's the harem with one face in it.

A free man can like a woman, tell her so plainly, and get on with his day while he waits for her answer. If she says no, he's sorry, and he's still himself. That's the kind of man a woman can say yes to.

## If she has said no {-}

This part isn't for every reader. But maybe a woman has told you no, and you're still writing to her.

A woman who wants a man to stop calling is usually told to say no once and outright, to give no reason, not to let him down gently, and never to answer again. Now hear that advice from the other side.

She may never have said the word. Many women don't. They want to be kind, or polite, or they're a little afraid of how you'll take it. So her no comes as a slow reply, then "I'm busy this week", then silence. Don't wait to hear it outright. A woman who wants to hear from you makes that easy to tell. If you have to wonder, she's answered.

You wouldn't call yourself a stalker. You'd say you're persistent, or in love. From where she stands, she said no and she meant it. Every message since has frightened her a little more. That isn't love. Love wants her good, and she's told you what her good is.

The best thing you can do is also the only thing. Respect her decision, and stop all contact today. No last message, no apology, no explanation. Stay away from her house and from her pictures.

- **It's the only way to leave with your dignity.** Every further message spends a little more of it.
- **Expect nothing from it.** If she ever thinks differently, it'll be her doing and on her terms. It will never be because you pushed.
- **You're thinking like a starving man.** She's not the only woman in the world. There are women who'd be glad of you, and you could be with one who wants you back.

It'll be hard for a few days. That's the Little Monster banging on the floor, and it passes. If you find you can't stop, tell a doctor or a counselor today. In many places this is also a crime.

Six men, then. One at the party, one who waits, one who swipes and one who wins. One texting a woman who lives mostly in his head, and one who can't let go. Each of them thinks he's the hunter. Look again at who's doing the leading.
