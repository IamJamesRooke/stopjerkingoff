# The Chase {#the-chase}

Here lust borrows a man's feet. This is the chase. If that has never been you, read this for the man you know who is living it.

You text an old girlfriend at midnight. You stay in something you know is wrong, because leaving means going without. You'd tell a friend he was being a fool. The reasons you gave yourself were made up afterward. The decision had already been made, lower down. That is thinking with the wrong head.

It is the same loop as the screen: cue, urge, relief. A woman pays you attention, the itch stirs, and you do whatever makes it stop. It isn't love and it isn't courage. A man who loves a woman can think. A man led by the itch can't.

There's a man you may recognize at any party. He walks in and goes straight to the women. He doesn't say hello to the men. He buys a round, and every glass goes to a girl. Ask him why, and he says, "I'm only being friendly. I'm a generous guy."

Try a test. Does he buy the men a drink? Does he spend as much time talking to the men? A generous man is generous with everybody. This man is spending, and he knows what he is shopping for. It is a hit dressed up as a gift.

## The man who waits {-}

The man at the party is the loud kind. There is a quiet kind, and there are far more of him.

He has a friend who is a woman, and he has been in love with her for three years. He drives her to the airport. He fixes her computer. He listens for an hour while she talks about the man she is actually seeing. He calls it friendship.

But a friend wants nothing back, and this man is keeping count. Every favor is a payment into an account that he believes will pay out one day. She may know it and enjoy the service. She may have no idea. Either way, he isn't being kind to her. He is waiting to be paid. And under the good manners he is angry that the payment hasn't come.

Put the same test to him. Would he do all this for a man? Would he do it for her if he knew for certain that nothing would ever come of it? If the answer is no, it was never a gift.

If that is you, the way out is plain. Stop the favors that are really payments. Tell her once, outright, what you want, or step back and say nothing. Then see what is left. Either the friendship stands on its own, or it never was one.

## The man who swipes {-}

The chase has a pocket version, and it looks respectable. It's the dating app.

Wanting a wife is good, and so is meeting her through a phone. Plenty of good marriages began that way. But be honest about why you open it. A man looking for a wife reads slowly and writes to one woman. A hungry man swipes. He goes through fifty faces in five minutes and sorts each one in half a second. What he's after is the small jolt when one of them matches.

One man had been on an app for eight years. Then he met a woman at a party, and they decided to get married. He wrote on a forum that he was still swiping, though he never spoke to a single match. He had what the app was supposed to be for, and he couldn't put it down. Somebody answered him, "Don't you get it? You won!"

That is the same itch in better clothes. The swipe is the scroll, with a chance attached.

So the test is simple. Are you looking for a wife, or looking for a hit? If it's a wife, you won't need fifty at a time. If it's a hit, you know what to call it.

## The one girl {-}

The app is fifty faces. The opposite case is one.

You check your phone forty times an hour. You've read her last message until you know it by heart, and you write your answer five times before you send it. You know when she was last online. It feels like love. It even feels like the opposite of the harem, because she is one woman and she is real.

Look closer. You aren't thinking about her. You're thinking about what you want from her, and about how you look to her. What you love is the longing. She has become a picture in your head, and you're texting the picture. It's the harem with one face in it.

A free man can like a woman, tell her so plainly, and get on with his day while he waits for her answer. If she says no, he's sorry, and he is still himself. That's the kind of man a woman can say yes to.

## If she has said no {-}

This part is not for every reader. But there may be a woman who has told you no, and you are still writing to her.

Remember the woman with the caller who wouldn't stop. She was told to say no once and outright, to give no reason, not to let him down gently, and never to answer again. Now hear that list from the other side.

She may never have said the word. Many women don't. They want to be kind, or polite, or they are a little afraid of how you'll take it. So her no comes as a slow reply, then "I'm busy this week", then silence. Don't wait to hear it said outright. A woman who wants to hear from you makes that easy to tell. If you have to wonder, she has answered.

You wouldn't call yourself a stalker. You would say that you're persistent, or in love. From where she stands, she said no and she meant it. Every message since has frightened her a little more. That isn't love. Love wants her good, and she has told you what her good is.

The best thing you can do is also the only thing. Respect her decision, and stop all contact today. Send no last message, no apology and no explanation. Stay away from her house and from her pictures.

- **It is the only way to leave with your dignity.** Every further message spends a little more of it.
- **Expect nothing from it.** If she were ever to think differently, it would be her doing and on her terms. It would never be because you pushed.
- **You are thinking like a starving man.** She is not the only woman in the world. There are women who would be glad of you, and you could be with one who wants you back.

It will feel hard for a few days. That is the same itch in another coat, and it passes. If you find you can't stop, tell a doctor or a counselor today. In many places this is also a crime.

Five men, then: the one at the party, the one who waits, the one who swipes, the one texting a picture in his head, and the one who can't let go. Each of them thinks he is the hunter. Look again at who is doing the leading.
