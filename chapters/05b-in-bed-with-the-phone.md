# In Bed with the Phone {#in-bed-with-the-phone}

If you're like most men, the phone is in your hand before your head has touched the pillow. What follows starts without any decision at all. Try to remember each time you masturbated last week. You'll recall one or two, the most recent or the one you'd waited for, and the others have vanished. That's how little they meant.

"I do it when I'm bored." But boredom is a state of mind. Give a man something that absorbs him and he can go for days without a thought of it. Leave him lying on a bed with nothing to do and a screen full of half-dressed strangers. Now there's nothing between him and the Little Monster's tapping. He isn't bored and then tempted. He's itchy, and with nothing to distract him he notices it.

The psychiatrist Alok Kanojia says the number one cause of relapse is not strong emotion. It is boredom. His reason takes one line: "if you've got a gap in your day porn will fill it".

The most famous fall in the Bible began in an empty hour. David, Israel's greatest king, was alone on his roof on an idle evening, while his army was away at war. You know that hour.

And far from curing boredom, the habit breeds it. Afterward you're heavy and dull. You don't get up and call a friend or fix the shelf. You lie there and scroll. Boredom is a signal. It tells a man he ought to be doing something. The habit mutes the signal and leaves the boredom.

## "It clears my head" {-}

This is the opposite claim: now the habit is supposed to help you concentrate. You sit down to the essay or the tax return and you're stuck. The itch nags, so you "get it out of the way", go back to the desk, and conclude that it helped.

It didn't.

It removed a distraction that it had planted, and it sent you back with a duller head. The man who never had the habit got stuck, stayed in his chair and got unstuck.

## The shower and the quiet evening {-}

"It relaxes me."

Does it? There's a locked door, and one ear is listening for footsteps on the stairs. You delete the history and cover your tracks. In the shower you keep an eye on the clock, because people are waiting for the bathroom. A man who is relaxing doesn't behave like that. A burglar does.

Take the morning shower. It's the habit at its most mechanical: warm water, a locked door, five minutes, the same as yesterday. Nothing in you asked for it. The shower asked for it.

Now take the evening. A man comes in from work, eats, puts his feet up, and he's content. The man with the habit can't quite be. He has another appetite waiting, and until it's fed he's not fully in the room. He'll hurry the children's bedtime story. He'll be short with his wife for no reason she can find. He'll wait for the house to go to bed. Afterward, for half an hour, he's as calm as other men are all evening.

Men call it the perfect end to the day. It's a debt collector at the door.

Why is a free man relaxed without it? And why can't you relax after one or two days without? Read what men write in the first week of a willpower "streak": pacing, snapping at people, counting the hours. That's a man robbed of the one comfort he believes he has. He has forgotten what ordinary peace feels like.

## "I can't sleep without it" {-}

For many men this is the last excuse to fall, because the fear feels so practical. "I've got work in the morning. I can't lie awake."

You already know who keeps you awake. Here is how he does it. The bed is the cue. Lights out is the cue. So every night the Little Monster wakes up just as you lie down, and you can't settle until he's been fed. Then you sleep, and you thank the habit.

It's a poor sedative in any case. The session runs late and the phone runs later. At seven the next morning a man drags himself out of bed and blames the mattress.

When you stop, you may have a few nights when the old cue fires and you lie there for a while. Don't dread them. Lie there and smile, because you know what's dying. If you still sleep badly a month from now, that is a different problem, and a doctor is the person to see. But most men are soon sleeping the way they did as boys. David wrote of it in Psalm 4: "In peace I will both lay myself down and sleep, for you, LORD alone, make me live in safety." He says he'll sleep in peace. He doesn't say he'll sleep worn out.

## Energy {-}

Most men know the flat ten minutes afterward. Fewer notice what the habit does to their energy across years, because it happens so slowly.

The tiredness gets filed under "getting older". Your friends are tired too, so it seems normal. At twenty-eight you talk like an old man, and you assume that drive belongs to teenagers.

Suppose I could lift you out of today and set you down a few weeks after stopping, just for one morning. You'd feel the difference all at once, and that alone would do it. You wouldn't say, "Is this how good it gets?" You'd say, "Had I really sunk that far?" I can't carry you there. You can walk.

I can't prove that difference with a lab report. It is testimony, and the testimony is remarkably consistent. Men say the energy comes back, and many say it starts within days. There is one condition. If you stop by willpower, feeling deprived, the gloom will swallow every gain. Stop this book's way and you get to enjoy each gain as it comes.

Underneath all of these occasions lies one more, and it is the saddest.
