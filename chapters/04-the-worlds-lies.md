# The World's Lies

> "Be careful that you don't let anyone rob you through his philosophy and vain deceit, after the tradition of men"
>
> — Paul, in Colossians 2:8

The flesh supplies the itch. The world supplies the reasons to scratch it.

Picture a man with a rash. He goes to the doctor. Call him Dr. Scratchmore. The doctor leans in and says, "Keep scratching. More. MORE!" You'd walk out and find another doctor. That's the world's whole medical advice for this itch, and you've been taking it for years.

![](images/illustrations/worlds-lies-keep-scratching.png){.illustration width=80%}

The itch is small. The lies are far bigger, and once you believe them they move into your head and do all the talking. That's the Big Monster: every lie you were told about the habit, and every lie you told yourself.

## Who's behind it {-}

The Bible says plainly who runs the world's sales pitch. John, one of Jesus's closest friends, wrote that "the whole world lies in the power of the evil one." Jesus called Satan "the prince of this world." Paul called him "the god of this world," who "has blinded the minds of the unbelieving."

Blinded is the word. He doesn't drag a man up the stairs. He just makes sure the man never sees what he's carrying up there.

If you don't believe in a devil, look at who profits. Every site, app and ad that keeps you scratching gets paid when you do. You don't need horns to explain that.

If you lived alone on an island with the Little Monster, starving him would be simple. You don't. You live in the devil's world, and he wants you sedated, weak and small. A man like that is no trouble to anybody.

And the world doesn't work alone. It sells the lies. Your own mind repeats them, in your voice, at the worst moment. Your fear finishes the job: fear of a long evening with nothing to do, fear of a bad day without him, fear that you'll try and fail again. The world, your mind and your fear all push the same way. Up the stairs, with his plate.

## The lies about the hand {-}

Start with the loudest lies, the ones about masturbation. Your grandfather was told it would make him go blind, grow hair on his palms and land him in an asylum. Nonsense, and it got laughed out of the room. But when the scare stories went in the trash, the truth went with them. Now a boy hears the opposite sermon, just as confident. It has six points.

**"Everyone does it."** Nearly true. But a crowded trap is still a trap. Since when do you take directions from a crowd that's lost?

![](images/illustrations/worlds-lies-crowded-trap.png){.illustration width=80%}

**"It's healthy. It's harmless. Doctors say so."** Medicine says it doesn't hurt your body. Fine, but the doctor is answering a question you never asked. You didn't ask about your prostate. You asked why you can't stop, and why you feel like a smaller man afterward. A Christian writer put the distinction neatly: "But just because something isn't harmful physically doesn't mean it's not harmful spiritually."

The experts don't even agree among themselves. The manual American psychiatrists use, the DSM-5, doesn't list porn or sex addiction at all. The World Health Organization does. Since 2019 its list of diseases, the ICD-11, has included compulsive sexual behavior disorder. So when you hear that the experts say it's fine, ask which experts.

Then ask whose life is backing the advice. Picture a man who finally tells a counselor about his habit. The counselor tells him it's not really that bad. A few sessions later, the counselor is cheering on his idea of a threesome with his girlfriend. Is that medicine? Or is it somebody feeding the same monster, telling him what they tell themselves?

Experts are people. They live in the same world and hear the same pitch. Plenty of counselors help men get free, and if you need one, go find one. Just pick one whose life matches the advice.

**"I'm not doing anything wrong."** Then say it out loud. Say it to your wife, or your mother, or the father of the woman on the screen. A thing that's fine can be said at the dinner table. You've never said this one to anybody.

**"It's natural."** So is plenty you wouldn't copy. One preacher, who was bound by masturbation for years himself, answers in nine words: "We also see in nature where pigs eat trash." Natural isn't the same as good.

![](images/illustrations/worlds-lies-pigs-eat-trash.png){.illustration width=80%}

**"A man needs the release."** You need air, water and food. Skip those and you die. No man has ever died from going a week without it, on a camping trip or anywhere else.

**"It keeps you out of worse trouble."** It doesn't even work. Ask the men who quit porn but kept masturbating. The hand let the porn back in. And nowhere does the Bible tell a man to commit one sin to avoid another.

## The opposite lie {-}

The hype runs the other way too. Stop, whole corners of the internet promise, and you'll get superpowers. The biggest forum of them all started with a thread about a study claiming a testosterone spike after seven days. The study was retracted in 2021.

All that hype ought to get men out. Instead it keeps them in, because it turns stopping into a feat. You count days, take vows, call yourself a warrior. That's a lot of armor for evicting a pipsqueak.

![](images/illustrations/worlds-lies-armor-for-a-pipsqueak.png){.illustration width=80%}

Then day thirty comes, the prize hasn't shown up, and back you go.

Both sermons, "it's good for you" and "quitting gives you powers", agree on one thing: stopping is a big deal. It isn't.

## The quiet pulpit {-}

You'd expect the church to push back. Mostly it says nothing. A man can sit in a pew for thirty years and never hear masturbation named once. A boy hears jokes about it on the school bus and silence in church. He decides it doesn't matter, or that he's the only one. Some kind and respected Christian teachers even said it was fine, to spare teenagers sleepless nights of guilt.

## Two things to be fair about {-}

Its defenders have two fair points, and I'll grant both.

First, the Bible never mentions masturbation by name. If you were hoping for a verse that says "you shall not", there isn't one, and this book won't pretend there is. But Jesus didn't need to name the act. He went behind it. In Matthew 5:28 he put the sin in the look and in the heart, and that's where masturbation lives. Ask yourself how often it's happened without lust, and you have your answer. The Catholic Church goes further. It teaches that the act is wrong in itself, because sex was made to join a husband and wife. Many Protestant pastors get to the same place through Jesus's words about the look.

Second, the numbers. I have to borrow from porn here, because porn is what gets studied. In one 2017 study of adults who watch porn, nearly nine in ten weren't compulsive in the sense a clinic uses. About one in eight used porn little and still felt great distress over it. If that's you, the war has hurt you as much as the habit. This book means to end both. It doesn't call every man with the habit a clinical addict. Its judgment is moral, not medical. And you didn't pick up a book with this title because you were fine.

None of this is a ban. You can still do it tonight, and nobody's stopping you. Just know who's asking. It's the pipsqueak upstairs, and he's hungry.

Those are the lies about the hand. Porn has its own set, and somebody else pays for those too.
