# The Plaster Egg {#the-plaster-egg}

Right at the peak, your brain tells you this beats sex. Don't argue with it. It's an honest report of what you feel.

Now look at what it's reporting on.

## The bird and the egg {-}

Last century a Dutch scientist named Niko Tinbergen offered nesting birds plaster eggs. He made them bigger than real eggs, with brighter color and bolder spots. Given the choice, most birds picked the fakes over their own. One fake was so big the mother bird kept sliding off it, and she climbed back on anyway.

![](images/illustrations/feels-good-the-plaster-egg.png){.illustration width=80%}

Nothing was wrong with those birds. Their wiring said "big spotted egg, sit", and somebody built an egg that shouted louder than a real one. Scientists have a name for a fake like that. They call it a supernormal stimulus: the real thing turned up past anything nature ever makes, so it pulls harder than the real thing does.

## You are that bird {-}

Here it is in plain words.

**YOU ARE THAT BIRD.**

You're built to be drawn to a woman. Her face, her figure, her curves. Nothing's dirty about that. It's wiring, and it's there so a man will want a wife and stay with her.

Now look at what your phone shows that wiring before breakfast. Breasts a surgeon has enlarged. Skin an app has smoothed. Lighting set up by a professional. Waists pulled in and everything else pushed out with a slider. Your great-grandfather could have lived to ninety and never seen one woman shaped like that. You see a hundred in ten minutes, each one new, and not one of them can turn you down.

Nothing in your wiring was built for that, so it scores it off the chart. That's the plaster egg: bigger, brighter and bolder than anything real. The Little Monster doesn't care whether it's real. He only cares that it's loud.

![](images/illustrations/egg-you-are-that-bird.png){.illustration width=80%}

## Hours for one picture {-}

Take the woman on Instagram who seems to wake up looking like that. She doesn't. Count what went into the one picture you scrolled past in half a second. An hour or two of hair and makeup. A ring light. Two hundred shots from the same spot: chin up, chin down, hip out, try again. Then the best one goes through filters and editing apps until her own mother would squint at it. Then it goes up at the hour her numbers say men are online.

![](images/illustrations/egg-take-two-hundred.png){.illustration width=80%}

Some accounts go further. They post two versions of the same picture to different people and keep whichever one gets more clicks. Marketers call it A/B testing. It means you're the lab rat. Somebody tweaks the egg, watches whether you sit on it, and tweaks it again.

![](images/illustrations/egg-lab-rat.png){.illustration width=80%}

## The beetle and the bottle {-}

If the bird doesn't make you laugh, this will. In the early 1980s, two scientists in Western Australia found male jewel beetles trying to mate with empty beer bottles. The bottles were brown and shiny, with a row of little bumps around the bottom. To a beetle, that looked like the biggest, glossiest female he'd ever seen. The males crawled all over the glass and ignored the real females nearby. Some stayed on so long that ants swarmed them and ate them.

The beetle thinks he's found the date of his life. He's making love to litter.

![](images/illustrations/egg-beetle-bottle.png){.illustration width=80%}

That's you at one in the morning with your phone. Not because you're a pervert. Because somebody built a better bottle.

## Made to order {-}

The screen beats the real thing on one more count. It's made to order. You pick the woman, the act and the angle. In your head it's you in the picture, and everything goes exactly your way. Nothing real ever goes exactly your way.

That's the giveaway. A woman who does just what you want, when you want it, with no will of her own, isn't a woman. She's a puppet, and you're working it. You wrote her lines. You're alone in that room, feeling what your body feels when somebody's with you.

A plaster egg never hatches. The bird warms it for nothing, and her own eggs go cold.

There's one more part of the kick, and it has nothing to do with the woman on the screen. It's the locked door.
