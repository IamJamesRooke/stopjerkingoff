# The First Touch {#the-first-touch}

It's morning. You wake up hard, as a healthy man does. Nobody's home, and you have half an hour.

You don't decide anything. Your hand drifts down, only to adjust. One touch. Then a second, a little longer. It feels good, and better if it's been a while. Somewhere around the third, the question is over. You never said yes. You're just a man who doesn't want to stop.

That's how it nearly always happens. Nobody sits up in bed and announces a plan.

## The needle {-}

Ask where a heroin addict makes his decision. It isn't when the drug hits. By then nobody's left to decide. It's before the needle goes in. Once it's in, it's in. A man who's swallowed the pill can't unswallow it on the stairs.

The first touch is the needle going in. After it he can still stop, but now he's arguing with the drug. The cheap place to choose is before.

## The free sample {-}

Picture the lie at that moment. It's a dealer on a street corner, and this morning he isn't selling. He's giving.

"First one's free. Just a taste. You don't have to buy anything."

You'd laugh and keep walking. Everybody knows what the free one is for. The first touch is the free sample. It costs nothing and commits you to nothing, and that's exactly how it works.

## Just one chip {-}

The makers of Lay's potato chips once ran a famous slogan. "Betcha can't eat just one." It may be the most honest ad ever written. They weren't bragging about the taste. They were telling you how the product works.

The first chip isn't a small helping of chips. It's what opens the bag.

## Fasting is easier than dieting {-}

Anyone who's tried both knows it's easier to fast than to diet.

The dieter has to stop mid-meal, food in his mouth, a hundred times a week. The man who fasts decides once, before he's hungry, and then there's nothing to argue about.

"A little, and then I'll stop" is a diet. It's the hardest thing you could ask of yourself. None is the easy version.

## Where the choice is {-}

This is no rule to grit your teeth over. It's a map of where the choice is. The choice is in the first two seconds, while you're still wholly yourself. It costs nothing there.

So once your No is said, the instruction fits in four words, and your grandmother could have given it. Stop touching your junk.

When you wake up hard, it's plumbing. It's not a summons. Get up and make the coffee.

The lie has one more trick. It points at another man and says, "Look at him. He has it under control."
