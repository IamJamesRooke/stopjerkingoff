# At Least I'm Not Him {#at-least-im-not-him}

Three men in the trap have an "at least" that sounds like a good one. Each is sure he is not as bad as the next man. Take them in turn.

## The married man {-}

The married man has an "at least" of his own. At least he has never cheated. He thought the wedding would end it. Many of us did. He found out within the month that it had come with him on the honeymoon.

He believes he's owed a release every day or two. His wife is not always of the same mind. She's tired, or pregnant, or away, or they've had a fight. So he "fills the gap". Her tiredness is never his excuse, and the habit doesn't stay inside the gap anyway. Soon it's happening on nights when she would have said yes, because it's quicker, because it asks nothing, because nobody has to be won over. In time he's half glad when she turns over, since it hands him his excuse.

And when she does reach for him, he's already spent, or his mind is on a reel she isn't in. He gave the best of it to nobody, and she gets what's left over.

If the habit really gave a man so much, he wouldn't need a wife. It gives nothing, and it takes from her. One husband wrote, after he finally saw it:

> "I was beside myself over how I had grieved God and my wife"

Whatever you believe about marriage, you promised her this part of yourself. A man who takes it back in secret, a little at a time, is keeping something from her. Some part of her knows it, even if she couldn't tell you what's wrong.

## The "imagination only" man {-}

The next man would never go near a screen, and that is his "at least". "I don't look at anything. It's all in my head."

Everybody starts there, and the scenes don't stay tame. Soon the mind wants a real face: a coworker, a friend's wife, the girl who serves your coffee. Then it wants "just a photo, to get started".

The head is never empty, and you knew that already. The imagination-only man is proud that no one else's sin is on his screen. But he's the director, the casting agent and the whole audience of his own private movie theater, and the women in it never agreed to appear.

Worst of all is the husband who believes he's safe because he only ever pictures his wife. He's still teaching his body that the finish line is himself, alone, on his own schedule. The version of her in his head never says "not like that" and never needs anything. Then he's puzzled that the real woman feels like harder work than she used to.

## The church guy who has never told anyone {-}

The last man makes the opposite mistake. He doesn't think he is better than the other men. He thinks he is the only one. He serves on the welcome team, leads a small group, perhaps plays in the band. He has done this since he was fourteen and has never said one word about it to a living soul.

On Sunday he's at the front with his hands raised. On Saturday night he was where he always is. He has confessed it so many times, in the same words, that he's embarrassed to bring it up with God again. So he's stopped bringing it up, and after a while he's stopped bringing much else up either.

Peter once asked Jesus how many times he had to forgive his brother. Seven times? Jesus said seventy times seven. God doesn't hold himself to a lower standard than the one he gave Peter.

It leaks into everything he might have done. He turns down the mission trip, lets someone else lead, and never says why. He is enlisted, and he keeps reporting sick.

He looks down the row at the other men and assumes they're all clean. They're looking back down the row at him and assuming the same. The habit is feeding on that silence.

## One trap {-}

That is every man in the trap. You don't need to envy any of them, and you don't need to look down on any of them, and you don't need to be one. Life is simply better outside. A nineteen-year-old who got out wrote:

> "From that moment on, I began rejoicing that Jesus had delivered me from this 5 year addiction"

He didn't write that he was managing it, or that he'd reached day ninety. He wrote that he'd been delivered, and that he was rejoicing.

Jesus told a story about two men who went up to the temple to pray. The first was a Pharisee, and the Pharisees were the strictest religious men of that day. He stood and prayed, "God, I thank you that I am not like the rest of men". Every man in the trap says a version of that prayer. "At least I don't look at porn." "At least it's only once a week." "At least it's only in my head." "At least I've never cheated." "At least I'm not married yet." The second man was a tax collector, and Jesus said he was the one who went home right with God that day. He was a man everyone despised. He compared himself with nobody. He only said, "God, be merciful to me, a sinner!"

There aren't really any types. There's one trap, and men are standing at different depths in it. The way out is the same for all of them.

Every one of these men is feeding the same small monster, in one or more of the five ways. So take the five one at a time, beginning where lust always begins.
