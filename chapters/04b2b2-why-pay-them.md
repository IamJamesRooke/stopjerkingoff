# Why Pay Them? {#why-pay-them}

> "Have no fellowship with the unfruitful deeds of darkness, but rather even reprove them."
>
> — Ephesians 5:11

Maybe Satan is a cartoon to you, and witchcraft is incense and nonsense. You don't have to believe in either one to see what's being aimed at.

Look at what keeps getting poked:

- The church your grandmother knelt in.
- The vow a man and a woman make for life.
- A girl's modesty.
- A father's place at the table.

Provoking pays, and those are the easiest things to provoke with. That's no accident. It's the business plan.

Now picture a comedian whose whole act is mocking your mother's faith and laughing at your wedding ring. Maybe he's funny. Every week he holds out his hand for twenty dollars.

Would you keep paying him?

![](images/illustrations/mud-would-you-pay-him.png){.illustration width=80%}

That's the deal you're offered every time you hit play. They want three things from you:

- **Your time.** Every hour you watch is an hour they sell.
- **Your money.** The ticket, the subscription, the seventy-dollar game.
- **Your attention.** The most valuable of the three, because that's what they sell to advertisers.

Even if witchcraft is pure nonsense, the business is real, and it isn't neutral. It's shown you what it thinks of what you hold sacred. And look at what it's selling between the jokes. It's an ad for the same appetite you've been carrying dinner upstairs to. Nobody takes your dime. You hand it over.

## The first kiss was booked for the gasp {-}

One more boot needs talking about, and it needs care. So start with the line, because it's the same for everybody. Sex belongs in marriage, between a husband and his wife. That's not only because the Bible says so. Marriage is the one place sex comes with a promise, and nobody is being used. That line rules out the straight couple in the show, and the straight couple in the video. It rules out what's on your screen tonight.

Now think back to the first famous same-sex kisses on stage and screen. In 2003, at the MTV Video Music Awards, Madonna kissed Britney Spears and Christina Aguilera. In 2008 Katy Perry had a hit called "I Kissed a Girl," about doing it "just to try it." Whatever you believe about how people live, notice how those moments were made. The 2003 kiss was kept secret before the show, so the gasp would be real. Then the next show had to go further, because a gasp only works once.

Gay people didn't write that playbook. Marketers did, and they've sold straight shock the exact same way for decades. It's the same ladder porn climbs, and the same ladder your own habit climbed. First it shocks. Then it bores. Then it needs something stronger.

The point isn't who's kissing whom. The point is that someone was selling your shock, and you bought it.

## Wiping your feet {-}

Paul, the first-century apostle, told the first Christians not to cheer that stuff on, but to expose it. In his letter to the church in Ephesus he wrote, "For it is a shame even to speak of the things which are done by them in secret."

Today it isn't done in secret. It's done at halftime.

You don't have to start a boycott. You don't have to picket Netflix. You only have to see it for what it is: mud on purpose, carried in by people who are proud of the stain. Next time the ad plays or the halftime show starts, just say it in your head. That's a boot. Once you see that, you'll be amazed at how little of it you miss.

![](images/illustrations/why-pay-thats-a-boot.png){.illustration width=80%}

There's one more kind of mud. This kind isn't on a stage. It's on your phone right now, pouting at you.
