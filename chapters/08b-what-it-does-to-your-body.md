# What It Does to Your Body {#what-it-does-to-your-body}

The doctors say the act itself does no physical harm, and I won't argue. But there are three places where the body gets pulled into it, and you should hear them told honestly.

The plainest is sleep. The habit lives late at night. It takes the hour, and then it takes the sleep after the hour. A man who drags himself out of bed every morning is paying a physical price, whatever the textbooks say about the act.

The second is trouble in bed with a real woman. Some men who lean heavily on porn find that they can't get ready with a partner, or can't stay ready. Be careful with this one. The research is mixed, and nobody has proved that porn is the cause. Doctors say that in a young man the usual cause is worry, and a man carrying a secret has plenty of that. Drink, medicines and illness can do it too. If it has gone on for weeks, see a doctor.

The third has a name of its own, and it needs more room.

## Death grip {-}

Death grip is men's own slang, and the name fits. A hand can squeeze far harder than a woman's body ever will, and it can move far faster. Now add years of the same hard, hurried, usually dry habit, always done the same way. The body learns. It comes to answer to one thing only, which is its owner's hand.

Then a real woman arrives. She is warm and soft, and to a body trained on a fist she feels like almost nothing. Some men can't finish with her, however long it goes on. Some can't stay ready. A woman on a forum for men who are stopping wrote this about the man she had been with: "when he did have sex he felt nothing".

Sex therapists and urologists see these men regularly, and they have their own drier names for it. Alok Kanojia is an American psychiatrist with a large following, who goes by Dr. K. In 2023 he put it as bluntly as a doctor can. A tight, dry hand, he said, "feels different from a vagina". A man who knows nothing else has trained his body away from a real woman.

What is actually going on? Most doctors describe it as training. Think of a man who salts his food a little more heavily every year. His tongue isn't ruined, but plain food now tastes of nothing. Some doctors go further, and say that years of dry friction may dull the skin and the nerves themselves. That may be so. Nobody has proved it, and you will hear it said with more confidence than the evidence allows. The training explanation has the most behind it. It is also the hopeful one, because a body that learned one thing can learn another.

A medical student told his story on the NoFap forum. With his first girlfriend, "even after months, still no orgasm for me. I couldn't even get close." Then comes the sentence every man should hear: "She started to think it was her fault, but deep inside, I knew what the problem was."

Let's keep this honest. Death grip isn't an official diagnosis, and nobody has proved the cause. A study published in 2023 looked at several hundred young men. Those with erection trouble were about twice as likely to masturbate in these rough ways. That shows a link and no more. Some doctors doubt it's common. And if this is your trouble, see a doctor, because medicines and other conditions can do the same thing.

Notice what the specialists who treat this prescribe, including the ones who say the habit is harmless. They usually ask a man to stop for a while, and then to change the way he does it. This book asks you to stop for good. That is my conviction, not their prescription. If a sex therapist gives you exercises, that is treatment. Tell the therapist your convictions at the start. A good one will work within them. And the medical student? A little over a month after he stopped: "I had an orgasm during sex, for the first time in my life." For other men it has taken many months. Nobody can promise you a date. What the men who stop do report is that the body often recovers.

The body can mend. So why has a list like this never once made you stop?
