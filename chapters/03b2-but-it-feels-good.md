# But It Feels Good {#but-it-feels-good}

Yes. It does.

For a few seconds at the end, it feels very good. The orgasm is real. The rush is real. Your body was built to deliver it, and it delivers. A book that told you otherwise would be lying, and you'd be right to close it.

So how can this book say the habit gives you nothing?

## Sugar {-}

A can of cola tastes great. Nobody denies it. Drink six a day for twenty years, then ask what the taste was worth. The taste was real every time. It was also the bait.

"It feels good" and "it does me good" are two different sentences. The whole trap lives in the gap between them. Every drug on earth feels good for a moment. Nobody counts that as a point in its favor. That moment is how the drug works.

A fish would tell you the worm was delicious.

## "One little vice" {-}

Maybe you'll grant all that and shrug. It's like candy. It's like a can of cola. Every man gets one little vice, and this one isn't going to hurt me.

Look at how you treat candy. You eat it in front of people. You don't lock the door. You don't wipe the wrapper out of your history. You've never finished a chocolate bar and stared at the ceiling.

A little vice stays little. One cola is one cola. This one doesn't hold still. It wants more, and it wants newer, and you've been giving it more for years.

And a little vice is one you could drop on a Tuesday and never think about again. You've tried to drop this one. How many times?

It isn't candy. Candy never made you promise anything.

## Take the few seconds apart {-}

Three things are mixed together in that moment. Pull them apart.

**The first is relief.** The banging on the floor stops. This is most of what you feel, and it isn't pleasure at all. It's an irritation ending, like a car alarm that finally shuts off. The man who never started doesn't need it, because nothing's banging in his house.

**The second is the rush itself.** This part is real, and it lasts a few seconds. So weigh it like anything you buy. On one side, those seconds. On the other, the hour before, hunting for the right video. Then the flat ten minutes after, with the screen dark and your mood darker. Then the itch, set going again for tomorrow.

The rush shrinks, too. It was bigger at fifteen than it is now. It'll be smaller next year. The price goes up, and the portion goes down.

Look at where the excitement sits. It isn't at the end. It's in the lead-up: the door clicking shut, the scroll, the search for the right video. That's why so many men drag it out. A man will spend forty minutes choosing the video for an ending that takes six seconds. He has picked jobs with less care.

**The third is what the rush is for.** A body doesn't hand out its strongest pleasure for nothing. That rush was built to tie a man to a woman. The habit spends it on nobody, behind a locked door, then wipes it up.

## The next minute {-}

Here's a simple test for a real pleasure. Ask how you feel one minute later.

A minute after a good dinner with friends, you're glad you went. A minute after a swim in the sea, you're glad you went. A man who's made love to a wife he loves is glad a minute later.

A minute after this, you wish you hadn't. You clear the history. You can't meet your own eyes in the bathroom mirror.

Judge a thing by the whole of it, the bait and the hook together.

## What the claim is {-}

Get the claim exactly right. This book has never said it doesn't feel good. It says the habit gives you nothing. The few seconds are real, and they're the bait.

Where that pleasure belongs is a question for later, and it gets a straight answer. For now, see the few seconds for what they are. They're the one thing you've been defending, and they're how the trap is set.

One more thing about that lead-up. It never stays the same size.
