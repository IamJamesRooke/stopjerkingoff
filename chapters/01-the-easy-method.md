# The Easy Method

The usual way of quitting begins at the foot of a mountain. You stare up at it and take a deep breath. Then you spend weeks dragging yourself uphill, craving and feeling robbed.

This way begins somewhere else. You begin with relief, like a man walking out of the doctor's office with the all-clear. And the feeling doesn't fade. Years from now you'll look back at this stretch of your life and scratch your head over why you ever did it. When you meet men who are still stuck, you'll feel sorry for them. You won't envy them for a second.

How? The method has one aim: to change the way you think about the habit.

That's all. It won't make you stronger, and it won't make you more disciplined. It will change what you see when you look at porn, at masturbation, and later at the pull women have on you. Once you see it clearly, stopping is easy.

## Why the normal way is so hard {-}

The normal way of quitting goes like this. You write down everything the habit costs you: the guilt, the tiredness, the lost time, the distance from God. Then you tell yourself:

"If I can just hold out long enough, the urge will finally die, and then I'll be happy."

It sounds sensible. A few men do get free this way. But it is the hard road, and most men who take it end up where one man on a Christian forum did. He wrote that everything that depended on his own effort failed him sooner or later.

Why is it so hard? There are two reasons.

**1. Guilt and fear make you reach for it.** What does a man do when he feels ashamed, anxious and worthless? He looks for comfort. And what has he trained himself to use for comfort? Tell a man the habit is ruining him and you've just handed him one more reason to escape into it tonight. A counselor on a Christian recovery blog saw it clearly: "Shame is the problem for so many, many Christians." This is why the scare videos and the thundering sermons have never freed you. They load the gun. A man who tries to quit out of desperation is working against himself, because desperation is the very thing that drives him back. That is true of porn, and it is just as true of the hand.

**2. Every reason to stop makes stopping harder.** That sounds crazy, so take it slowly. First, your reasons tell you that you *ought* to give something up. So part of you goes on believing there is something to give up: a comfort, a treat, an old friend. You feel like a man being marched away from his only pleasure. Second, your reasons point your eyes at the wrong question. You don't do it because of the reasons you should stop. Nobody opens the site or shuts the bathroom door *because* it leaves him flat and guilty. The real question is the other one: why do you want to do it at all?

The man from the Christian forum, the one whose own effort failed him, spotted the trap inside all that effort. The harder he tried to stop thinking about porn, the more it became the center of his thoughts. Swap in "masturbation" and nothing changes.

## Three questions {-}

So for now we're going to put your reasons for stopping on a shelf. All of them, including the good ones, including the godly ones. We'll take them down again later, when they can no longer be used as a stick to beat you with.

In their place, look straight at what you do and ask:

1. **What is it actually doing for me?**
2. **Do I actually enjoy it?**
3. **Why am I doing this to myself?**

The rest of the book exists to prove one thing to you: the habit does nothing for you. Nothing at all. The porn doesn't. The hand doesn't.

Be clear about what that means. Nobody is saying the costs are bigger than the benefits. Every man already knows that, and it has never helped him. There are no benefits.

Not one.

The relaxation, the sleep, the stress relief, the "release", the thrill of something new, the feeling of being wanted: each of them is an illusion. We'll take them apart one at a time. But you shouldn't have to take that on trust, so here is one of them now.

## A sample: "It helps me sleep" {-}

It does seem to. You masturbate, and you drop off. Case closed?

Ask who was keeping you awake. You slept like a stone when you were nine. The man who never started this is asleep ten minutes after his head touches the pillow, and nothing has helped him. You are the only one of the three lying there restless, and you are the only one with a squatter banging on the floor.

So the habit didn't give you sleep. It took your sleep, and every night it rents a little of it back to you. Then you thank it.

A man who wears tight shoes all day for the pleasure of taking them off isn't enjoying a benefit. Every "benefit" on that list works the same way, and each one gets its turn.

## What to do tonight {-}

Tonight, do three things. None of them is "try harder".

1. **Call off the war.** No vow, no streak, no cutting down. You may also stop hating yourself this evening. It never helped once.
2. **Watch.** Nobody is telling you to sin, and if you have already stopped, stay stopped. But if you fall, keep your eyes open. Notice how it starts, how long the good part lasts, and how the ten minutes afterward feel.
3. **Keep reading, in order.** A chapter or two a night is plenty.

If something loosened in your chest at "call off the war", that is the first of the relief. As you read, the wish to do it will shrink without any effort from you. Let it.

There's no "one last time" ceremony waiting for you in the closing pages. There is only a plain No, and you'll be glad to say it. It is not the old vow. The old vow was a promise made in your own strength, before you understood the trap, and you know how long those lasted. This one you'll say with your eyes open, and if you pray, you'll say it leaning on God.

## Who is telling you this {-}

You should know who is talking. I'll keep it short, because this book is about you.

I was an awkward boy who couldn't talk to girls. At sixteen I found masturbation, and it became my drug, several times a day. At twenty-one I got fast internet, and porn came with it. Nobody could have told. I did well at everything people could see.

Then I had my first girlfriend, and I couldn't finish. A real woman was no match for what porn had taught me to expect. So I kept the porn.

The habit cost me three relationships, an engagement and a marriage. After that I went the other way, woman after woman, to push a number up. I was on the apps all day. When I liked one, I sat waiting for her texts, and I kept ten conversations going in case she went quiet. When I got sick of the chase, I paid for sex and told myself it was only her job. None of it was ever enough.

Then a woman loved me anyway, baggage and all. I knew that if I didn't fix this, I would wreck that too.

I had been fighting it for years. A free book called [*The Easy Peasy Way to Quit Porn*](https://easypeasymethod.org/) got me off porn for almost a year. I went back because I didn't follow its instructions. Later I joined a men's recovery group at a church. I had always dismissed Christianity as silly superstition. Sitting in that room, I saw it differently. It has stood for two thousand years, and it is the foundation of the civilization I want to live in. I don't understand all of it. But there is a deeper power behind it, and I decided to take it seriously.

The group, though, fought the way everyone fights, with willpower. That seemed backward to me. If grace is real, freedom shouldn't be an arm-wrestle that lasts for life.

That room changed how I saw something else too. Social justice ideology teaches a man that he is a victim. The evil is out there, in the system or in the other side, and he is the one it was done to. Christianity teaches the opposite. It says we are all sinners. Every one of us is the villain in somebody else's story. Left to ourselves we rape, enslave and slaughter, and history is the record of it. The miracle is that we ever do anything else.

Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn was a Russian writer who spent eight years in the Soviet labor camps. He came out saying that the line between good and evil does not run between us and them. It runs through every human heart. Jordan Peterson, a Canadian psychologist, puts it more plainly: clean your own room before you try to fix the world.

I had been praying for months, asking God how to fix the world. Then one night, at the eleventh hour, the answer came, and it wasn't about the world. It was about my own room. Men are sedated. Porn, games, junk food and drink have put a generation half to sleep, and I was one of them. I wanted a book like that free one, but one that went past porn and woke men up. I couldn't find it, so this is that book.

Most of the men you'll meet in these pages, I have been. That is all you need to know about me.

I didn't invent this way of quitting. A man named Allen Carr did, for smokers. His book, *The Easy Way to Stop Smoking*, has helped a great many people put down cigarettes without a fight. The free book that helped me took Carr's method and turned it on porn. This book borrows from both, and takes the same method to lust itself. The credit is theirs. Any mistakes are mine.

This book quotes the Bible, because the best pictures of this trap are in there. If you don't believe, read it as you would any old book that turns out to know you. If you hold another faith, bring it with you. When you hear God or sin mentioned, take it as your conscience talking.

And if you are a Christian, hear one verse early. Romans 8:1 says, "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus". When this book says "sin", it is telling the truth. It is not beating you.

Paul, the apostle who wrote much of the New Testament, told the Christians in Rome to "be transformed by the renewing of your mind". He didn't say to try harder. "Be transformed" is something done to you, by God, as your mind is made new. If you're not a believer, call it changing your mind. Either way, it starts with seeing clearly.

So start with the squatter. If he does nothing for you, why does he keep coming back? The best answer anyone has given is two thousand years old. It is a story about a house.
