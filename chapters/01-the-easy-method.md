# The Easy Method

The usual way of quitting begins at the foot of a mountain. You stare up at it and take a deep breath. Then you spend weeks dragging yourself uphill, craving and feeling robbed.

This way begins somewhere else. You begin with relief, like a man walking out of the doctor's office with the all-clear. And the feeling doesn't fade. Years from now you'll look back at this stretch of your life and scratch your head over why you ever did it. When you meet men who are still stuck, you'll feel sorry for them. You won't envy them for a second.

How? The method has one aim: to change the way you think about the habit.

That's all. It won't make you stronger or more disciplined. It will change what you see when you look at porn, at masturbation, and later at the pull women have on you. Once you see it clearly, stopping is easy.

## Why the normal way is so hard {-}

The normal way of quitting goes like this. You write down everything the habit costs you: the guilt, the tiredness, the lost time, the distance from God. Then you tell yourself:

"If I can just hold out long enough, the urge will finally die, and then I'll be happy."

It sounds sensible. A few men do get free this way. But it is the hard road, and most men who take it end up where one man on a Christian forum did. He wrote that everything that depended on his own effort failed him sooner or later.

Why is it so hard? There are two reasons.

**1. Guilt and fear make you reach for it.** What does a man do when he feels ashamed, anxious and worthless? He looks for comfort. And what has he trained himself to use for comfort? Tell a man the habit is ruining him and you've just handed him one more reason to escape into it tonight. A counselor on a Christian recovery blog saw it clearly: "Shame is the problem for so many, many Christians."

This is why the scare videos and the thundering sermons have never freed you. They load the gun. Desperation is the very thing that drives a man back. That is true of porn, and just as true of the hand.

**2. Every reason to stop makes stopping harder.** That sounds crazy, so take it slowly. First, your reasons tell you that you *ought* to give something up. So part of you goes on believing there is something to give up: a comfort, a treat, an old friend. You feel like a man being marched away from his only pleasure. Second, your reasons point your eyes at the wrong question. You don't do it because of the reasons you should stop. Nobody opens the site or shuts the bathroom door *because* it leaves him flat and guilty. The real question is the other one: why do you want to do it at all?

The man from the Christian forum, the one whose own effort failed him, spotted the trap inside all that effort. The harder he tried to stop thinking about porn, the more it became the center of his thoughts. Swap in "masturbation" and nothing changes.

## Three questions {-}

So for now we're going to put your reasons for stopping on a shelf. All of them, including the good ones, including the godly ones. We'll take them down again later, when they can no longer be used as a stick to beat you with.

In their place, look straight at what you do and ask:

1. **What is it actually doing for me?**
2. **Do I actually enjoy it?**
3. **Why am I doing this to myself?**

The rest of the book exists to prove one thing to you: the habit does nothing for you. Nothing at all. The porn doesn't. The hand doesn't.

Be clear about what that means. Nobody is saying the costs are bigger than the benefits. Every man already knows that, and it has never helped him. There are no benefits.

Not one.

The relaxation, the sleep, the stress relief, the "release", the thrill of something new, the feeling of being wanted: each of them is an illusion. We'll take them apart one at a time. But you shouldn't have to take that on trust, so here is one of them now.

## A sample: "It helps me sleep" {-}

It does seem to. You masturbate, and you drop off. Case closed?

Ask who was keeping you awake. You slept like a stone when you were nine. The man who never started this is asleep ten minutes after his head touches the pillow, and nothing has helped him. You are the only one of the three lying there restless, and you are the only one with a squatter banging on the floor.

So the habit didn't give you sleep. It took your sleep, and every night it rents a little of it back to you. Then you thank it.

A man who wears tight shoes all day for the pleasure of taking them off isn't enjoying a benefit. Every "benefit" on that list works the same way, and each one gets its turn.

## Who is telling you this {-}

This book is about you. But you should know who is talking.

I was the awkward boy who couldn't talk to girls. I never went to a school dance. Who would even want me?

At sixteen I found masturbation, and it became my drug. Several times a day. At twenty-one I got fast internet, and the porn came with it. Nobody could have told. From the outside, my life looked fine.

Then I got my first girlfriend, and I couldn't finish. My body had been trained on a screen. A real woman was in my bed, and I kept the porn.

It cost me three relationships, an engagement and a marriage.

After that I went the other way. I chased woman after woman, to push a number up. I spent my nights in bed with the phone, swiping and sexting. When I liked one, I checked my phone for her texts all day, sick with nerves. To ease the nerves, I kept a dozen other women talking as backups. That is not a man with options. That is a man with thirteen ways to be anxious.

Then I started paying for it. I would like to tell you I was lonely. I wasn't. I could get dates. I just didn't want the dates. I wanted one thing, and I couldn't stay interested in any woman past it. Paying seemed more honest. It was also cheaper, and it took less time.

Men like me have a saying. You don't pay her for the sex. You pay her to leave.

I used to think that was a clever line. Now I hear it. It is a man talking about a human being as something he rents. The habit taught me to talk like that.

And none of it was ever enough.

Then one woman loved me anyway. Baggage and all.

I had already lost a fiancée and a wife to this. I knew how the story went. If I didn't fix it, I would lose her too.

I had been fighting it for years. A free book called *The Easy Peasy Way to Quit Porn* got me off porn for almost a year. Then I went back, because I hadn't followed its instructions. That one is on me, not the book.

Later I joined a men's recovery group at a church. I had always filed Christianity under silly superstition. Sitting in that room, I changed my mind.

Not about everything. I don't understand all of it. But this thing has lasted two thousand years. The civilization I want to live in was built on it. Nothing silly lasts that long or builds that much. There is a deeper power behind it. I don't deny God, and I decided to take all of it seriously.

One idea in that room hit me harder than the rest.

The modern world teaches a creed of social justice, and its hero is the victim. You are good, and they are evil. It calls society "problematic" and its faults "systemic". So the traditional values of the West have to be torn down.

It is a comfortable creed. Nothing is ever your fault. I could have used it. The awkward boy nobody wanted makes a fine victim.

Christianity teaches the opposite. We are all sinners. Every one of us is the villain in somebody else's story.

I am the villain in several. You have just heard about some of the women who would tell it that way.

Human nature is not basically good. Rape, slavery and slaughter are the default. Just read a history book. Morality has to be taught, and every one of us falls short of it.

Aleksandr Solzhenitsyn, a Russian writer who survived the Soviet labor camps, said it best. The line between good and evil doesn't run between one group and another. It runs through every human heart. Mine included.

The group got one thing wrong, as far as I could see. It fought the way everyone fights, with willpower. That seemed backward to me. If grace is real, freedom shouldn't be an arm-wrestle that lasts for life.

Around then I had been praying for months. I was asking God how to fix the world. That is a large request from a man who couldn't manage his own evenings.

Here is what I thought was wrong with the world. Men are sedated.

Look at one ordinary evening. A man comes home tired and pours a drink to take the edge off. Dinner is a burger and fries out of a paper bag, then a sleeve of cookies. He plays a game until midnight. He scrolls for an hour. He finishes on a porn site.

Another man's evening has weed in it, or a betting app. The drug changes. The job is the same. Each one takes the edge off, and the edge is the part of a man that gets things done.

None of it is a crime. All of it is anesthetic.

The feeds do something extra. They leave him anxious, and he can't say why. They leave him half sure that nothing matters anyway. Then he looks at the news and the price of a house, and he decides the game is rigged.

He is not entirely wrong.

But a sedated man can't fix a rigged game. He can't even fix his own room. Some version of that man was me.

Then one night, at the eleventh hour, a thought came. It wasn't about the world. It was about my own room.

Jordan Peterson, a Canadian psychologist, tells young men the same thing. Clean your room before you try to fix the world.

I couldn't clear every drug off that list. I only wanted to be cured of one. Lust.

I looked for a book like the free one, but one that went past porn to lust itself. I couldn't find it. So I made this one, for one reader. Me. It was made to do what years of fighting never did.

Then I gave it away, because I am not the only one.

I am not close to the only one. In that church I saw the problem was bigger than I had thought. The men in the pews have it too.

If I ran a church, I would throw everything at this one thing. Get the men to stop jerking off. Get them to stop watching porn. Get them to stop simping, which means groveling after women for crumbs of attention. Do that, and you change the church for good. It fills with men who have balls, and who spend that energy on doing something.

I don't run a church. I have a book.

Most of the men you'll meet in these pages, I have been. If a man with my record can see through this, so can you.

That is enough about me.

I didn't invent this way of quitting. A man named Allen Carr did, for smokers. His book, *The Easy Way to Stop Smoking*, has helped a great many people put down cigarettes without a fight. The free book that helped me, [*The Easy Peasy Way to Quit Porn*](https://easypeasymethod.org/), took Carr's method and turned it on porn. This book borrows from both, and takes the same method to lust itself. The credit is theirs. Any mistakes are mine.

The best pictures of this trap are in the Bible, so this book quotes it. If you don't believe, read it as you would any old book that turns out to know you. If you hold another faith, bring it with you. When you hear God or sin mentioned, take it as your conscience talking.

And if you are a Christian, hear one verse early. Romans 8:1 says, "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus". When this book says "sin", it is telling the truth. It is not beating you.

Paul, the apostle who wrote much of the New Testament, told the Christians in Rome to "be transformed by the renewing of your mind". He didn't say to try harder. "Be transformed" is something done to you, by God, as your mind is made new. If you're not a believer, call it changing your mind. Either way, it starts with seeing clearly.

## What to do tonight {-}

Tonight, do three things. None of them is "try harder".

1. **Call off the war.** No vow, no streak, no cutting down. You may also stop hating yourself this evening. It never helped once.
2. **Watch.** Nobody is telling you to sin, and if you have already stopped, stay stopped. But if you fall, keep your eyes open. Notice how it starts, how long the good part lasts, and how the ten minutes afterward feel.
3. **Keep reading, in order.** A chapter or two a night is plenty.

If something loosened in your chest at "call off the war", that is the first of the relief. As you read, the wish to do it will shrink without any effort from you. Let it.

This book ends with a plain No, and you'll be glad to say it. It is not the old vow. The old vow was a promise made in your own strength, before you understood the trap, and you know how long those lasted. This one you'll say with your eyes open, and if you pray, you'll say it leaning on God.

So start with the squatter. If he does nothing for you, why does he keep coming back? The best answer anyone has given is two thousand years old. It is a story about a house.
