# Good News, Twice {#good-news-twice}

Some men find all this alarming. "So I'm hooked. That makes it worse." No. It's the best news you've had in years, for two reasons.

**First:** the hook is the only reason you do it. We carry on because we think there's something in it for us. We know the costs are bigger than the gains, but we believe in the gains, and we picture a gap in our lives if the habit went. There is no gap. All you ever got was a few good seconds and the Little Monster going quiet. The habit gives you nothing. It takes.

**Second:** you're not badly hooked. You never were. The Little Monster is tiny. The urge, by itself, is so faint you've mistaken it for boredom for half your life.

Then why do so many men find stopping so hard, suffer for months, and still long for it years later? Because of the second of the two causes. The first is the flesh. The second is the lies. The flesh on its own is easy to handle, and your own life proves it.

## The week away {-}

Think of the week you spent at your parents' place or your in-laws', sleeping in the guest room with the thin walls. The camping trip with your friends. The conference where you shared a hotel room with a coworker. Basic training, where a soldier doesn't get a minute to himself for a month. Did you suffer? Most men hardly think about it. You went days, and it cost you nothing.

![](images/illustrations/good-news-the-camping-trip.png){.illustration width=80%}

Then you got home, shut the bedroom door, and the Little Monster was up and banging before you'd unpacked.

![](images/illustrations/good-news-home-again.png){.illustration width=80%}

What changed? Not your body. Same body in the tent as in the bedroom. The cue came back, and the expectation came with it. He stays quiet when no meal can come, and he bangs the second one might. What you felt wasn't need. It was expectation.

So the flesh's part in the habit is easy to live with, even now, while you're still in it. That's why some men do it once every two weeks all their lives and are every bit as caught as the daily man. It's why a man can go a year clean, have "just one", and be back on the old path within the week.

The flesh isn't the main problem. It's the hook the real problem hangs on. That doesn't make the damage small. Ask any man who's been at it for twenty years what it's taken from him. But the damage isn't permanent. You may have heard the path is worn in for life. That's false.

Grass grows back.

![](images/illustrations/good-news-grass-grows-back.png){.illustration width=80%}

Once you stop walking the path, nothing feeds the Little Monster, and he shrivels fast.

It's never too late. One man wrote that he finally got free in the forty-third year of his life. If you've been at this for decades, or several times a day, take heart. It's just as easy for you as for the man who does it once a month, and in one way it's better. The further down it's taken you, the bigger the relief. Many men go from daily to nothing in one step. The so-called withdrawal is a few restless evenings, and a man can even enjoy them, because he knows what he's hearing. It's the Little Monster's stomach, rumbling.

Now take Paul's words again: "the flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh". Paul never says the flesh is beaten by more flesh, by a harder jaw and a tighter fist. The sentence just before it, Galatians 5:16, reads: "But I say, walk by the Spirit, and you won't fulfill the lust of the flesh." He puts the weight on what to walk toward.

In his letter to the Romans he does tell believers to put to death the deeds of the body. Look how he says it: "if by the Spirit you put to death the deeds of the body, you will live." The Spirit does the heavy lifting.

If you don't pray, the point still holds. Nobody grows self-control by gritting his teeth. It grows by itself once the lies stop feeding the itch.

Back in Galatians, a few verses on, he lists what grows in a man who walks by the Spirit. The last item on the list is self-control. He calls it a fruit, and fruit grows on a tree. Nobody ever got an apple by clenching his teeth.

![](images/illustrations/good-news-clenched-teeth-no-apple.png){.illustration width=80%}

So the flesh is the small part. The big part is what you were told.
