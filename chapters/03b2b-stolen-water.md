# Stolen Water {#stolen-water}

Part of the kick is that it's forbidden. The locked door. The ear cocked for footsteps. The getting away with it. That's real too. The Bible's book of Proverbs, nearly three thousand years old, has a woman called Folly calling to men in the street: "Stolen water is sweet." The very next line says her guests end up among the dead.

Ask what's sweet about it. It isn't the water. It's the stealing. The secret is doing a job the thing itself can't do.

And look at what that tells you. Nobody gets a thrill from sneaking a glass of orange juice. You only sneak what part of you has already judged.

![](images/illustrations/feels-good-sneaking-orange-juice.png){.illustration width=80%}

## Why nobody's forbidding you {-}

This is part of why this book hasn't told you to stop.

Forbid a thing and you sweeten it. Lock the cookie jar and a boy thinks about nothing else. If this book had opened with "Stop now and don't you dare", it would have handed the habit a fresh coat of forbidden and made it sweeter than ever.

So nobody's forbidding you anything. You can feed the Little Monster if you like. Nobody's stopping you. Try stealing from a fridge that's standing open, and see how sweet it tastes.

## Cartoons {-}

When you were a boy, somebody probably told you to stop watching so many cartoons. Maybe they switched the TV off. Maybe you snuck an extra hour when they weren't looking, and it was the best hour of your week.

Now you can watch cartoons all day. Nobody's stopping you. Chances are you don't.

Nobody made you quit. You never counted days without cartoons. You never stood up in a church basement and said, "My name is Mike, and it's been ninety days since my last cartoon." You didn't fight it at all. You grew up, and one day they just didn't interest you anymore. No shame, no streak, no meetings. You moved on.

![](images/illustrations/stolen-cartoons.png){.illustration width=80%}

That's how this ends too. Not with a grown man white-knuckling his way past the TV. With a man who glances at it and thinks, "That's for kids."

## Childish things {-}

The apostle Paul, writing to the church in the Greek city of Corinth, put it in one line: "When I was a child, I spoke as a child, I felt as a child, I thought as a child. Now that I have become a man, I have put away childish things."

He didn't say he fought them off. He didn't say he locked them up and stood guard. He put them away, the way a man boxes up his old toys, because he's outgrown them.

![](images/illustrations/stolen-childish-things.png){.illustration width=80%}

Now picture the habit honestly. A grown man hunched behind a locked door like a monkey at the zoo, one ear on the hallway, then wiping up and slipping out like a boy who's been in his dad's wallet. That's not what a man on a mission looks like.

Take the man with the biggest mission there ever was. On his last night, Jesus ate supper with his friends, washed their feet, and went out into a garden to pray, knowing what the morning would bring. Try to picture him slipping off first for one last secret release before the cross. You can't even hold the picture in your head. It's absurd, and you know it's absurd, believer or not. A man with something to do has no room for it.

That's who you're growing into. Not a boy who's been told no. A man who's got better things to do.

There's one more thing the trap never tells you. Whatever gives you the kick this year won't give it to you next year.
