# Crossing the Red Line {#crossing-the-red-line}

Whatever you repeat goes stale. The memory that worked last month doesn't work this month. The video that shocked you last year bores you now. The same meal keeps him quiet for less time. So you go looking for a bit more, or something new.

Porn is built on this. It never runs out of new, so a man can chase "a bit more" for hours, tab after tab, and finish emptier than he started. In *The Screwtape Letters*, C.S. Lewis has his senior devil, Screwtape, state the recipe: "An ever increasing craving for an ever diminishing pleasure is the formula."

So the fantasy creeps. First it's a daydream you barely notice. Then a memory. Then a woman you actually know: someone at work, a friend's wife, the neighbor across the street. Then a little help from the phone: a photo, a feed, a "harmless" video. Then porn, and then harder porn.

Every man has a red line.

- "I only use my imagination."
- "I'd never think about a real person."
- "I'd never watch that kind of thing."

![](images/illustrations/red-line-toes-on-the-line.png){.illustration width=80%}

Every man knows the tug of war at that line. His conscience pulls one way and the Little Monster the other. Paul the apostle was describing it in his letter to the Galatians when he wrote, "the flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh". They want opposite things, and you're the rope.

![](images/illustrations/red-line-tug-of-war.png){.illustration width=80%}

So you cross the line, or you go right up to it, and afterward you feel better than you did ten minutes earlier. Yet you're more restless and less at peace than the man who never started.

And the line moves. A man will jerk off to anything: a stranger's vacation photos, a swimsuit ad, the woman on a box of hair dye.

We do it when we're sick. We do it when we're so tired we can't see straight, when we're sad and when we aren't remotely in the mood. Married men do it an hour after making love to their wives. Does that sound like enjoyment? It sounds like a man feeding something.

The Little Monster wants feeding, and you pick the times. They come in two pairs of opposites:

- **Boredom and concentration.**
- **Stress and relaxation.**

What kind of medicine wakes you up and puts you to sleep, calms you down and gives you a lift? There's no such medicine.

![](images/illustrations/red-line-no-such-medicine.png){.illustration width=80%} The habit does none of those things. It ends an itch it created, and then you carry on with whatever you were doing.

Thinking of retreating to a "milder" version? Imagination only, weekends only, never with a screen? Everything in this book applies to all of it. Same loop, same monster.

So you're hooked. Is that bad news?
