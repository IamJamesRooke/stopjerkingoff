# Crossing the Red Line {#crossing-the-red-line}

Whatever you repeat goes stale. The memory that worked last month doesn't work this month. The video that shocked you last year bores you now. The same meal quiets him for a shorter time. So you go looking for a bit more, or something new.

Porn is built on this. It never runs out of new, so a man can chase "a bit more" for hours, tab after tab, and finish emptier than he started. Lewis has Screwtape, his senior devil, state the recipe: "An ever increasing craving for an ever diminishing pleasure is the formula."

So the fantasy creeps. First it's nothing much. Then a memory. Then a woman you actually know: someone at work, a friend's wife, the woman at the gym. Then a little help from the phone: a photo, a feed, a "harmless" video. Then porn, and then harder porn.

Every man has a red line. "I only use my imagination." "I'd never think about a real person." "I'd never watch that kind of thing."

Every man knows the tug of war that goes on at that line. His conscience pulls one way and the Little Monster the other. It is what Paul was describing when he wrote, "the flesh lusts against the Spirit, and the Spirit against the flesh". They want opposite things, and you're the rope.

So you cross the line, or you go right up to it, and afterward you feel better than you did ten minutes earlier. But you're more restless and less at peace than the man who never started.

And the line moves. A man will jerk off to anything: a stranger's vacation photos, a swimsuit ad, the woman on a box of hair dye.

We do it when we're sick. We do it when we're exhausted, when we're sad and when we aren't remotely in the mood. Married men do it an hour after making love to their wives. Does that sound like enjoyment to you? It sounds like a man feeding something.

The Little Monster wants feeding, and you pick the times. They come in two pairs of opposites:

- **Boredom and concentration.**
- **Stress and relaxation.**

What kind of medicine wakes you up and puts you to sleep, calms you down and gives you a lift? There's no such medicine. The habit does none of those things. It ends an itch that it created, and then you carry on with whatever you were doing.

Are you thinking of retreating to a "milder" version? Imagination only, weekends only, never with a screen? Everything in this book applies to all of it. It's the same loop and the same monster.

So you are hooked. Is that bad news?
