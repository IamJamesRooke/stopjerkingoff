# Where the Line Is {#where-the-line-is}

> "Drink water out of your own cistern, running water out of your own well."
>
> — Proverbs 5:15

Start with what is not on the list.

Noticing a beautiful woman is not a head. Wanting a wife is not a head. Wanting your own wife certainly isn't. Desire was God's idea. He made it strong on purpose, strong enough to pull a man out of his father's house and into a promise.

Lust is desire with the promise taken out. It wants the woman without the vow, and the pleasure without the person.

So where is the line? The Bible draws it in one place, and it hasn't moved in three thousand years. Sex belongs inside marriage. The book of Proverbs says it the way a father would say it to his son: "Drink water out of your own cistern, running water out of your own well." Inside a marriage, sex is a gift, and what a husband and wife enjoy with each other is on the right side of the line. The screen, the hand, the private film of another woman, the chase and the bed that isn't yours are all on the other side.

If you're not a believer, here is the same line in plainer clothes. Ask one question of anything you do: is this a gift, given inside a promise I have already made? Or is it a hit I'm taking?

A gift has a person at the other end and a promise underneath it. A hit has only you.

## Who decides? {-}

You may be thinking that a grown man ought to draw his own line. It's a fair thought, and it deserves a straight answer.

Ask yourself when you last redrew it. It wasn't on a calm Sunday morning with a cup of coffee. It was at eleven at night with the phone in your hand. The line that gets drawn at that hour has an author, and it isn't you.

Nobody lets the dog write the grocery list.

That is why the line has to sit somewhere outside you, where your wanting can't reach over and nudge it. You have moved your own line a hundred times. Each time it moved in the same direction.

## The right woman and no promise yet {-}

The hardest case is the man who has found the right woman. He loves her. He means to marry her. So when she sends him a photograph meant for his eyes alone, or when they're alone on a Friday night, he asks a reasonable question. How can this be lust? This is the woman I'm going to spend my life with.

He is closer to the right side than he has ever been. He has the right woman. What he doesn't have yet is the promise. "Someday" isn't one. A promise has a date on it, and witnesses.

There is good news for this man. He is the only one in this book who can move the line himself, and it takes about twenty minutes and a ring.

Until then, waiting is the kindest thing he can do for her. He will be bringing her a man who can wait. Every wife needs one of those.

## Not a verdict {-}

If you have spent years on the wrong side of the line, so has nearly every man who ever lived. That includes the one writing this. This chapter isn't a verdict on you. It's a map, and you can't leave a place until you know where its edge is.

And don't try to drag yourself across the line tonight by force. That is the sword again. For now it's enough to know where the line is. Keep reading.

So this book is for one kind of man, and he comes in every kind. Single or married, believer or not, with one head biting or all five: he is any man whose desire has been making his decisions for him.

You know the heads now, and you know the line. That leaves the question you have asked yourself a hundred times. You aren't stupid. You have wanted out for years. So why are you still in?
