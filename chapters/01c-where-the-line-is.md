# Where the Line Is {#where-the-line-is}

You have followed him through every room but one. Look, think, touch, chase, take. Those are the five ways he has been fed, with your eyes, your mind, your hand, your feet and your bed. In every room the same small test did the work. Is this a gift, or a hit I'm taking?

Before the last room, that test deserves a proper look, because the last room is where men argue with it.

Start with what the test does not condemn.

Noticing a beautiful woman is not lust. Wanting a wife is not lust. Wanting your own wife certainly isn't. Desire was God's idea. He made it strong on purpose, strong enough to pull a man out of his father's house and into a promise.

Lust is desire with the promise taken out. It wants the woman without the vow, and the pleasure without the person.

So where is the line? Put the test in full. Is this a gift, given inside a promise I have already made? Or is it a hit I'm taking?

A gift has a person at the other end and a promise underneath it. A hit has only you.

Run the five through it once more. The screen is a hit. So is the hand, and so is the private film. The chase is a man working a woman for what he can get, which is the opposite of a gift.

That leaves the bed, and the test is hardest there, because she is in it too and she may want it as much as he does. So look at what sex is. It ties two people together. It can make a child. It is the most one body can say to another: all of me, to you. With no promise under it, his body says that and he doesn't mean it. That is why it so often leaves a flat feeling behind, even when both of them were willing.

The Bible drew the line in the same place three thousand years ago. Sex belongs inside marriage. The book of Proverbs says it the way a father would say it to his son: "Drink water out of your own cistern, running water out of your own well." Inside a marriage, sex is a gift, and a husband and wife who give themselves to each other are on the right side of the line. The screen, the hand, the private film of another woman, the chase and any bed outside marriage are on the other side.

You may not accept that yet, and you don't have to accept it to keep reading. Just keep the test in your pocket, and try it on your own life as we go.

## Who decides? {-}

You may be thinking that a grown man ought to draw his own line. It's a fair thought, and it deserves a straight answer.

Ask yourself when you last redrew it. It wasn't on a calm Sunday morning with a cup of coffee. It was at eleven at night with the phone in your hand. The line that gets drawn at that hour has an author, and it isn't you.

Nobody should write his grocery list while he's starving.

That is why the line has to sit somewhere outside you, where your wanting can't reach over and nudge it. You have moved your own line a hundred times. Each time it moved in the same direction.

## Not a verdict {-}

If you have spent years on the wrong side of the line, so has nearly every man who ever lived. That includes the one writing this. This chapter isn't a verdict on you. It's a map, and you can't leave a place until you know where its edge is.

And don't try to drag yourself across the line tonight by force. That is sweeping the house again. For now it's enough to know where the line is. The one exception is another person. If someone else is in it, you are making a choice about her, and you can stop making it today.

So that is the line. One room is left, and it is the hardest in the house. The man in it isn't using a stranger. He has found the right woman, and he means to keep her.
