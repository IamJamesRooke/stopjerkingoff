# What Your Body Will Do {#what-your-body-will-do}

> "I will give thanks to you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made."
>
> — Psalm 139:14

A man who stops is often caught off guard by his own body. It goes on being a man's body. Some of what it does will look, to a nervous man, like a sign that something has gone wrong. Nothing has. Here is what to expect.

## Four things that are not a slip {-}

**Ordinary desire.** You're a man, and that isn't going to stop. You'll still notice a beautiful woman, and your body will still stir. What goes is the *need*, the nagging sense that something must be done about it. One man, seventy-six days in, described what's left: "I get urges. But wave them away like annoying flies."

**Morning erections.** You'll wake up hard, as you did before. This isn't a temptation or a sign of anything. It's plumbing. Don't test it to check that your body works. Leave it to nature. Doctors say a morning erection can occur at any age, and that it is tied to the cycles of sleep. An erection is not an instruction. Get up and it goes.

**Wet dreams.** Sooner or later you'll probably have one. Doctors describe them as "orgasms during sleep that happen without conscious control". Sin needs a decision, and you weren't there to make one. It is not a sin, and it is not a slip. You have nothing to confess and nothing to reset. Change the sheets and carry on. Don't lie awake in the morning replaying the dream either. That part you *are* awake for.

**A flat spell.** Some men report a week or two when desire seems to switch off altogether. The forums call it the flatline. For a few it runs longer, and it can be alarming. One medical student wrote that his body "literally 'died' on me. I lost my libido completely and felt depressed." It passed, and a few weeks later he was better than he had ever been. Another man, who went through a long one, reported from the far end: "the other side is beautiful". If it happens, leave it alone. The one mistake is to "check that everything still works". That isn't a check. It's the habit with an excuse. A psychiatrist who treats these men tells them the flat feeling is generally temporary.

## When to see a doctor {-}

One caution, and it matters. If low desire or erection trouble lasts more than a few weeks, see a doctor. It can be an early warning about your heart or your blood sugar, especially after forty. And if you feel low most of the day for more than two weeks, or lose interest in things you used to enjoy, that is not the Little Monster. See a doctor about that too. It is very treatable. You don't have to feel glad for this book to work.

## If your body doesn't work with your wife {-}

Some men find, after years of the habit, that their body doesn't answer properly when they are with their wife. How long until it does? The honest answer is that nobody can give you a date. One medical student who wrote about it was well in a little over a month. One recovery coach went through it himself. He says it was about two and a half months before things worked at all, and another six of gradual progress after that. A few men report longer. A sex therapist who has written a great deal about it asks his patients to pause for a time and then retrains them. He reminds them, again and again, that the restraint is "only temporary". If you are under a therapist's care for this, follow the treatment.

So be patient with your body, and tell your wife what is going on, because the worst part for her is thinking it's her fault. See a doctor too, since medicines and other conditions can cause the same trouble. And don't "test" things on your own in the meantime. You know what that is.

## Try it and see {-}

The forums call going without "semen retention", and they promise a great deal for it. Men who stop do say they feel better: more energy, more confidence, more focus. Those are reports, and I won't dress them up as proof. Your body doesn't soak semen back up as some kind of fuel. Nobody has shown that holding it raises your testosterone. And a wet dream doesn't "lose" you anything.

Why would men feel better, then? A man who has stopped has his evenings back. He sleeps, and he isn't carrying a secret. He has kept a promise to himself for the first time in years. That would put a spring in anybody's step. So don't take the forums' word for it, or mine. Try it and see. If the energy comes, enjoy it as a bonus. You didn't stop for the energy. You stopped to be free.

One last word. If you're married, none of this means holding back from your wife. She isn't the habit.
