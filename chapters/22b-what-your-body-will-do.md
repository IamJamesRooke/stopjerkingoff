# What Your Body Will Do {#what-your-body-will-do}

> "I will give thanks to you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made."
>
> — Psalm 139:14

A man who stops is often caught off guard by his own body. It goes on being a man's body. Some of what it does will look, to a nervous man, like a sign that something has gone wrong. Nothing has. Here is what to expect.

## Four things that are not a slip {-}

**Ordinary desire.** You're a man, and that isn't going to stop. You'll still notice a beautiful woman, and your body will still stir. What goes is the *need*, the nagging sense that something must be done about it. One man, seventy-six days in, described what's left: "I get urges. But wave them away like annoying flies."

**Morning erections.** You'll wake up hard, as you did before. This isn't a temptation or a sign of anything. It's plumbing. Don't test it to check that your body works. Leave it to nature. Doctors say a morning erection can occur at any age, and that it is tied to the cycles of sleep. An erection is not an instruction. Get up and it goes.

**Wet dreams.** Sooner or later you'll probably have one. Doctors describe them as "orgasms during sleep that happen without conscious control". Sin needs a decision, and you weren't there to make one. It is not a sin, and it is not a slip. You have nothing to confess and nothing to reset. Change the sheets and carry on. Don't lie awake in the morning replaying the dream either. That part you *are* awake for.

**A flat spell.** Some men find that desire switches off altogether for a while. The forums call it the flatline. It is not a slip, and it is not damage. But there is a trap inside it, and the trap has sent a great many men back. So it gets a warning of its own.

## The trap in the flat spell {-}

It goes like this. A man stops, and for the first days his desire is loud. Then, somewhere in the first couple of months, it goes quiet. He stops waking up hard. A beautiful woman walks past and nothing stirs, and he feels unplugged below the waist. One medical student wrote that his body "literally 'died' on me. I lost my libido completely and felt depressed."

Nobody warned him, so he draws the obvious conclusion. Stopping has broken him.

There's a man you may recognize. He had gone weeks without it, his longest run in years. Then he noticed that nothing was happening. Day after day, nothing. He got scared. So one night he decided to check, just to be sure that everything still worked. It worked. And that was the end of his freedom for a long time, because he came away sure of one thing: stopping is dangerous. It was false, and it kept him in the trap.

Look at who gains. For years the Little Monster told you that you wanted it too much to stop. Now he tells you that you don't want it enough, and that you had better do something about it. His complaint has turned right around. His cure hasn't changed at all.

The check can't even tell you anything. If your body answers, he says, "Good. And now that you've started." If it doesn't, you panic and go looking for something stronger. He wins either way. That isn't a check. It's the habit with an excuse.

So what is really happening? No study has measured the flat spell, so all I can give you is what thousands of men report and the likeliest reason for it. For years your body was flooded with more stimulation than any man was built for. It coped by turning its own sensitivity down. Take the flood away, and for a while ordinary life isn't loud enough to register. Then the dial comes back up.

The flat spell is not damage, then. It is repair. A man in bed with the flu doesn't run a marathon to prove his legs still work. He rests, and he gets well.

How long does it last? Most men who get one say a week or two. For a few it runs much longer. One man, who went through a long one, reported from the far end: "the other side is beautiful". Some men never get one at all. There is no timetable, so don't measure yourself against one. A psychiatrist who treats these men tells them the flat feeling is generally temporary.

See the flat spell for what it is. The caller has stopped calling, and you are worried about the silence. He has no desire left to use on you, so he uses fear. Enjoy the quiet. It is the easiest stretch you will get.

So don't test. Not with a screen, not with your hand, not "just to check". A man who goes back only has to walk through the same quiet stretch again the next time he stops. Leave your body alone and it will announce its own return, often on some ordinary morning, with no help from you.

## When to see a doctor {-}

One caution, and it matters. If low desire or erection trouble lasts more than a few weeks, or simply worries you, see a doctor. That is a real check. The Little Monster's kind is not. Trouble that lasts can be an early warning about your heart or your blood sugar, especially after forty. And if you feel low most of the day for more than two weeks, or lose interest in things you used to enjoy, that is not the Little Monster. See a doctor about that too. It is very treatable. You don't have to feel glad for this book to work.

## If your body doesn't work with your wife {-}

Some men find, after years of the habit, that their body doesn't answer properly when they are with their wife. How long until it does? The honest answer is that nobody can give you a date. One medical student who wrote about it was well in a little over a month. One recovery coach went through it himself. He says it was about two and a half months before things worked at all, and another six of gradual progress after that. A few men report longer. A sex therapist who has written a great deal about it asks his patients to pause for a time and then retrains them. He reminds them, again and again, that the restraint is "only temporary". If you are under a therapist's care for this, follow the treatment.

So be patient with your body, and tell your wife what is going on, because the worst part for her is thinking it's her fault. See a doctor too, since medicines and other conditions can cause the same trouble. And don't "test" things on your own in the meantime. You know what that is.

## Try it and see {-}

The forums call going without "semen retention", and they promise a great deal for it. Men who stop do say they feel better: more energy, more confidence, more focus. Those are reports, and I won't dress them up as proof. Your body doesn't soak semen back up as some kind of fuel. Nobody has shown that holding it raises your testosterone. And a wet dream doesn't "lose" you anything.

Why would men feel better, then? A man who has stopped has his evenings back. He sleeps, and he isn't carrying a secret. He has kept a promise to himself for the first time in years. That would put a spring in anybody's step. So don't take the forums' word for it, or mine. Try it and see. If the energy comes, enjoy it as a bonus. You didn't stop for the energy. You stopped to be free.

One last word. If you're married, none of this means holding back from your wife. She isn't the habit.

Your body will look after itself. What undoes most men is something much smaller.
