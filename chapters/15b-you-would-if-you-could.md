# You Would If You Could {#you-would-if-you-could}

This room has no screen in it. The film plays in a man's own head.

Start with the man who has a wife or a girlfriend. He's never touched another woman. He only thinks about them.

He says it like this. "I'm jerking off, but I'm only thinking about other women. I'm not doing anything about it."

He's doing something about it. He's doing the only part he can reach.

Notice when "it isn't cheating" sounds true. Only while he's worked up. Catch him ten minutes later, with his pulse back to normal, and nobody has to explain it to him. A calm man knows what he was just doing behind her back. The lie only works on a man with his blood up.

What's the fantasy? A rehearsal. In his head he's already said yes. Let her walk into the room and make the offer for real. In the fantasy, he has never once said no.

So what's keeping him faithful? Not his heart. Only that she isn't in the room.

Chris Rock, the American comedian, has a hard joke about this. A man, he says, is only as faithful as his options. Big laugh, because every man in the audience knows exactly what he means.

Is that the man you want to be?

**Faithful only because nobody offered.**

That's what Jesus meant by adultery in the heart. He wasn't exaggerating. He was describing a man who would if he could.

Some men will say, "That's not me. I could, and I don't." Look at why you don't.

Take a man with a girlfriend, and it's getting serious.

One Friday he's out with friends, and a woman he half knows starts talking to him. She laughs at everything he says. She touches his arm. She's not really his type. But she's pretty enough for tonight, and he can tell that if he made a move, she'd say yes.

He doesn't make one. He walks home with his chest out. "See? I'm a faithful man."

But he knows why he left, and it wasn't his girlfriend. It was the work. The drinks to buy, the talk to keep going for another hour, then the morning, and her messages, and the lies to keep straight. He never wanted her. He wanted to use her, and using her was too much trouble.

So at home he takes the cheap way. He lies down in the dark and thinks of her face. "Nobody's hurt", he tells himself. "She'll never know."

The next evening his girlfriend is cooking dinner. She looks up, smiles, and asks how his night was. "Quiet", he says.

Then a question lands in him that he can't put down. How can she trust a man like this?

She can't. He hasn't touched anyone. But all that stood between her and a betrayal was the bother. He sees it now, and he doesn't like the man he sees.

She deserves a man who wouldn't, even if he could. Not a man whose loyalty depends on his luck.

That man isn't gritting his teeth. He's stopped wanting it, so there's nothing left to offer him.

The single man thinks none of this touches him. He has nobody to betray. But look at what he's practicing. Every night he takes a woman who has no say, no needs and no name tomorrow. If he wants a wife one day, he's training to be a man she can't trust. If he never marries, he still has to live in that head. And the film is part of why he has nobody. A woman made of memory never says no, so real ones start to feel like work.

Try one test. The woman from work is in his film tonight. Could he describe it to her at lunch tomorrow? If the film were harmless, he could.

Same question as the other rooms. What has the film ever given him, except the itch for the next showing?

## Filling the gap {-}

The married man has one more line. At least he's never cheated. He believes he's owed a release every day or two, and his wife isn't always of the same mind. She's tired, or pregnant, or away, or they've had a fight. So he "fills the gap", and the film in his head plays while he does.

Her tiredness is no excuse, and the habit doesn't stay inside the gap anyway. Soon it happens on nights when she'd have said yes, because it's quicker, because it asks nothing, because nobody has to be won over. In time he's half glad when she turns over.

And when she does reach for him, he's already spent, or his mind is on a reel she isn't in. He gave the best of it to nobody, and she gets the leftovers. One husband wrote, after he finally saw it:

> "I was beside myself over how I had grieved God and my wife"

Whatever you believe about marriage, you promised her this part of yourself. A man who takes it back in secret, a little at a time, is keeping something from her. Some part of her knows, even if she couldn't say what's wrong.

## The harem in your head {-}

No filter blocks a memory. That's why some men think the film in the head is the safe option. "I don't look at anything. It's all in my head."

It's the same smoke. The film in your head is the cigarette you roll yourself. Porn is the factory kind, built to hook you faster. Neither one is the safe option.

Everybody starts there, and the scenes don't stay tame. Soon the mind wants a real face: a coworker, a friend's wife, the girl who serves his coffee. Then it wants "just a photo, to get started".

This man is proud that no one else's sin is on his screen. But he's the director, the casting agent and the whole audience of his own private movie theater, and the women in it never agreed to appear.

Even the husband who only ever pictures his wife isn't safe. He's still teaching his body that the finish line is himself, alone, on his own schedule. The version of her in his head never says "not like that" and never needs anything.

In a private letter in 1956, C.S. Lewis wrote about a harem of imaginary brides, and he saw what it does to a man. The imaginary women are always available. They never refuse, and they never ask a man to give anything up. Nobody's at the other end of any of them. So, Lewis wrote, "this harem, once admitted, works against his ever getting out and really uniting with a real woman." A real woman has moods, needs and a mind of her own. The harem has none, so the real woman feels like hard work.

The 2020s gave the harem a voice. A man can now talk every night to a girlfriend made by a computer. She's always awake and always pleased with him. She never has a bad day and never says no. You may never have tried it, but it shows where the film in a man's head is headed. In one American survey in 2025, nearly a third of young adult men said they had chatted with a machine as a boyfriend or girlfriend. Of everyone in the survey who had tried it, about one in five said they liked it better than talking to a real person. Now the harem talks back.

Lewis warned where it ends: "The danger is that of coming to love the prison."

That's the thought life. Each visit, the cell gets a little more comfortable.

## Taking thoughts captive {-}

So what does a man do with a head like that? Paul, writing to the church at Corinth, gives one line: "bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ". The picture is a soldier's. A thought turns up at the gate. It doesn't wander where it likes. It's seen for what it is, and it goes no further.

That doesn't mean "try not to think about it". You've tried that. It turns the thought into the biggest thing in the room. An old saying does better. You can't stop a bird flying over your head, but you can stop it building a nest in your hair.

![](images/illustrations/would-bird-not-nest.png){.illustration width=80%}

The bird is the first thought. It comes by itself, and it's no sin and no failure. The nest is what a man builds out of it afterward.

Watch a nest go up. A man's stopped at a red light, and a woman crosses in front of his car. He thinks, "She's attractive." That's the bird, and so far nothing has happened. Then he plays it forward in his head. Then he tells himself, "Nobody's hurt. It's only a thought." And tonight, alone, the choice feels sudden. It isn't. He made most of it at the red light, when he let the bird stay.

So act at the start, where it costs almost nothing. One man who had broken the habit wrote, "The easiest thought for me to say, 'No' to, is the first one. And if I don't entertain the thought, I'm not going to do it." He isn't fighting the thought. He just doesn't invite it in.

That's the whole instruction. Let the bird fly over. Don't build it a nest.

It's easier than you fear. A man builds the nest because he believes there's something in it for him. Once he sees there isn't, the bird finds nowhere to land and flies on.

That's the women who aren't there. The next excuse is about the ones who are. "Fine. But I can't walk around with my eyes shut."
