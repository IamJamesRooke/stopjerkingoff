# Who Needs to Know {#who-needs-to-know}

> "Confess your offenses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed."
>
> — James 5:16

Should you tell your wife? Your pastor? A friend? Secrecy is this habit's power, so the question deserves a straight answer. You've already told God, and that matters most. If you're Catholic, that means Confession too. As for people, here's what I'd do.

## Tell one man {-}

Tell one man. He might be a pastor or a priest, an elder, or an older friend with some sense. If you have nobody like that, a counselor or a doctor counts, and so does a helpline. If you're under eighteen, tell a parent, a school counselor or a doctor. The apostle James tells believers, "Confess your offenses to one another, and pray for one another, that you may be healed." There's a reason. A thing that's been said out loud to another man loses half its size.

But be clear what you're asking him for. You're not hiring a policeman. A weekly "did you or didn't you" is the Willpower Method with a witness, and it keeps the subject at the center of your life. Tell him as a free man giving news: "This had me for years. It's over. I wanted one person to know." Then let him ask whenever he likes.

## Your wife {-}

A marriage is better with no locked rooms in it, so yes. Tell her, and don't leave it for years. She may already know, or half know.

Be clear what this is to her. To you it has felt like a private habit. To her it may be a betrayal, and she is entitled to call it that. So tell her for her sake. If you only want to unload your guilt so that *you* feel lighter, you're using her again.

Tell her once, and tell her the truth: what it was, for how long, and whether there was porn or another woman. Don't bring it to her as good news. Say something like, "I've hidden this from you for years. I've stopped, and I'll tell you if that ever changes." Don't make her drag it out of you in pieces over six months. Half a confession followed by a second one does more harm than the first. Spare her the details unless she asks, and answer what she asks.

Then let her set the pace. She may be angry, or cold, or want to see your phone, or need time. None of that is a failure to forgive. Trust comes back slowly, and you don't get to say when. Don't ask her to keep score for you. But if she asks to look, show her.

Ask her what she needs, and mean it. If she wants a counselor, a pastor or a friend of her own to talk to, help her get one. If there was an affair, anything against the law, or anything that puts her health at risk, talk to a pastor or counselor first about how to tell her well. Do it soon. If there was another person or paid sex, both of you should see a doctor and be tested before you are intimate again.

She doesn't owe you praise for stopping. You owe her a husband who is all there.

## Nobody else {-}

Nobody else needs to know. Don't post "Day 1" anywhere. You don't need an audience.
