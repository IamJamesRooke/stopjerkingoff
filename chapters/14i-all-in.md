# All In {#all-in}

So far this walk has only shut doors. This one opens.

You may have read the last few chapters as a list of things taken away. Look at what's on offer.

## Forty first chapters {-}

A man who's slept with forty women thinks he knows a great deal about women. He's read the first chapter of forty books.

First chapters are all alike. The nerves, the pressed shirt, the performance. He's never read a chapter ten. He doesn't know what a woman is like when she trusts him, because none of them had a reason to.

A man who's been married to one woman for thirty years has read the whole book. He's in it.

![](images/illustrations/all-in-thirty-years.png){.illustration width=80%}

New is the cheapest thing in the world. The screen taught you that. The forty-first tab was no better than the first, and a new woman is a new tab. Being known is the one thing you can't get new. It takes years. You can't buy it, match it or download it.

## What the bet buys {-}

The bet buys someone who knows the worst of you and stays.

It buys a bed with no performance in it, no clock running, and nobody creeping out before morning.

It buys a witness to your whole life, and a reason to climb out of bed that isn't you.

And it gives your desire somewhere to go. Everywhere else it went, it was spent. Here it lays another brick each time.

## Two men who said so {-}

Hugh Grant, the British actor, stayed a bachelor into his fifties. In 2015 he told a magazine, "I'm not really a believer in marriage." He married in 2018, at fifty-seven. A radio host asked him about it the year after. "Well, I was just plain wrong," he said. "It was another thing I put off too many decades."

Don't hear a plan in that. Hear a man counting decades he can't get back.

Jeff Bridges, the American actor, has been married to the same woman since 1977. "What they don't tell you about marriage is that it just keeps getting better on all levels," he told an interviewer. He named three of them: "emotion, sex, intimacy."

The surveys agree with them, as far as a survey can. In a large American survey in 2022, married men were about twice as likely as unmarried men to say they were very happy. Married people also have sex more often than single people, on average. An average is no promise. Some marriages go cold, and no vow guarantees a man a willing wife. So talk about it before the wedding, and ask what each of you would do.

Be fair to that too. Happier men are more likely to marry, so the ring isn't the whole cause. And it holds only for a good marriage. A Harvard study followed 724 men from 1938 into old age. The men happiest in their relationships at fifty were the healthiest at eighty. The marriages full of conflict were very bad for the men in them.

## The bet {-}

Marriage is an all-in bet, and nobody should tell you otherwise. You shove everything to the middle of the table. Your money, your name, your future. You can't take it back without losing half of yourself.

![](images/illustrations/all-in-the-bet.png){.illustration width=80%}

It can go wrong, and you've seen the numbers. Men have been ruined by a bad marriage, and so have women. Some wives lie, and some walk out, just as some husbands do.

That's no argument against the bet. It's an argument for choosing with clear eyes. And the habit never let you choose that way. A man ruled by lust picks a wife with the one part of him that can't read character.

## How a free man chooses {-}

A free man has two advantages. He isn't hungry, so he can walk away from the wrong woman. And he isn't in a hurry, so he can watch.

Watch for these. Does she tell the truth when it costs her? How does she speak about people who can do nothing for her? Does she want the same life you want, in children, in faith, in money, and in where to live? Can she say sorry? Can you? Do you like her at breakfast, hair flat, eggs burnt, nothing to gain?

She's choosing too. Her risks aren't the same as yours, and they're real. So be a man that such a woman would bet on.

Nobody can promise you a wife. But a man who keeps his word, who wants one woman, and who isn't looking over her shoulder is rare.

One man in the hall has heard all of this and shaken his head. He doesn't expect a wife at all.
