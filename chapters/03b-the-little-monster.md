# The Little Monster {#the-little-monster}

A cue comes, and with it a small, restless urge. We'll call that urge the Little Monster.

The first thing to know about him is how small he is. The urge involves no pain at all. It's a faint, empty, fidgety sense that something is missing. That's why men mistake it for sex drive, or for hunger, or for plain boredom. Left alone for a while it can turn into edginess and a short temper. It's a hunger, but a hunger for something that doesn't feed you.

In the first months you don't notice him at all. The itch and its relief are so slight that you'd laugh at the idea you were hooked. Later, when you're doing it most days, you tell yourself you've come to enjoy it, or that you've "got into the habit". By then the truth is that you have a tenant. The Little Monster has moved in, he pays no rent, and every so often you go around the loop to feed him.

One man in the trap put the entire thing in seven words:

> "I hate it, but I continually feed it."

That is the only reason any man carries on, whether he does it three times a day or twice a month. He's feeding the Little Monster.

And the Little Monster isn't picky. A video feeds him. A fantasy feeds him. A long look at a stranger feeds him, and so does a flirty message from a woman you have no business messaging. It's all one diet. He has five mouths and one small belly.

Here is the cruelest part. The "pleasure" a man gets from it is mostly the pleasure of getting back to how he'd have felt anyway. He's trying to return to the peace and ease he had as a boy, before any of this started. And he keeps using the very thing that took it from him.

## The annoying alarm {-}

Have you ever had a car alarm going in the street for half the afternoon? You stop hearing it after a while, but your shoulders don't. Then somebody finally shuts it off, and the quiet is wonderful.

But nothing wonderful has arrived. An irritation has ended.

That's what the relief is. Before the habit, you were complete. You had no alarm. Each time you go around the loop you feel better for a moment, and you also set the alarm to go off again.

All you know is that there's an itch, you do the thing, and the itch goes. But it doesn't stay gone. The relief you just bought is what sets up the next urge. Men on the recovery forums have a name for this: the chaser.

It works like salt water. The more you drink, the thirstier you get.

It's the oldest trick there is. So why can't men see it? There are three reasons.

1. **You've been told since boyhood that it's natural and harmless.** There's a grain of truth in that, which is what makes it stick.
2. **The itch doesn't hurt.** It feels exactly like ordinary restlessness, tiredness or stress, and those are the very moments you reach for it. So you think of the feeling as normal life, and never as something the habit put there.
3. **It works back to front.** This is the big one. You suffer the empty feeling when you're *not* doing it, so you never blame the last time. And the moment you start, the feeling eases, so the habit gets the credit. The thing that causes the itch gets thanked for scratching it.

Men say masturbation is "satisfying". But nobody can be satisfied who wasn't dissatisfied to begin with. The free man lies down at night and goes to sleep. He isn't satisfied, because he was never unsatisfied. You can't rest until the Little Monster has been fed.

## A pleasure or a crutch? {-}

Let me repeat the main point, because everything turns on it. Men find it hard to stop because they believe they'd be giving up a real pleasure or a real support. You need to see that you're giving up nothing at all.

The best way to see it is to compare the habit with eating, because it has disguised itself as eating.

Eat at regular times and you hardly notice hunger. Push a meal back and you feel it: no pain, just an empty, slightly edgy feeling. Then you eat, and satisfying that hunger is one of life's real pleasures.

The habit looks just the same. There's an empty feeling, you act, and you get relief. That resemblance is what fools us. "It's an appetite, like any other. A man has to eat."

In fact the two are opposites.

- **Food feeds you. This drains you.** You get up from a meal with more than you sat down with. You get up from this with less.
- **Food is good, and stays good.** You'll enjoy Sunday dinner at eighty. Whatever this once was, it stopped being a treat years ago. It's a chore you do with one eye on the door.
- **Eating doesn't create hunger. It ends it.** The habit creates its own itch. Far from ending it, each time guarantees the next.

"But isn't sex a God-given appetite too?" It is. And that is the point. Sex with your wife is the meal. This is staring at the photograph on the menu. It was never what the hunger was for. Sex is the one appetite a man was given in order to give it away. The habit turns it into something he keeps entirely for himself. A husband who'd carried the habit into his marriage came to see it plainly:

> "I've come to realize that masturbation, any way you slice it, is a selfish act"

Notice, too, where the excitement actually is. It's in the lead-up: the door clicking shut, the scroll, the search for the right video, the building of the scene. The end itself lasts seconds, and the flatness lands straight after. That's why so many men drag it out. A man will spend forty minutes choosing the video for an ending that takes six seconds. He has picked jobs with less care.

And the lead-up has a problem. It never stays the same size.
