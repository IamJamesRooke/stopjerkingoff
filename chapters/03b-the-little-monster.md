# The Little Monster {#the-little-monster}

Meet him properly, then. When a cue comes, he's the small, restless urge, banging on the floor.

The first thing to know about him is how small he is. The urge doesn't hurt. It only feels huge because of the fear the lies pile on top of it. On its own it's a faint, empty, fidgety sense that something's missing. That's why men mistake it for sex drive, or hunger, or plain boredom. Left alone for a while, it turns into a short temper. It's a hunger for something that doesn't feed you.

In the first months you don't notice him. The itch and its relief are so slight you'd laugh at the idea you were hooked. Later, when you're doing it most days, you tell yourself you've come to enjoy it, or that you've "got into the habit". Really, the Little Monster has moved in. He pays no rent, and every so often you carry his dinner up the stairs.

He isn't picky. A video feeds him. A fantasy feeds him. A long look at a stranger on the train feeds him. It's all one diet, and it all goes to one small belly.

![](images/illustrations/little-monster-one-small-belly.png){.illustration width=80%}

One man in the trap put the entire thing in eight words:

> "I hate it, but I continually feed it."

That's the only reason any man carries on, three times a day or twice a month. He's feeding the Little Monster.

Most of what a man calls the pleasure is just getting back to how he'd have felt anyway. He's trying to return to the peace and ease he had as a boy, before any of this started, and he keeps using the very thing that took it from him.

## The annoying alarm {-}

Ever had a car alarm going in the street for half the afternoon? You stop hearing it after a while, but your shoulders don't. Then somebody finally shuts it off, and the quiet is wonderful.

![](images/illustrations/little-monster-car-alarm.png){.illustration width=80%}


But nothing wonderful has arrived. An irritation has ended.

That's the relief. Before the habit, you were complete. You had no alarm. Each time around the loop you feel better for a moment, and you also set the alarm to go off again.

The relief you just bought sets up the next urge. Men on the recovery forums call that the chaser.

So why can't men see it? Three reasons.

1. **You've been told since boyhood that it's natural and harmless.** There's a grain of truth in that, which is what makes it stick.
2. **The itch doesn't hurt.** It feels exactly like ordinary restlessness, tiredness or stress, and those are the very moments you reach for relief. So you take the feeling for normal life, never for something the habit put there.
3. **It works back to front.** This is the big one. You suffer the empty feeling when you're *not* doing it, so you never blame the last time. The moment you start, the feeling eases, so the habit gets the credit. The thing that causes the itch gets thanked for scratching it.

![](images/illustrations/little-monster-firefighter-with-a-match.png){.illustration width=80%}

Men say masturbation is "satisfying". But a man can't be satisfied unless he was dissatisfied first. The free man lies down at night and goes to sleep. He has nothing to satisfy. You can't rest until the Little Monster's been fed.

## A pleasure or a crutch? {-}

Everything turns on one point. Men find it hard to stop because they believe they'd be giving up a pleasure worth having or a real support. You're giving up nothing at all.

The best way to see that is to compare the habit with eating, because eating is its disguise.

Eat at regular times and you hardly notice hunger. Push a meal back and you feel it: no pain, just an empty, edgy feeling. Then you eat, and that's one of life's real pleasures.

The habit looks the same. There's an empty feeling, you act, and you get relief. That resemblance is what fools us. "It's an appetite, like any other. A man has to eat."

In fact the two are opposites.

- **Food feeds you. This drains you.** You get up from a meal with more than you sat down with. You get up from this with less.
- **Food is good, and stays good.** You'll still enjoy a good dinner at eighty. The ending still feels good for a few seconds. Everything around it became a chore years ago.
- **Eating doesn't create hunger. It ends it.** The habit creates its own itch. Far from ending it, each time guarantees the next.

"But isn't sex a God-given appetite too?" It is, and that's the point. Sex with your wife is the meal. If you're single, that meal is still ahead of you. The habit is licking the photo of the steak on the menu instead of eating. It was never what the hunger was for. Sex is the one appetite a man was given in order to give it away. The habit turns it into something he keeps for himself. A husband who'd carried the hand into his marriage came to see it plainly:

> "I've come to realize that masturbation, any way you slice it, is a selfish act"

![](images/illustrations/little-monster-licking-the-menu.png){.illustration width=80%}

One objection is still standing, and it's the honest one.

"But it feels good."
