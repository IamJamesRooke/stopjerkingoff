# Mud on Purpose {#mud-on-purpose}

> "Woe to those who call evil good, and good evil; who put darkness for light, and light for darkness"
>
> — Isaiah 5:20

Some dirt gets in by accident. You forget to wipe your feet. Some dirt gets carried in by a man who looks you in the eye, stamps his boots on your carpet and grins.

Here's a short list of boots. Every one is on the record.

## The boots on the carpet {-}

- In 2017, at the Grammys, the music industry's biggest awards night, the superstar Beyonce performs dressed as a goddess. Her crown and veil are styled like old paintings of the Virgin Mary. Christian leaders object.
- In 2018 the singer Ariana Grande releases "God Is a Woman," a video that mixes church imagery and sex. On an awards show that year she stages it like the famous painting of Jesus's last meal with his disciples. She tells her fans, "Sex is empowering."
- In 2021 the rapper Lil Nas X slides down a pole from heaven into hell in a music video and dances on Satan's lap. It gets thirty-eight million views in three days. Asked about the anger, he says people already demonized who he was, so "I'm gonna make the best of it." An art company sells 666 pairs of matching "Satan Shoes," each with a drop of human blood in the sole. They sell out in under a minute.
- In 2023, at the Grammys, Sam Smith and Kim Petras sing "Unholy." Smith wears a top hat with devil horns, in front of cages and flames.
- In 2023 the singer Sabrina Carpenter films a music video inside a Catholic church in Brooklyn. She dances in a short dress and a black veil, with pink coffins in the aisle. The bishop says he's "appalled." He takes away the pastor's power to run the parish, and holds a special Mass to restore the church. Carpenter answers with a joke on her own last name: "Jesus was a carpenter."
- In 2026 Netflix releases a show called *The Body*, set in a Catholic high school. Its main characters are teenage girls, about seventeen, played by adult actresses. It has explicit sex scenes between two of them, and a mock initiation ritual. Its creator calls it "a raunchy teen psychodrama with a dash of Catholic horror." A psychodrama is a story about people's minds coming apart. By late November nearly 170,000 people have signed a petition from a Christian activist group asking Netflix to pull it.
- In 2026 the crime video game *Grand Theft Auto VI*, one of the most awaited games ever made, comes out rated for "Strong Sexual Content," "Nudity" and sex scenes. South Korea's game board nearly refuses to rate it at all.

## Why the holy things? {-}

Notice what keeps showing up. Not just bodies. Altars. Crosses. The Virgin Mary. The Last Supper. Satan.

Why drag church into a pop song?

Because sex hardly shocks anyone anymore. The house is already too dirty. A stage that wants a gasp needs something people still care about, and there isn't much left. One thing still works. What's holy.

You don't have to believe in anything holy to see why. Even a man with no faith knows that people's churches, funerals and vows matter to them. Poke at those and you get a reaction. A shocked grandmother is free advertising. An angry bishop is a headline. Every outraged post pushes the video higher. A dirty stage once had to hide from church. Now it rents the church.

## "Do they really believe in Satan?" {-}

Most of them, probably not. Let's be fair about that, because this book has no right to guess what's in anyone's heart. It won't tell you these singers worship the devil. Nobody named here has said so.

Even the Satanists mostly don't believe in Satan. The Church of Satan, founded in San Francisco in 1966, says its members don't think he exists. For them he's a symbol of pride and indulging your animal desires. The Satanic Temple, founded in 2012, is the same. It uses Satan as a symbol of rebelling against rules, and it pulls stunts on purpose to get attention.

When the Grammys put on "Unholy," a leader of the Church of Satan watched it. His review? "Nothing particularly special."

Think about that. The devil horns didn't even impress the Satanists.

![](images/illustrations/mud-the-monster-at-the-awards.png){.illustration width=80%}

A few people on stage have said they dabble. In 2017 the singer Lana Del Rey said she'd joined a mass "binding" spell, a ritual meant to tie the hands of President Trump. Asked about it, she said, "Yeah, I did it. Why not?" And off the stage there are people who truly believe Satan is real, and worship him.

Here's the thing, though. Whether the man on stage believes or not, the message the business sells never changes. It's the oldest one there is:

- What you were taught is holy is a joke.
- The rules your grandmother lived by are a cage.
- Whatever your body wants, give it.

That's not a new idea from a record label. It's the oldest pitch in the Bible. In the Garden of Eden, the serpent told the first woman that God's one rule was a cage, and the fruit would make her free. You don't need to believe the story to see the point. The pitch is old. It's only wearing a better outfit.

Maybe you think the whole thing is nonsense, serpent and all. Fine. You don't need to believe a word of it for the next part to cost you.
