# The Bent Gift {#the-bent-gift}

> "Therefore a man will leave his father and his mother, and will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh."
>
> — Genesis 2:24

Every one of the seven deadly sins bends something good. Pride bends self-respect. Greed bends ambition. Lust bends the best gift of the lot.

Here's the gift. The Bible opens with the story of the first man and the first woman. It ends that story with a line about every wedding since: a man "will join with his wife, and they will be one flesh."

One flesh. That's what the drive is for.

It isn't only there to feel good. It's there to pull two people so close they make something new. A home. A table with too many chairs around it. Now and then a kid with her eyes and your nose.

![](images/illustrations/bent-gift-one-flesh.png){.illustration width=80%}

If you're not a believer, ask a man who's been happily married for forty years what the best part is. It won't be one night. It'll be this: somebody knows him all the way down, and wants him anyway.

That's the good thing. Now watch the flesh go to work on it.

## Desire and lust {-}

Lust is what happens when the flesh gets hold of desire.

Desire says, "She's lovely." Lust says, "I want that, for me, now."

Desire sees a person. Lust sees something to use.

The drive was built to point out, at one woman. Lust bends it until it points back in, at you.

Paul asked his readers, "don't you know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you". He isn't saying your body is dirty. He's saying it's too good for this.

If you're not a believer, think of your grandfather's watch. You'd never use it carelessly. Lust picks up the watch and hammers a nail with it.

![](images/illustrations/flesh-watch-as-a-hammer.png){.illustration width=80%}

Masturbation is the plainest case, so we'll use it to show how lust works. A drive built to bind a man to one woman ends in a wad of tissue. It's a hijacking, and the plane lands nowhere.

![](images/illustrations/flesh-the-hijacked-plane.png){.illustration width=80%}

Like every other bend, it hands you less of what it promised. The drive was after closeness. Lust leaves a man more alone than when he started. He's in the dark with a phone, a screen away from a woman who doesn't know he exists.

## Lust's daughters {-}

Gregory, the pope who trimmed the list to seven, said each sin has daughters. They're the smaller sins it gives birth to. He named eight daughters of lust, and Aquinas kept all eight.

Here they are, with what they look like on a weeknight.

- **Blindness of mind.** Smart men get stupid. A man who runs a whole business makes choices at one in the morning he'd fire an employee for.
- **Rashness.** He acts before he thinks. The tab is open before he's decided anything.
- **Thoughtlessness.** He stops weighing the cost. Tomorrow's meeting, his wife's trust, his own word. None of it gets a vote.
- **Inconstancy.** He can't keep a promise. He swore it off an hour ago.
- **Self-love.** Everything bends back toward him. People turn into things that either please him or get in his way.
- **Hatred of God.** He starts to resent whoever says no, and God says no first. A man hates the rule he keeps breaking, because it keeps catching him.
- **Love of this world.** He clutches tonight and stops caring about next year.
- **Despair of the world to come.** He stops believing anything better is coming. That includes the belief that he could ever be free.

Gregory wrote that list fourteen hundred years ago, long before anybody owned a phone. He could have been describing a Tuesday night.

The last daughter is the cruelest, and she's a liar. A man can walk out of this. Plenty already have, and they didn't do it by being tougher than you.

They did it by seeing how the thing works. It's simpler than you'd think.

## How the loop works {-}

You'll find plenty of videos that explain this habit with brain chemistry and diagrams. Skip them. Some of it is shaky, and you don't need a drop of it. You don't have to understand an engine to notice the car keeps pulling left.

It's simple, and you can check every step against your own life.

1. **A cue.** You get into bed. You step into the shower. The house goes quiet. You're bored, or wound up, or lonely, or a photo on your phone caught your eye.
2. **An urge.** A small, restless "go on".
3. **Relief.** You do it, or you open the site. The fidgeting stops.

That's the loop: cue, urge, relief. Every lap teaches you that this cue leads to that relief. Do it a few hundred times and you know the lesson by heart.

Now the cue alone sets off the urge. You step into the shower, and upstairs the banging starts. You don't have to be aroused, or in the mood, or even paying attention.

![](images/illustrations/flesh-the-shower-cue.png){.illustration width=80%}

It's a shortcut across a lawn. The first time, you barely leave a mark. Walk the same line every night for ten years and you've worn a bare strip of dirt, and your feet take it without asking.

![](images/illustrations/flesh-path-across-the-lawn.png){.illustration width=80%}

You don't have a broken brain or a weak character. You have a path worn in the grass. And grass grows back.

The path isn't what keeps you on it. What keeps you on it is the belief that something good waits at the other end.

One more thing. The urge isn't really desire for a woman. It's the wish to end a restless feeling. Hold on to that, because everything hangs on it.

That restless feeling is the Little Monster. Time to meet him properly.
