# Round 5: fit for service

Researched 2026-11-15 for James's "soldier showing up drunk for duty" framing. Every Bible verse was checked word for word against the WEB JSON (TehShrike/world-english-bible). Every other quote was checked against its source page, with its link given.

## James's idea, in his words

> You may be a Christian. You may be on God's side. And he does love you. But you are like a drunk, a drug addict. If you are in His army, you are like a soldier showing up drunk for duty. You may be loved but you are also unfit for service, unable to fight at full capacity, putting your fellow soldiers in danger, not giving them the full fire support they need. You have a unique gift. God knit you in the womb and knew you by name. You have something unique. But you must be fit to fight. Luckily it's not hard to beat. Remember. If you aren't putting your recovery first you are putting everything else, your family, your god, your purpose... Last.

He wants this touched on near the beginning and built into an inspirational call to action for men to become who they need to be. He's OK with splitting Ch 8 if needed.

## The shape of the argument

1. **You are loved.** This isn't in question (Romans 8:1, Psalm 139). The book never says "get clean or God won't love you."
2. **You are made for something.** Knit together on purpose, with good works prepared in advance (Psalm 139:13-16, Jeremiah 1:5, Ephesians 2:10, 1 Peter 4:10).
3. **But you're reporting for duty drunk.** Loved, enlisted, and unfit. Not out of the army, out of the fight (2 Timothy 2:3-4, 1 Thessalonians 5:6-8).
4. **And it isn't only about you.** The men beside you are down a gun (Joshua 7, 1 Corinthians 12:26). Your wife and kids are under-covered.
5. **Recovery comes first, or everything else comes last.** The old AA wisdom (Akron Manual 1940, "First Things First"), in Jesus's terms "seek first" (Matthew 6:33).
6. **And the good news: it's easy.** The Easy Peasy core. You don't need a decade of boot camp, you need to see through the lie. Then: get up, sober up, report for duty (Isaiah 6:8, Hebrews 12:12-13).

## The best single story: Uriah, the sober soldier, and David, who got him drunk

This is 2 Samuel 11 again (already used in Ch 8, "Out of the fight"), but from the other side. It fits James's image almost too well.

- 2 Samuel 11:11 (WEB): "Uriah said to David, “The ark, Israel, and Judah, are staying in tents; and my lord Joab and the servants of my lord, are encamped in the open field. Shall I then go into my house to eat and to drink, and to lie with my wife? As you live, and as your soul lives, I will not do this thing!”"
- 2 Samuel 11:13 (WEB): "When David had called him, he ate and drank before him; and he made him drunk. At evening, he went out to lie on his bed with the servants of his lord, but didn’t go down to his house."
- **The point.** Uriah, the foreign mercenary, won't even sleep with his own wife while his comrades are in the field. That's a soldier fit for duty. David, the king and the man after God's heart, is home, off-duty, and lustful, and he literally gets the good soldier drunk to cover his sin. The man who should have been leading the army is the one making soldiers unfit. Even drunk, Uriah still won't go home. Which one are you?

## Scripture (WEB, verified)

**Loved and made on purpose**
- Psalm 139:13-14: "For you formed my inmost being. You knit me together in my mother’s womb. I will give thanks to you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made."
- Psalm 139:16: "Your eyes saw my body. In your book they were all written, the days that were ordained for me, when as yet there were none of them."
- Jeremiah 1:5: "Before I formed you in the womb, I knew you. Before you were born, I sanctified you." (Spoken to Jeremiah; apply it as a pattern, not as a direct promise to every reader.)
- Ephesians 2:10: "For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared before that we would walk in them."
- 1 Peter 4:10: "As each has received a gift, employ it in serving one another, as good managers of the grace of God in its various forms."
- Note on "knew you by name": Isaiah 43:1 ("I have called you by your name") is the usual verse. It's spoken to Israel, so present it as a pattern too.

**The soldier on duty**
- 2 Timothy 2:3-4: "You therefore must endure hardship as a good soldier of Christ Jesus. No soldier on duty entangles himself in the affairs of life, that he may please him who enrolled him as a soldier."
- 2 Timothy 2:21: "If anyone therefore purges himself from these, he will be a vessel for honor, sanctified, and suitable for the master’s use, prepared for every good work." *Suitable for the master's use* is "fit for service" in Paul's words.
- Ephesians 6:10-11: "Finally, be strong in the Lord, and in the strength of his might. Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil."
- Ephesians 6:13: "that you may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand."
- 2 Timothy 4:7: "I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith." (A good closing line.)

**Sober, not drunk: the Bible already uses James's image**
- 1 Thessalonians 5:6, 8: "so then let’s not sleep, as the rest do, but let’s watch and be sober. ... But since we belong to the day, let’s be sober, putting on the breastplate of faith and love, and for a helmet, the hope of salvation." Paul puts sobriety and armor in the same breath.
- Romans 13:12-14: "let’s throw off the deeds of darkness, and let’s put on the armor of light. Let’s walk properly, as in the day; not in reveling and drunkenness, not in sexual promiscuity and lustful acts ... make no provision for the flesh, for its lusts." Drunkenness and lust in the same list, answered with armor.
- 1 Peter 4:7: "be of sound mind, self-controlled, and sober in prayer."
- Titus 2:11-12: "For the grace of God has appeared ... instructing us to the intent that, denying ungodliness and worldly lusts, we would live soberly". It's grace that trains us to live soberly. That's the book's whole balance in one verse.

**Training and fitness**
- 1 Corinthians 9:25-27: "Every man who strives in the games exercises self-control in all things. ... I fight like that, not beating the air, but I beat my body and bring it into submission, lest by any means, after I have preached to others, I myself should be rejected."
  - **Caution:** don't use 9:27 to suggest a man could lose his salvation. Use it for discipline and fitness, not for fear. (Alistair Begg reads the "disqualification" of 9:27 as a warning about apostasy, so quote him carefully or not at all.)
- Hebrews 12:1: "lay aside every weight and the sin which so easily entangles us, and let’s run with perseverance the race that is set before us".
- Hebrews 12:12-13: "Therefore lift up the hands that hang down and the feeble knees, and make straight paths for your feet, so what is lame may not be dislocated, but rather be healed." This is the call to action: get up and heal.

**Your fellow soldiers pay too**
- Joshua 7:11-12: "Israel has sinned. ... They have even taken some of the devoted things ... They have even put it among their own stuff. Therefore the children of Israel can’t stand before their enemies." One man's hidden sin (Achan's) and the whole army loses at Ai.
- 1 Corinthians 12:26: "When one member suffers, all the members suffer with it."
- Galatians 6:2: "Bear one another’s burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ." The counterweight: brothers help each other back to fitness, they don't shoot the wounded.
- 1 Chronicles 12:33: soldiers of Zebulun "who could command and were not of double heart". Undivided men.

**First things first**
- Matthew 6:33: "But seek first God’s Kingdom, and his righteousness; and all these things will be given to you as well."
- Luke 14:31: "what king, as he goes to encounter another king in war, will not sit down first and consider whether he is able ...?"

**The call**
- Isaiah 6:8: "I heard the Lord’s voice, saying, “Whom shall I send, and who will go for us?” Then I said, “Here I am. Send me!”"

## Supporting voices (verified)

**"Recovery first, or everything else last": the AA roots**
- *A Manual for Alcoholics Anonymous* ("The Akron Manual", 1940), PDF at https://media.silkworth.net/pdf/akron_manual_booklet.pdf:
  > "Sobriety is the most important thing in your life, without exception. You may believe your job, or your home life, or one of many other things comes first. But consider, if you do not get sober and stay sober, chances are you won't have a job, a family, or even sanity or life."
  
  This is the documented source of James's point. Public-domain-era pamphlet; keep the quote to one or two sentences anyway.
- AA Grapevine, "Second Things Second" (Feb 1972), https://www.aagrapevine.org/magazine/1972/feb/second-things-second: "FIRST THINGS FIRST is a great Slogan, and nobody needs it more than we alcoholics." / "It is a statement of the truth that without our sobriety little else of life will remain."
- The popular form, "anything you put ahead of your sobriety, you will lose", is a saying heard in meetings with no traceable author (no source gives one; Jeff Vircoe's history of the "First Things First" slogan, https://edgewoodhealthnetwork.com/blog/first-things-first-slogan-deep-roots-outside-recovery/, says even AA's first secretary didn't know where Bill W. got the slogans). Present it as "what they say in the rooms", not as a quote from anyone.
- James's own line, "if you aren't putting your recovery first, you're putting everything else last", is his and needs no source. It's a sharp inversion of the AA line. Use it as the book's own voice.

**One man's sin, the whole army's defeat**
- Ray Pritchard, "Sin in the Camp" (Keep Believing Ministries, 20 May 2019), https://www.keepbelieving.com/?p=24041: "One man disobeyed, and that's why there are 36 funerals." / "You've never committed a private sin because there is no such thing." / "Every evil word, every evil deed, every evil thought hurts those around you." The last two are exactly the answer to "it's private, it doesn't hurt anyone."

**The warrior who isn't fighting**
- Craig Groeschel, "Your Porn Battle Plan" (already in Ch 8 via round 4): the enemy wants "to distract you, disengage you, discourage you, take you out of the war". Reuse only if the Author wants a callback; don't repeat the full quote.
- Ed Litton (First Baptist North Mobile), in Baptist Press's report on his church's men's porn plan (https://www.baptistpress.com/resource-library/news/pornography-plague-confronted-by-churchs-battle-plan/print/): "You are kidding yourself if you think you can fight this battle by yourself". A man from that church: "I have actually come from behind enemy lines where I was a hostage to pornography." Good for the "fellow soldiers" point and for accountability.

**Discipline and calling**
- Rick Renner, "Don't Disqualify Yourself" (https://renner.org/articles/dont-disqualify-yourself/): "The primary reason people become discredited and shamed is that they don’t control their flesh." / "Don’t cut your flesh too much slack, friend".

## Suggestions for the Author

- **Near the beginning (Intro or Ch 1):** one short paragraph planting the image. "You may be on God's side, and he loves you. But right now you're a soldier showing up drunk for duty." Keep it short; the payoff comes later.
- **The full speech:** best near the end, where the Easy Peasy method turns to action. Ch 27 ("The final step") or Ch 32 ("Now"), or as a new short chapter just before Ch 31 ("The instructions"). Built like a commander's address:
  1. You're loved (Psalm 139).
  2. You're made for something no one else can do (Ephesians 2:10).
  3. Right now you're unfit, and your brothers, wife and kids are short a man (Uriah versus David, Joshua 7).
  4. Recovery first, or everything else last (Akron Manual, James's line).
  5. The good news: this one is easy to beat. You only need to see it for what it is.
  6. "Here I am. Send me." Get up, sober up, report for duty (Hebrews 12:12-13, Isaiah 6:8, 2 Timothy 4:7).
- **Ch 8:** if "Nobody's lever" and "Out of the fight" make it too long, the round 4 and round 5 material together could become a new chapter about strength and the fight (James OK'd a split).
- **Balance:** "unfit for service" must never read as "unloved" or "unsaved". Titus 2:11-12 (grace trains us to live soberly) and Romans 8:1 belong close to the speech. Fit to fight is the goal, not the entry fee.
- **"Fire support":** James's phrase is vivid and good in the book's voice. No source needed.

## Not used, and why

- Gideon's 300 (Judges 7): the "lapping men were more alert" reading is a popular sermon illustration but the text doesn't say why God chose them. Leave it out.
- Begg on 1 Cor 9:27: frames disqualification as possible apostasy, which pulls against the book's grace message.
- "Anything you put ahead of your sobriety you will lose": unattributable, so used only as "what they say in the rooms".
