## The case against (state it fairly in Ch 4 before answering)
- **NPR**, Lisa Hagen, "Consider This" on men's health and masturbation abstinence (Jan 2026). https://www.npr.org/2026/01/01/1198916105/mens-health-masturbation-abstinence
  - Ashley Winter, urologist, on retention boosting testosterone: "there is no medical evidence that it does any of those things" -> ch19, ch21 (agree: the book makes no such claim)
  - Joshua Grubbs (researcher and therapist, University of New Mexico): "Abstaining from masturbation is not a reasonable or, in my opinion, ethical treatment goal," / "If you want to view pornography less, that's a reasonable goal." -> **ch04: the strongest mainstream statement of the opposing view.** Answer: this book isn't medical treatment; it's a moral and spiritual choice the reader makes freely.
  - Kelsy Burke (sociologist): some who buy "porn addiction rhetoric also buy into these more extremist beliefs" -> ch04 (the book distances itself from manosphere misogyny)
  - NPR also notes that NoFap LLC says it is peer support for problematic porn use and "not anti-masturbation", that the US has no official "porn addiction" diagnosis, and that the WHO lists compulsive sexual behaviour disorder.
- **Dr. Martha Tara Lee** (sex coach), "The Harmful Effects of the NoFap Movement: A Sex-Positive Perspective", 1 May 2025. https://www.eroscoaching.com/2025/05/the-harmful-effects-of-the-nofap/
  - "Their shame isn't organic; it's manufactured by NoFap's pseudoscientific rhetoric." / "NoFap mislabels healthy sexual expression as pathological." / "NoFap's 'relapse' rhetoric mirrors substance recovery language, pathologizing biological impulses."
  - She cites "28.9% of NoFap participants report suicidal ideation post-'relapse'" (Prause & Binnie, 2024; **unverified**). Whatever the true figure, it underlines why the book's grace-first, no-shame mechanism matters, and why Ch 28 needs the crisis note.
- **How to use this:** Ch 4 can grant the critics three things honestly: (1) the "superpower" claims are unproven (the book agrees, Ch 19); (2) shame-driven streak culture harms men (the book agrees, Ch 10 and 20); (3) the body is fine (the book says so). Then it parts ways on one point: this book is about lust, the heart and drive, not the body (Matthew 5:28).
