The sequel's exact audience. Read 2026-11-14 in Chrome from talkingsober.com (public Discourse JSON); quotes copied exactly. Reddit's r/easypeasy and r/pornfree are blocked. Anonymise in the book ("a man on a recovery forum who had read EasyPeasy").

## "Easypeasy - is it worth?" (18 posts, Nov–Dec 2024) https://talkingsober.com/t/easypeasy-is-it-worth/189461
- Reader A (quit porn with EasyPeasy, still stuck on MO):
  - "In my case I 'devaulated' videos long ago, blocked them successfully, but I had/have problem with MO. Definitely I placed value in 'O', but it's hard to say where is the root of the problem." -> **Intro, ch02: the sequel's whole premise in one reader's words.** He places value in the orgasm itself, not the porn.
  - "in the past I 'cheat' very often - looked at 'soft' photos as alternatiive to get my body 'ready'. Sometimes I only 'checked' if my body response for touch." -> **ch24 Substitutes, ch11, ch22** ("just checking it works" is a real loophole men use)
  - After finishing EasyPeasy: "I don't care anymore if I have erection seeing photo, scene in movie or in the morning. And I don't try to get it to check if body works. I just left it to the nature." -> ch21 (morning erections are nothing), ch28
  - "I think nobody in my life pointed out that by avoiding PMO I loose nothing ... In the past it always looked like I sacrifice something." -> ch06
  - "That's my 12th sober day, and it's truly clean, no cheatting at all and no sacrifice!" / "In the past, keeping such a break period would be a tiring battle. Now, I just refer to myself as 'nonuser' ... And more important: I feel much happier as nonuser." -> ch21, ch26
  - But also: "keep my guard up and focus on achieving another milestone" — still counting milestones -> ch10 nuance
- Reader B (porn and lust; ex-SAA/SLAA, "a chronic relapser"): "After I read Easy Peasy, I followed up with Allen Carr's books ... these books helped me to uncover a major root problem that I had not yet identified. It was the fact that I was placing value on my DOC and felt that I was making a sacrifice by choosing to quit." -> ch06, ch10. Elsewhere (thread 183644, Jun 2024), he links it to faith: "Often, a broken relationship with God happens to be what I consider the biggest problem." and "it's brainwashing that tells me that porn can relieve stress or boredom." -> ch04, ch05. And on fantasy (thread 197816, Aug 2025): "The easiest thought for me to say, 'No' to, is the first one. And if I don't entertain the thought, I'm not going to do it." -> **ch14**
- Reader C, the sceptic (read 50–75%): "I don't think it goes deep enough." And the key objection: "you're losing your on-demand short-term gratification strategy ... if you lost nothing, nothing would change and you wouldn't notice anything, I don't understand that." -> **ch06 should answer this head-on** (you lose the drug and its hangover; "nothing" means nothing *good*). Reader A's reply: "I lose nothing, then what was that what caused so many problems? Were I hypnotised or something?"
- Reader C on what worked for him: realising he was "worshipping an ancient God" (Priapus) — "I felt like I needed to commit apostasy and leave this weird cult" -> ch04 (idolatry framing fits the Christian scaffold); he admits "It was definitely a sacrifice" and "If my life collapsed ... I would probably find it a lot harder to resist" -> contrast for ch23
