Read 2026-11-14 in Chrome from the full texts. Quotes copied exactly. These can stand in for modern creators in the body, and they're free to quote.

## Augustine, *Confessions*, Book VIII (E. B. Pusey translation, public domain)
Source: Project Gutenberg #3296, https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/3296/pg3296.txt

- **"Give me chastity and continency, only not yet."** Context: "But I wretched, most wretched, in the very commencement of my early youth, had begged chastity of Thee, and said, 'Give me chastity and continency, only not yet.' For I feared lest Thou shouldest hear me soon, and soon cure me of the disease of concupiscence, which I wished to have satisfied, rather than extinguished." (VIII.7)
  -> **ch16 Timing** (the "not yet" man), ch01. The perfect picture of a man who wants freedom *later*.
- **The chain:** "Which thing I was sighing for, bound as I was, not with another's irons, but by my own iron will. My will the enemy held, and thence had made a chain for me, and bound me. For of a forward will, was a lust made; and a lust served, became custom; and custom not resisted, became necessity. By which links, as it were, joined together (whence I called it a chain) a hard bondage held me enthralled." (VIII.5)
  -> **ch02 Why Is It Difficult** (how a habit becomes a need), ch03. Also: "Thus did my two wills, one new, and the other old, one carnal, the other spiritual, struggle within me; and by their discord, undid my soul." -> ch10 (willpower = civil war; pairs with Romans 7)
- **The old mistresses:** "The very toys of toys, and vanities of vanities, my ancient mistresses, still held me; they plucked my fleshy garment, and whispered softly, 'Dost thou cast us off? and from that moment shall we no more be with thee for ever? and from that moment shall not this or that be lawful for thee for ever?'" (VIII.11)
  -> **ch17 Will I Miss It?** — the fear of "never again" is the trap's last whisper. Also: "the worse whereto I was inured, prevailed more with me than the better whereto I was unused" -> ch06.
- **"Why not now?":** "How long, how long, 'tomorrow, and tomorrow?' Why not now? why not is there this hour an end to my uncleanness?" (VIII.12)
  -> **ch16, ch20, ch32 Now**.
- **The moment of decision (tolle lege):** a child's voice chanting "Take up and read; Take up and read"; he opens Paul and reads Romans 13:13–14, "Not in rioting and drunkenness, not in chambering and wantonness ... but put ye on the Lord Jesus Christ, and make not provision for the flesh, in concupiscence. No further would I read; nor needed I: for instantly at the end of this sentence, by a light as it were of serenity infused into my heart, all the darkness of doubt vanished away." (VIII.12)
  -> **ch26 Moment of Revelation, ch27 Final Step.** "make not provision for the flesh" -> ch25 (avoiding temptation). (Book should quote Romans 13:14 itself in the WEB.)

## John Owen, *Of the Mortification of Sin in Believers* (1656, public domain)
Source: CCEL plain text, https://www.ccel.org/ccel/o/owen/mort/cache/mort.txt

- **"be killing sin or it will be killing you."** Context: "Do you mortify; do you make it your daily work; be always at it whilst you live; cease not a day from this work; be killing sin or it will be killing you." (ch. 2)
  -> ch08, ch12. (Caution: "daily work ... cease not a day" sounds like a willpower programme; use the punchline, not the regime.)
- **Lust never stops at the small thing:** "Sin aims always at the utmost; every time it rises up to tempt or entice, might it have its own course, it would go out to the utmost sin in that kind. Every unclean thought or glance would be adultery if it could" ... "every rise of lust, might it have its course, would come to the height of villany: it is like the grave, that is never satisfied." Then: "it is modest, as it were, in its first motions and proposals, but having once got footing in the heart by them, it constantly makes good its ground, and presseth on to some farther degrees in the same kind." (ch. 2)
  -> **ch03 (the "red line" creeping), ch12 Just Once, ch14 Thought Life, ch11 Cutting Down.** Owen saying in 1656 what NoFap calls escalation.
- **Not by willpower:** "Mortification from a self-strength, carried on by ways of self-invention, unto the end of a self-righteousness, is the soul and substance of all false religion in the world." (ch. 1) / "A man may easier see without eyes, speak without a tongue, than truly mortify one sin without the Spirit." (ch. 8) / The Spirit "is the fire which burns up the very root of lust." (ch. 7)
  -> **ch10 The Willpower Method** — the Christian backbone for EasyPeasy's "willpower doesn't work". ch20.
- How lust fogs the mind: "Perplexing reasonings, extenuating promises, tumultuating desires, treacherous purposes of relinquishment, hopes of mercy, all have their share in disturbing the mind" ("treacherous purposes of relinquishment" = "I'll quit tomorrow") -> ch04, ch16

## Thomas Chalmers, "The Expulsive Power of a New Affection" (sermon on 1 John 2:15, c. 1820s, public domain)
Source read: Grace Gems edition, https://gracegems.org/2025/expulsive_power_of_a_new_affecti.htm (adds subheadings and may lightly modernise; check wording against the C.S. Lewis Institute PDF of the original, https://www.cslewisinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/ExpulsivePoweDiscourseIX.pdf, before printing)

- **Thesis:** "The heart is not so constituted; and the only way to dispossess it of an old affection--is by the expulsive power of a new one."
  -> **ch06 What Am I Giving Up** (the void), ch20, ch24 Substitutes (the "new affection" is God, not another pleasure — which squares Chalmers with EasyPeasy's no-substitutes rule).
- **Willpower can't do it:** "It is seldom that any of our tastes are made to disappear by a mere process of natural extinction. At least, it is very seldom, that this is done through the instrumentality of reasoning. It may be done by excessive pampering--but it is almost never done by the mere force of mental determination. But what cannot be destroyed, may be dispossessed" -> **ch10**
- "The heart must have something to cling to--and never, by its own voluntary consent, will it so denude itself of its attachments, that there shall not be one remaining object that can draw or solicit it." -> ch06, ch17
- "the boy ceases, at length, to be the slave of his appetite--but it is because a manlier taste has now brought it into subordination" -> ch08 (manhood), ch29
