## 4. What has happened to men and to society

General warning for the writer: every item here is a trend. **None of these sources shows that porn caused it**, and the best-known sexlessness study found the opposite association (4.1). The honest line is "this is the world lust promised to fill, and here is what it looks like", not "porn did this".

**4.1 Young men with no sex in the past year (General Social Survey).**
- Claim: the share of American men aged 18 to 24 reporting no sexual activity in the past year rose from about one in five to nearly one in three in under twenty years.
- Exact: men 18 to 24: 18.9% (2000 to 2002) to 30.9% (2016 to 2018). Women 18 to 24: 15.1% to 19.1% (not a significant change). Men 25 to 34: 7.0% to 14.1%. Women 25 to 34: 7.0% to 12.6%.
- Who: Ueda, Mercer, Ghaznavi and Herbenick, JAMA Network Open, 2020; General Social Survey, about 4,300 men and 5,200 women aged 18 to 44.
- What it does not show: cause. Men with low income, part-time or no work, and students were more likely to be sexually inactive. **In this study, porn use was associated with lower odds of sexual inactivity** (men who watched were more likely, not less, to have had sex), and the authors say the pornography theory was not supported by their analysis.
- URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7293001
- Confidence: PRIMARY (the paper). Solid numbers; do not attach a porn-caused-it claim to them.
- Commentary in the same journal, Jean Twenge, 2020 (joined from three pieces): "The increasing availability of pornography may play a role; however, because those who watch pornography are more sexually active, not less, this explanation is difficult to support with individual-level data." She points instead to delayed adulthood and screens in general (joined from pieces): "Put simply, there are now many more choices of things to do in the late evening than there once were and fewer opportunities to initiate sexual activity if both partners are engrossed in social media, electronic gaming, or binge watching." URL: https://ia601508.us.archive.org/30/items/twenge_2020_ic_200042/twenge_2020_ic_200042.pdf. PRIMARY.

**4.2 The same trend in a second survey (National Survey of Family Growth).**
- Exact: men aged 22 to 34 with no sex in the past year: 9% (2013 to 2015) to 24% (2022 to 2023); women 8% to 13%. Never had sex: men 4% to 10%; women 5% to 7%.
- Who: Lyman Stone, Institute for Family Studies, 21 Jan 2025.
- What he says causes it: marriage. "Married people have more sex, and for most young adults, marriage is occurring later or not at all." He rejects the idea that a few men are taking all the women ("no change at all"). He does not blame porn in this piece. In 2018 he wrote that "the primary cause of the rise in sexlessness is simply the increasing delay of marriage."
- URLs: https://ifstudies.org/blog/sexless-america-young-adults-are-having-less-sex and https://ifstudies.org/blog/male-sexlessness-is-rising-but-not-for-the-reasons-incels-claim
- Confidence: PRIMARY for IFS's analysis; IFS is a pro-marriage think tank (say so if named). Stone notes most of the rise came between 2017-19 and 2022-23 and could partly reflect COVID or a change in survey method.

**4.3 Most young men are single (Pew).**
- Exact: "63% of men under 30 describe themselves as single, compared with 34% of women in the same age group." Single = not married, not living with a partner, not in a committed relationship. 57% of all single adults said they were not looking for a relationship or dates.
- Who and when: Pew Research Center, survey of 6,034 US adults, 5 to 17 July 2022, published 8 Feb 2023.
- Does not show: why. Pew offers no cause.
- URL: https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2023/02/08/for-valentines-day-5-facts-about-single-americans/
- Confidence: PRIMARY.

**4.4 Later marriage, more people living alone (US Census Bureau).**
- Exact: median age at first marriage in 2025: 30.8 for men, 28.4 for women; in 1975: 23.5 and 21.1. One-person households: 39.7 million, 29% of all households in 2025, up from 20% in 1975. 58% of adults aged 18 to 24 lived in their parents' home in 2025.
- When: release of 2 Dec 2025.
- URL: https://www.census.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2025/families-and-living-arrangements.html
- Confidence: PRIMARY. Updated every year (mild rot risk: give the year).

**4.5 Births.**

URLs: https://cdc.gov/nchs/pressroom/releases/20260409.html and https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/vsrr/vsrr043.pdf. PRIMARY. ## 5. History and first-person testimony

### 5(a) J. D. Unwin, *Sex and Culture* (Oxford University Press, 1934)

- Who: Joseph Daniel Unwin, Cambridge-trained social anthropologist. 600-plus pages.
- What he claimed (Wikipedia's summary of the book, opened; plus the book's own Preface, opened as a scan on archive.org): he compared 80 "uncivilised" societies and a number of historical civilisations (the summary says 86 cultures in all), sorted them by how strictly they limited sex before and after marriage, and concluded that the stricter the limits, the greater the society's "social energy" and cultural achievement. Societies that kept pre-marital chastity and strict monogamy for generations rose; when the rules loosened, energy fell within about three generations. He named Athenians, Romans and English as reaching the highest stage.
- His own words, Preface (from the scan; each returned in pieces and joined; check against the scan, pages vii, xiii, xiv):
  - "I have not tried to prove a thesis, and have none to prove; I have merely conducted an inquiry" (p. vii)
  - "The evidence is that in the majority of cases compulsory continence produces social energy;" (p. xiii)
  - "Briefly stated, my final conclusion is that the cultural behaviour (as defined) of any human society depends, first, on the inherent nature of the human organism, and, secondly, on the state of energy into which, as the result of its sexual regulations, the society has arrived." (p. xiv)
  - URL: https://ia801309.us.archive.org/11/items/b20442580/b20442580.pdf
- He started from Freud's idea of sublimation, not from religion. He also argued (per the summary) that strict monogamy required legal equality of women, and wrote that "the subjection of women and children is intolerable and therefore temporary". That matters for a book that must never mock women.
- Admirer: Aldous Huxley called it a "work of the highest importance" (as quoted on the Wikipedia page; primary not opened). URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_and_Culture
- How scholars regard it:
  - It was reviewed seriously at the time: Morris Ginsberg in Nature, 9 Feb 1935 (the abstract calls it a "highly elaborate investigation" into "a causal relationship between sexual opportunity and cultural condition"), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/135205a0; Raymond Firth in the journal Africa, Jan 1936, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/africa/article/sex-and-culture-by-j-d-unwin-london-humphrey-milford-1934-pp-xxiv-676-diagram-36s/7E4B27F4980D615947B283E6C9A5D218. **Both full reviews are paywalled; I could not read their criticisms and so cannot quote any.**
  - Yale's Human Relations Area Files lists his hypothesis as "Supported", but by "Comparison of raw scores", with significance "Not Applicable": that is, by Unwin's own tables, never by a modern statistical test. https://hraf.yale.edu/hypotheses/1384
  - What can fairly be said without a quotable critic: the book is ninety years old; it rests on colonial-era travellers' and missionaries' reports; its ladder of "zoistic, manistic, deistic, rationalistic" cultures is a kind of ranking of peoples that anthropology abandoned; it measures "culture" narrowly (largely by religious practice); it shows a pattern, not a cause; and it has not been replicated with modern methods. Today it is cited mostly by social conservatives, rarely by working anthropologists.
- Confidence: his claims and Preface quotes PRIMARY (scan); reception WEAK (I could not open a named scholarly critique). Advice: usable in one careful paragraph, "one Oxford-published scholar in 1934 argued... he set out with no thesis... his method would not pass today, and nobody has repeated it". Do not write "history proves". Do not use the popular internet line that he "found no exception in any society": I did not find those words in his text.
