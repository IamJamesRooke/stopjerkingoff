## Theme 1: a man who has mastered lust can't be compromised or controlled

### The idea in one paragraph

The seductress's whole leverage is one sentence: *you want to sleep with me, so you'll do what I want.* The Instagram model, the OnlyFans "girlfriend", the coworker who flirts so you'll do her work, and the spy who honey-traps a diplomat all use the same lever. Take away the thirst and the lever is gone. A man who doesn't need anything from her can't be bought by her. That isn't contempt for women. It's freedom, and it frees him to love his wife (or future wife) cleanly, because nobody else has a hook in him.

## Theme 2: if the devil is real, this is exactly what he wants

### The quote: "The devil doesn't need to destroy you if he can distract you"

**Verdict: no traceable author. Use it as a saying, not a quote from anyone.**
- The exact wording appears on many church pages without a source. Example: Joshua Tapp, True Words Baptist (Louisville), 15 May 2025, https://truewordsbaptist.org/if-the-devil-cant-destroy-you-hell-just-distract-you/: "The devil doesn’t need to destroy you if he can distract you." It names no origin.
- It's a modern variant of an older pulpit saying, "If the devil can't make you bad, he'll make you busy." That line is usually credited to Corrie ten Boom. John Mark Comer writes "Corrie ten Boom once said that if the devil can't make you sin, he'll make you busy" (The Ruthless Elimination of Hurry, Bible.com plan day 1, https://www.bible.com/en-GB/reading-plans/16928-the-ruthless-elimination-of-hurry/day/1). But no one gives a book, talk or date for it, and Craig Groeschel introduces the same line with "I've heard it said" (Weird, https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/7962539). Treat the ten Boom credit as unverified.
- **Suggested wording:** "There's a saying you'll hear in a lot of churches: the devil doesn't need to destroy you if he can distract you." Don't attribute it to anyone.

### A verified line that says the same thing, with a real author

- Marshall Segal, Desiring God (link above): "Temptation will please you to abuse you, seduce you to undo you, distract you to destroy you." Confirmed verbatim. This is the citable version of James's point.
- Craig Groeschel, "Your Porn Battle Plan" (Life.Church; transcript at https://sermons.love/craig-groeschel/5889-craig-groeschel-your-porn-battle-plan.html): the enemy's "goal is to steal, kill and destroy." "He wants to distract you, disengage you, discourage you, take you out of the war". He goes straight on to name the battle "of lust that's often triggered through pornography" and then preaches 2 Samuel 11. *Take you out of the war* is exactly James's "completely took you out of the fight."
  - Note: the transcript reads "the distracted warrior is always a dangerous warrior", which looks like a transcription slip for "*endangered* warrior". Don't quote that half.

### The perfect text: David, out of the fight

- 2 Samuel 11:1-2 (WEB): "At the return of the year, at the time when kings go out, David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel ... But David stayed at Jerusalem. At evening, David arose from his bed and walked on the roof of the king’s house. From the roof, he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was very beautiful to look at."
  - Note: the WEB says "at the time when kings go out", not "go out to battle". Quote it as written; the context is war.
  - The point: the greatest warrior-king in Israel's history wasn't beaten on the battlefield. He was beaten in bed in the afternoon, because he wasn't on the battlefield. No sword was needed, just a bit of skin seen from a roof. That's the "layup".
- David Guzik on the same passage (sermonindex PDF, https://sermonindex.net/pdf-text/pdf/d/david-guzik--2-samuel-a-not-so-sudden-fall.pdf): "He should have been out at the battle, but he wasn't." / "Satan was laying siege to the heart of David."

### Scripture for the enemy and the war (WEB, verified)

- 1 Peter 5:8: "Be sober and self-controlled. Be watchful. Your adversary, the devil, walks around like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour."
- 1 Peter 2:11: "abstain from fleshly lusts, which war against the soul". Lust isn't a hobby; it's a combatant.
- Ephesians 6:11-12: "Put on the whole armor of God, that you may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil. For our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against ... the spiritual forces of wickedness in the heavenly places."
- John 10:10: "The thief only comes to steal, kill, and destroy. I came that they may have life, and may have it abundantly."
- 2 Timothy 2:4: "No soldier on duty entangles himself in the affairs of life, that he may please him who enrolled him as a soldier."
- Nehemiah 6:3: "I am doing a great work, so that I can’t come down. Why should the work cease, while I leave it, and come down to you?" This is the anti-distraction verse: the answer to every "come down for a minute" from the screen.
- James 1:14-15 (the honest counterweight, so the devil isn't an excuse): "each one is tempted when he is drawn away by his own lust and enticed. Then the lust, when it has conceived, bears sin."

### C. S. Lewis, The Screwtape Letters (in copyright; brief quotes only)

- Letter 9 (p. 44 in the edition cited by Justin Taylor, TGC, https://www.thegospelcoalition.org/blogs/justin-taylor/ever-increasing-craving-for-ever/): "An ever increasing craving for an ever diminishing pleasure is the formula." That one sentence describes escalation in porn.
- From the same passage: "To get a man's soul and give him nothing in return". Use one of these two, not both, to stay within fair use.
- Preface (https://www.thecatholicthing.org/2018/07/03/preface-to-the-screwtape-letters/): "There are two equal and opposite errors into which our race can fall about the devils." Lewis names them as disbelief and unhealthy fascination. This is useful for keeping the devil chapter balanced: take him seriously, but don't obsess or blame him for what's yours (James 1:14).

### For the atheist reader: call it evil, the result is the same

- Baudelaire, "Le Joueur généreux" (Le Figaro, 1864): "la plus belle des ruses du Diable est de vous persuader qu'il n'existe pas" ("the cleverest ruse of the Devil is to persuade you he does not exist", Shipley's 1919 translation). Source traced by Quote Investigator, https://quoteinvestigator.com/2018/03/20/devil/. The popular "greatest trick the devil ever pulled" wording comes from the film The Usual Suspects (1995), so credit Baudelaire for the idea and the film for that wording.
- **Suggested framing for non-believers:** you don't need horns and a pitchfork. Look at the results and ask who benefits. An industry earns more the longer you stay aroused, isolated and tired. Something wants men soft, numb and spent, with no energy for their wives, kids, work or causes. Call it the devil, call it evil, call it a business model; it's working on you either way. The "evil win" is that nobody had to fight you. A little skin on a screen and you took yourself off the field: "Disabled. No energy," as James put it.

### Where it fits

- **Ch 5 (the times we reach for it):** David's idle afternoon is the classic trigger story (bored, alone, off-duty, evening).
- **Ch 8 (what it's doing to you):** "taken out of the fight". Energy, drive and courage get spent on pixels.
- **A short new section, or a strong passage in Ch 3 (the flesh) or Ch 4 (the world's lies):** "If the devil is real, he loves this." Use the saying (unattributed), Segal's line, Groeschel's "take you out of the war", 1 Peter 5:8, then the atheist framing.
- **Ch 29 (help those on the sinking ship) / closing chapters:** the soldier image (2 Tim 2:4, Nehemiah 6:3). A free man gets back into the fight.
- **Balance:** keep James 1:14 and Lewis's "two errors" near any devil passage so it never reads as "the devil made me do it". Grace stays in view (Romans 8:1).
