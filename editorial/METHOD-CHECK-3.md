# Method check 3: Easy Way fidelity after the 2026-11-21 editorial pass

Read cold, front to back, on main at f2e3233: the Introduction, all chapters in `_bookdown.yml` order, the Appendix, Attribution, FAQ, Why This Book Exists and About. Read only, and no chapter files were touched. Line numbers are the file's own (`chapters/NAME.md:LINE`).

## Verdict

The method holds. Nothing in the book's structure asks for willpower. The last round's five leaks are all fixed:
- The door in Just Once is now "you don't open it".
- Whose Voice no longer says No again.
- The tenant no longer calls for life.
- The fiancé is "glad to keep it".
- The room in Now is "yours".

The fear checklist, the "for a time" quote, the cutting-down deals and the counter are still gone.

Three things need fixing. One is a line the editorial pass added: in The Easy Way to Stop, Chalmers now tells the non-believer that an old want only leaves when something he loves more arrives. That brings back the idea of a replacement right at the method chapter. The other two blur the plain No. Just Once still tells the reader to "say it once" every time the voice comes. The verses page tells him to say verses aloud whenever the Little Monster stirs, which is the opposite of "never answer again". Four smaller fixes follow, then three low ones.

## Key question: does anything imply willpower is needed?

The plain No is said once, at the final step, and afterward the reader simply doesn't answer. These lines get that right:
- 12b:50, "one plain No, with no hope left in it"
- 13:57, "When you give your answer, you give it once"
- 22a:11, "You have said it once, and outright"
- 28:27, "a plain No that you mean... once"
- 28:51, the prayer: "I can't keep this by my own strength, and I'm not going to try"
- 28a:13, "The other three keep it said"
- 32:12, "You said your plain No, once and outright"

These blur it: 13:51 (H2), 32:20 (M1) and, more mildly, 22a:19 (M2). One line, 21:62 (H1), says freedom also needs a new love to fill the space.

## High

### H1. The Easy Way to Stop, 21:62: "An old want doesn't leave an empty heart" (new in this pass)

The 11-21 pass added two sentences for the non-believer after the Chalmers quote: "If you're not a believer, take the law without the name. An old want doesn't leave an empty heart. It leaves when there is something you love more."

That contradicts the Empty House in three places:
- 01b:27: once a man sees the tenant gave nothing, "there is no vacancy".
- 25:20: "There is no substitute for lust, because nothing is missing".
- 01b:31: for the Christian, the owner "doesn't come as a substitute".

The new sentences tell the non-believer that seeing through the lie isn't enough, and that he needs a replacement love to push the want out. That is the substitute idea, placed in the method chapter itself. The Appendix entry, 34:56, says the same: "why a desire leaves only when a greater love arrives".

**Fix, 21:62.** Keep Chalmers and the sentence about God. Replace the last three sentences:

> If you're not a believer, take the half of it that holds for any man. A want is never pushed out by force. It goes when you see that it never gave you anything.

**Fix, 34:56.** Change the description to:

> One sermon on how a greater love pushes out a lesser one.

### H2. Just Once, 13:51: "And when the voice comes, remember the four. Say it once."

Read aloud, this gives a routine to run every time the voice comes: say No, give no reason, don't soften it. Saying No each time is the repeated No. It contradicts three places:
- 12b:53: "Even telling him to go away is contact".
- 13:57, six lines later: "you give it once".
- 22a:11 and 28a:13, which already split the four correctly: the first is said once, and the other three keep it said.

**Fix, 13:51.** Replace the first two sentences:

> And when the voice comes, remember the four. The first is said only once, at the end of this book, and then it is said for good. The other three are how it stays said. Give no reason. Don't soften it. Never answer again.

Keep the rest of the paragraph ("You don't have to win the argument...") as it is.

## Medium

### M1. The Rules in One Place, 32:20: "say them aloud when the Little Monster stirs"

The rules above this line tell the reader to "never answer again", and Let It Ring tells him to stay in the chair. This line then tells him to answer every stir with a spoken verse. It also calls the verses the book's version of "affirmations", which turns them into a weapon against the itch.

**Fix.** Replace the paragraph:

> Here are some older words to keep. Pick one or two and learn them by heart. They aren't for shouting back at the Little Monster, because he gets no answer. They are for you, on any ordinary day, to remember whose you are. If you're not a believer, borrow them anyway. They will serve you better than a slogan.

(29:59, "If a twinge comes and you're a praying man, pray", can stay. That is speaking to God, not answering the caller, and the line goes straight on to "get on with your day".)

### M2. Whose Voice Is That?, 22a:19: "a true sentence to set against a false one"

Twelve lines earlier the same chapter says, "Don't argue and don't agree." Setting a sentence against the voice is arguing with it. The list itself is fine. Only the framing needs to change.

**Fix.** Replace the first two sentences:

> These aren't spells, and they aren't answers to the voice. He gets none. Each one is a true sentence for you, to remind you of what you already know.

### M3. Fit for Service, 27b:3: the epigraph opens with "endure hardship"

This is the last chapter before the final step. The book has just said the step "doesn't take courage" (27b:35), and the epigraph opens with endurance.

**Fix.** Keep only verse 4, and the soldier image survives:

> "No soldier on duty entangles himself in the affairs of life, that he may please him who enrolled him as a soldier."
>
> — 2 Timothy 2:4

### M4. The Empty House, 01b:15: the phone in the kitchen as an example of failed sweeping

01b:15 gives "you left the phone in the kitchen at bedtime" as an example of sweeping the house by force. But 26:54 and the FAQ (faq:83) recommend exactly that: "charge the phone in the kitchen". A careful listener will hear the book contradict itself.

**Fix, 01b:15.** Replace the second example:

> Or you deleted every app and blocked every site, and the reel in your memory started up in their place.

## Low

This echoes 01c:43 on purpose.

### L2. The Locked Door, 16:13: "Lust's other two faces"

This is left over from the older three-faces model. Elsewhere the book now uses five mouths.

**Fix.**

> Lust's other mouths live the same way.

Keep the two sentences that follow.

### L3. Good News, Twice, 03d:19: "find the path across the lawn still there waiting for him"

Four lines later the same chapter says, "Grass grows back", and 17:43 agrees. The point of 03d:19 is that one time wears the path in again, not that it never grew over.

**Fix.** End the sentence:

> ...have "just one", and be back on the old path within the week.
