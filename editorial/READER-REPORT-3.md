# Reader report 3: nine cold readers on the restructured book

Written 2026-11-20 from nine full cold reads of main at 395fee4: 69 files, 63,741 prose words (the Introduction plus 66 chapter files plus the Attribution; HTML comments and code chunks excluded). Each reader saw only the book: no plan, no earlier reports. Read only: no chapter was edited. This is the "simulated readers, run again" step of the Quality gate in `HYDRA-PLAN.md`. It follows `READER-REPORT.md` and `READER-REPORT-2.md`, and checks what is still true after the restructure.

**The nine readers** (simulated; treat as strong hints, not data):
1. **Churchgoer**, 28, single, evangelical small group, years of recommitments.
2. **Non-Christian**, 31, secular, heavy porn and masturbation.
3. **Skeptic**, 20, single university student, online since ten, ex-church.
4. **Relapser**, 36, dozens of failed quits, a 90-day streak ended on day 61.
5. **Porn-only man**, 26, steady girlfriend, nightly screens only; does not chase, pay or cheat.
6. **Hookup man**, 34, apps several times a week, honest with women, not religious.
7. **Simp**, 29, pays creators, orbits a woman friend, in debt.
8. **Married man**, 43, lapsed Catholic, wife has gone quiet about it.
9. **Engaged man**, 27, sleeping with his fiancée, porn is a problem between them.

Every one kept reading past the first 1,500 words, most of them narrowly. All nine would finish (several by skipping); only the churchgoer would recommend it freely, and the rest would pass it on only to a Christian friend or only with a reading order.

## 4. Where each type felt unaddressed

Ordered by how big I think the group is among readers who would pick this up, with the fix. A fix is marked **[blur]** when it risks widening the book beyond what James wants.

**Relapser (36).** The book dismisses his tools (blockers, accountability partners: `16-a-secret-habit`, `28b-who-needs-to-know`, `30-help-those-on-the-sinking-ship`) and says nothing for a slip that keeps coming weekly for years, an urge that does not fade in two minutes, or being lonely with no one to tell. `29b-if-you-slip` sits near the end (about 90% in): he may quit before he reaches it. The Appendix says only "a counselor" with nothing on therapy for compulsion, group programs or the depression and shame underneath. His strongest quit was "absurdly easy". Fix: a pointer to 29b in the Introduction or early chapters; an honest clause in `11` that the fight is real and weeks of strong urges are normal; a short "if this keeps happening" line in the Appendix.

**Hookup man (34).** No chapter speaks to the honest, mutual, non-religious casual man. Dating apps are treated as "a hit" or "a wife search"; his actual loop (open the app, match, message) gets a few lines and no plan for Friday nights or touch. `01c` and `29a` state the line without argument, and "If you slept with her and left, you were one of them" (`04b`) angers him. Fix: one practical section on the app loop (delete, no "just checking", what to do with the evening); in `01c`/`29a` give the secular reason in three sentences (the gift-or-hit test already does this; lead with it). **[blur]** if it becomes a dating guide.

**Engaged man (27).** No rung on the Ladder for "engaged, sleeping together, porn is a couple problem". `01c` and `14f` push hard on sex with his fiancée, and then the book goes quiet: instruction 1 and the final-step prayer don't mention sleeping with her, and the clause "nothing you do harms another person or breaks the law" (`28-the-final-step`) reads to him as **permission**. `14f`'s "as good as married... then marry" angers him with no practical next step (it ends with "you start from today", one line), and "she may be hurt for an evening" treats her as someone to manage. He wants: what to say to her, read it together or not, premarital counselling. Fix: keep the line, add three practical sentences to `14f` and drop the "breaks the law" clause or add "and nothing that is hers to give" so it can't be read as permission. **[blur]** if it grows into a premarital chapter.

**Simp (29).** His lust is for connection, not orgasm: nothing on parasocial attachment, creators who reply by name, or "she might like me". Debt gets one sentence (`19`) and "cancel the subscriptions" (`26`); the orbited friend in `14b` has no step; the Little Monster as an itch doesn't match wanting a person; "being worked" in `14a` offends him because he thinks some creators are real. `21` and `50` (body, death grip) are technique he can't use. Fix: an early line in `06`-`13` that sends chase-men to the chapter on paying for attention; one short section with the monthly sum written out, cancel steps, and what to say (or not say) to the friend; soften "he is being worked" to "even when she is kind, it's her job".

**Married man (43).** "Tell her" assumes a wife who doesn't know; nothing for a wife who has gone quiet or is resigned rather than angry; nothing on rebuilding intimacy or couples counselling; "common and mostly harmless" is answered only in `12` and `45`. His chapter (`14c`) comes after four chapters of singles, simps and collectors. Fix: move `14c` earlier in the run, add two lines on the quiet wife and one pointer to couples counselling.

**Porn-only man (26).** He is unmarried and partnered, uses only porn, doesn't chase, pay or cheat, and isn't sure he wants to stop for good; the book says "wife" about forty times and never covers whether to tell a girlfriend. He feels "boxed in with strangers" by the five-mouth framing, the "porn is prostitution / crack" line offends him, and he reads the final-step prayer as accusing him. Fix: give the Ladder and the types a "partnered, not married" rung; a porn-only version of the prayer; a one-line "if you only know the first mouth, this is the part for you". `14a`'s "the nightly man" was the first place he saw himself.

**Skeptic (20).** Wants something concrete for a dorm student (phone, nights, flatmates) and a plainer treatment of casual consensual sex. Overreach costs credibility: "Nothing. Not one thing", "you never enjoyed it", "mild itch like a dentist's waiting room", porn equated with prostitution, "Isn't it marvelous" and "YES! I'M FREE!" as self-talk, the "read Mark" nudge after "play along". The 11-bullet quiz and the caps "DO NOT SKIP CHAPTERS" nearly lost him.

**Non-Christian (31).** Faith becomes a requirement here (all still there): `29a` ("The Bible's answer... if you're single, wait", no secular reason); `21-the-easy-way-to-stop` (the decision is renamed "repent and receive grace", and Chalmers says nothing smaller than love of God can push the desire out, which contradicts the early "faith is not required"); `28-the-final-step` (prayer first, secular second, "I have used people... That was wrong"); `22c`/`29b` (Psalm 32 and confession to God or a priest, with no secular equivalent); `33-now` (Mark, "God, if you're there", after the promise of no conversion); `01b-the-empty-house` (the spirit house story assumes belief; two sentences rescue it); the devil in `27b` and `30` (saved by "If you don't believe in a devil, keep the picture" and "call it a business model"); the closing sections of `14c`. Also: single men with no partner, church or confidant to tell; the reading list is all Christian; the wives, parents and pastors chapter sits after the farewell, "an odd last chapter".

**Churchgoer (28).** Likes the book and would recommend it. His concerns are the opposite of the secular readers': the caller drill reads as technique and the Spirit and church appear mainly in rule 11; the book pushes back on pledges, weekly check-ins and counters and says "tell one man" only after he has stopped, which clashes with James 5:16 and his small group (he wants a "confess now, with your group" path); Job's "covenant with my eyes" (`23`) sits against "no vow"; `04b` (abortion, a quoted author) and `14d` (Ahab) are culture-war detours; the priest and Confession references skew Catholic; "easy" needs a clause saying it means grace, not effortless; the Little Monster can minimise real shame. Both his and the secular readers' needs are met by **labelling**: Christian paths marked as Christian, with one secular line beside each, not by cutting either.

## 6. The recurring images

## 7. The Quality-gate watches

- **Does the line chapter become a willpower vow?** Nobody read it as a vow, but eight of nine heard it as a rule or lecture, and the engaged man as a threat. The position isn't the issue; the placement (sixth chapter, before the book has earned trust), the one-way statement, and the duplicate in `29a` are.
- **The fiancée chapter** (`14f`) works for the engaged reader and is warm, short and mostly free of scolding; "as good as married... then marry" and "she may be hurt for an evening" are the two places to adjust, plus one practical step.
- **Grace getting the credit:** the churchgoer finds it does (Romans 8:1, "nothing in this book saves anyone"). The non-Christian finds Christ's work bound into the decision itself (`21`).
- **Does the empty house make anyone feel possessed?** No reader said so. The non-Christian says it assumes belief; the churchgoer wants more of the Spirit. No pagan-myth complaint (the Hydra is gone).
- **Five-mouth coverage** reads clearly as a device but is over-restated; see section 3.
- **Hooks and lengths:** the three short chapters work (`15d` 469, `14f` 541, `01c` 704 is fine on length); the problem is run length of the four-to-six-chapter blocks, not any single chapter.
