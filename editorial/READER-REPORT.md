# Reader report: four simulated readers

Written 2026-11-17 from four full reads of main (commit 81955ba) by four reader personas. Inputs for the Author's cut, polish and read-aloud passes. This file changes no chapter text. The raw per-chapter notes (about 52,000 words) are not in the repo; ask for them if you need one chapter's detail.

**Personas:** (S) a 19-year-old sceptical student; (M) a 41-year-old married churchgoer with a secret habit; (N) a 30-year-old non-Christian heavy user; (W) a 38-year-old Christian wife who found her husband's use, reading over his shoulder. They are simulated, so treat this as a strong hint about where real readers will struggle, not as data.

## 1. Word count

- **Prose total: 70,402 words** for Introduction + Ch 1 to 33 (including 26b and 28b) + Attribution, with HTML comments, code chunks and the website-only FAQ and "Why this book exists" excluded. (The raw `.Rmd` files total 90,071 including comments, FAQ and Why This Book Exists.) BIBLE.md says about 69,000; the 60,000 in the planning chat was low.

Reaching the 35,000 to 45,000 range would need Ch 30, the Appendix and the Attribution moved out of the audio/numbered book as well (about 4,600 words) and the deeper "could be" targets in the table.

## 2. Top cross-persona problems, ranked

1. **Ch 26b "Fit for Service" is the worst chapter for every reader** (engagement 3, 3, 6, 3). It is a sermon (David and Uriah, Ai, Nehemiah) then a guilt list ("your wife is short a husband, your church is short a man"). S and N quit here. W: Bathsheba is "a woman bathing", unnamed, no voice, no harm. Fix: keep about 450 words (the "who gains from your habit" idea, "you were worth taking out of the fight", one line on people who depend on you, a secular wording), move the rest to a believers-only section or appendix, and rewrite the David section so Uriah and Bathsheba are people who were harmed.
2. **Ch 8 breaks the Introduction's promise "nobody is going to shame you".** All four flag the "coward" run, Samson and the "Nobody's lever" section, four ruined marriages, the "fuse", and the Piper/Challies stack. W also reads "lever" as blaming the woman (Delilah, the woman selling subscriptions). It is the longest chapter and the first big drop in engagement (4 to 5). Cut by about 1,500 and rewrite the tone.
3. **The Introduction is too long and loses people at about word 150.** 3,926 words; S says it should be about 1,200, M about 2,000, N cuts 700. The "drunk soldier" line sits next to "nobody will shame you" and nearly loses S, M and W. The "About this book" provenance, the three-faces essay, the glossary and the eight-testimony quote wall are skipped by everyone. It is also written for young single men (M gets one clause). All four still kept reading, mainly for "no ninety-day counter", "carry on as normal" and the checklist.
4. **The final step is a gate, and the secular reader is stranded.** Ch 1, 7, 16 and 20 each foreshadow the prayer (N: builds dread, say it once in the Introduction). Ch 27 is a Christian prayer with a thin off-ramp; Ch 28 lists "he skipped the prayer" as a reason the method fails; Ch 31 opens "this page is for the man who has prayed". S: after nineteen chapters of "no vows", the climax is a spoken vow ("I will not masturbate again"). N: about 70% of the method works without faith, but he is told it doesn't. Fix: print a full secular twin of the prayer, reword the vow as a decision, and say honestly in the Introduction what the end asks.
5. **Repetition.** Counts the readers reported: "a man who can't control his lust can't be trusted" at least 9 times; the drunk soldier 9; Augustine 8 (plus Owen, Lewis, Pascal re-quoted); "carry on as normal" restated 6 times; "YES! I'M FREE!" 8+ times in the last chapters (it becomes a chant aloud). Ch 10 to 13 re-tell Ch 2 to 6. The middle (Ch 16 to 24) is the flattest stretch (engagement 4 to 6).
6. **Over-promising and self-contradiction.** "Easy", "absurdly easy", "permanent", "cravings zero", "nothing to lose", "withdrawal is a few evenings" (Ch 6/16) against the flatline in Ch 21. "I still want it, so you contradict yourself" and "failure means you broke an instruction" are heads-I-win arguments that S, M and N all flag. Ch 16 has an internal contradiction M asks to resolve before cutting, and the "you have a roof and a fridge" line dismisses stress and nearly loses S.
7. **Women and the wife.** W's quit list: 26b, the lever material, and Ch 30 "To wives" (about 700 of 70,000 words, half of it telling her how to behave: praise him, don't hit back, don't check his phone). Missing across the book: naming it as betrayal, one full disclosure, what he owes her, her setting the pace of trust, betrayal-trauma/couples help. Also her no, tiredness or illness appears as his trigger (Ch 5, 12, 13, 14, 16, 25) with no line saying it is never his excuse. "Carry on as normal" needs one line that harm to someone at home is happening now.
8. **Tone swings between grace and blame** (M): "find the instruction you broke" and "then you've missed an instruction" against the lovely mercy in 25, 27, 28b. "Don't mope. Rejoice." and "Isn't it marvellous?" jar after what he has done; W wants sorrow before rejoicing.
9. **Structure and findability.** The slip chapter (28b) comes after the final prayer, so a man who relapses may never reach it (M, N: move it forward or signpost it from the Introduction). Checklists duplicate across Ch 27, 28 and 31. The best practical tools sit in the Appendix ("Whose voice is that?", the Coping Lines): all four say move them into the main text. Ch 30 (for wives, parents, pastors) is not for the man reading and should not sit in the numbered sequence.
10. **Missing pieces.** A secular finish; a first-72-hours plan for a daily user; a one-page "1am" summary; a clinical next step for compulsive use; honesty about success rates; a married-man section (Ch 5, 15, 23); guidance for single young men on dating (S); a real wives' section and resources; a title M can carry home to his wife or pastor.

**Wife (W).** Would not hand it over as it stands; with the changes she would give him the whole book and sit beside him. ## 5. The opening

All four kept reading, but barely, and none were won by the first 1,500 words; they stayed because of three things: no ninety-day counter to babysit, "carry on as normal" (you don't have to stop while you read), and the checklist/"button" thought experiment. They were nearly lost by: the DO NOT SKIP shouting (five times), "drunk soldier" at about word 150 right beside "nobody will shame you", "if you're an atheist, play along", and the provenance and glossary block. Suggested order for the Author's polish: (1) the hook line, then the promise ("you don't have to stop to start"), (2) who this is for, in one line each (single, married, non-believer, a wife reading), (3) the honest line about what the end asks, (4) the checklist, (5) move provenance, glossary and testimonials to the end or the website. Hook candidates the readers liked: "desire looks at a person, lust looks at something to use", "stopping was never the problem", "his will was fine" (Ch 10). Consider holding the soldier frame until after the reader has a reason to trust the book. M also notes the title is not something he can carry home to his wife or pastor (the cover can't change, but a subtitle on the web or audio can).

## 6. The ending

- **Reader feelings on the last page:** S: flattered, a bit pressured, slightly alone ("congratulations, you walked out" to a reader who hasn't acted yet). N: warmly blessed but recruited ("report for duty and bring somebody with you"), unsure he is free. W: congratulated on his behalf and unseen on hers.
- **Missing at the end:** a plain one-page summary for 1am; a secular finish; a first-72-hours plan; what to do if the urges are compulsive (a clinical next step); a step where he goes to his wife and asks what she needs, and a repair plan with professional help; "tell her and one man the same day" after a slip; honesty that some men will need help beyond a book.
- **Specific fixes:** cut "YES! I'M FREE!" to one use (it is 8+ now); stop saying "free" before the reader has acted; move 28b forward and end the book on the mercy of 28b and the prodigal "I still smell like yesterday" rather than a recruiting call; keep the Numbers 6 blessing, which all four liked, but check "Yahweh" for the narrator; stop the ending with a URL.
