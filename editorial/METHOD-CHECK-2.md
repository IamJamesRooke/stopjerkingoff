# Method Check 2: The Restructured Book

This is the second Easy Way fidelity audit, run on 2026-11-20 against main at commit c514bd9. The book now has 63 chapters, the new swept-and-empty-house image, the five mouths and the line. I read every chapter from the Introduction to the end cold, in running order, along with the quality gate in editorial/HYDRA-PLAN.md. No chapter text was changed.

I gave the most weight to the five chapters flagged for this check: The Empty House, Where the Line Is, The Back Door, The Collector and the Customer, and But I'm Going to Marry Her.

The coordinator's key question was: **does anything imply that willpower is needed, when the book promises one plain No, said once?**

## Verdict

**Yes, in five places. All five are wording, not structure, and all can be fixed.** The method itself is intact, and it is clearer than before.

- **The Empty House** is the best image the book has had. It says the Willpower Method in Jesus's own words: a man throws the thing out by force, keeps the room ready, and it comes back. Its cure is the Easy Way cure: see that the tenant never paid rent, and then there is no vacancy. It keeps the enemy small, and it asks the reader not to throw anything out yet.
- **The Back Door** is strongly on-method. It names the hand's own lie ("this is nothing") and takes it apart.
- **The Collector and the Customer** removes the illusion of benefit (the "at least") rather than relying on fear, apart from one fear-heavy list (M6).
- **Do Not Negotiate** turns Carr's "never take one" into "never answer", and that is a gift to the method. "The plain No, said once" now runs consistently through The Easy Method, The Empty House, Do Not Negotiate, Just Once, The Final Step, Let It Ring and the Rules.
- **The fixes from the first check are in.** The vow ends "I give it gladly". The prayer says "it never gave me anything". There is no checklist after "YES! I'M FREE!". The David retelling in Fit for Service is trimmed. "Block the page tonight" and the "for a time" quote are gone.

Where the book leaks willpower, it is because the new pictures have started to contradict each other:

1. **The door.** Just Once says to rule over sin by keeping "the door shut". Two Wills calls holding a door shut a siege, which is the Willpower Method. The Rules says "You aren't guarding a door."
2. **The repeated No.** Whose Voice Is That tells the reader to "say No" each time the voice comes, as a test. Do Not Negotiate says that even telling him to go away is contact.
3. **The tenant who never dies.** Let It Ring says the old tenant "isn't dead" and may telephone "for the rest of your life". Six chapters say the Little Monster starves, dies and stops calling. A caller you must ignore for life is willpower for life, which is exactly what Beware of Cutting Down says the reader is escaping.
4. **The man who can wait.** The engaged man is told to become "a man who can want her and wait". That is endurance of a real deprivation. The Easy Way frame is that nothing is lost, only kept.
5. **The empty room.** Now ends with "An empty room doesn't stay empty for long, so decide what goes in it." After the Empty House, that reads as "fill the gap, or the seven come back". That is the substitute logic Don't Swap It for Something Else rules out.

Moralizing and fear do not outweigh the method. But three chapters added heavy material this round: The Lies About Porn and About Women, Just One Little Look, and The Collector and the Customer. Those are M4 to M6.

## Ranked fixes

H = high, M = medium, L = low. Line numbers are for the `chapters/*.md` files at c514bd9. Match on the quoted text.

### H1. "The door stays shut" is the siege that Two Wills condemns

**Where:** `13-just-once.md:69` and `:77`.

**Problem:** "You are to rule over it. Ruling doesn't mean wrestling him on the doorstep. It means the door stays shut." and "Leave the door shut." Two Wills (`11c-two-wills.md:25`) defines the Willpower Method as "holding a door shut for the rest of your life against something heavy leaning on the other side. That isn't self-control. It's a siege." The Rules (`32:15`) says "You aren't guarding a door", and Now (`33:33`) says "not as a guard posted at the door". The epigraph, Genesis 4:7, was approved by James, so it stays. Only the gloss needs to change. HYDRA-PLAN itself warned that this verse reads as "mastering by effort".

**Fix:** Line 69:
> You are to rule over it. Ruling doesn't mean wrestling him on the doorstep, or leaning on the door all night. Nothing is leaning on it. It means you don't open it, and once you've seen what is outside, you have no wish to.

Line 77: replace "Leave the door shut." with "Don't open the door." Add one line after the James verse, so that "resist" reads as the book's "never answer" and not as a struggle: "To resist him is simply not to answer. He has nothing else."

### H2. The "say No" test is a fresh No every time

**Where:** `22a-whose-voice-is-that.md:7`.

**Problem:** "There's a simple test. Say No, plainly, and listen to what comes back." Four lines later the same chapter says "You have said it once, and outright ... Then never answer again." Do Not Negotiate (`12b:53`) says "Arguing with him is contact ... Even telling him to go away is contact." So the reader is told to answer the caller as a test, in the chapter that tells him never to answer. It also turns the No said once into a No said every time, which is willpower.

**Fix:**
> There's a simple test. Don't argue and don't agree. Just let the thought sit there, and listen to what comes next. The grammar changes.

The rest of the paragraph stands as written ("I could do with it" becomes "Oh, come on, *you* know you want to").

### H3. Let It Ring keeps the tenant alive for life

**Where:** `28a-let-it-ring.md:41`. Echoed in `33-now.md:9` ("He may still telephone. Let it ring.").

**Problem:** "The old tenant isn't dead. He has been put out, and he may telephone now and then for the rest of your life. He can't get back in, unless you go and open the door." Elsewhere the book says the opposite. The Little Monster "starves fast" (Good News, Twice). "The Little Monster gives up once he knows that you won't answer ... and then he stops" (Do Not Negotiate). "If the Little Monster is long dead" (Just Once). "Gone for good" (The First Weeks). "The Little Monster dying" (Isn't it marvelous). A caller you must ignore for the rest of your life is willpower for life. Beware of Cutting Down has just told the reader that is the thing he is escaping.

The pastor review asked for lifelong watchfulness, and that should stay. But watchfulness in this book means knowing that "just once" doesn't exist. It does not mean a tenant ringing for forty years.

**Fix:** At 28a:41, keep the lifelong rule and replace the three tenant sentences with:
> The Little Monster starves and stops calling. But years from now, on some odd evening, an old thought may drift past, the way you still sometimes reach for the light switch in a house you moved out of long ago. Nothing is behind it. It is only the memory of a call. Notice it, smile, and don't pick up. "Just once" is the one thing that could put a tenant back in the room.

At 33:9, replace "He may still telephone. Let it ring." with "If an old thought ever drifts past, you know what it is. Let it go by."

### H4. "A man who can want her and wait"

**Where:** `14f-but-im-going-to-marry-her.md:17`, `:19` and `:23`; `01c-where-the-line-is.md:37`.

**Problem:** This is the one place where the method has to be argued, not assumed, because sex with the woman he will marry is a real good. The Easy Way frame is that nothing is lost: the wedding night is kept, not taken from him. What he stops is the hit now. As written, the chapter praises endurance. "In that year she will need a man who can want her and wait." "I want to be a man who can wait for you." "He will be bringing her a man who can wait. Every wife needs one of those." Each of these tells him he is holding back a desire he still has, and that married life will ask the same of him.

Separately, line 23 ("Make it, and nothing in this chapter applies to you ever again") is false by the book's own account. At Least I'm Not Him (`14c:27`) warns about "the husband who believes he's safe because he only ever pictures his wife", and the chapter's own first half is about using her photograph as material.

**Fix:**
- 14f:17: "In that year she will need a man whose desire for her doesn't turn into a demand. He can show her now that she is getting one. No ring says it half as well."
- 14f:19: "I want you more than I can say, and I'm glad to keep it for our wedding."
- 14f:23: "Make it, and the bed is yours to share. What the first half of this chapter says about using her still holds, because lust doesn't stop at a wedding."
- 01c:37: "Until then, waiting is the kindest thing he can do for her, and it costs him nothing he won't have. He will be bringing her a man who doesn't need to take. Every wife needs one of those."

### H5. "An empty room doesn't stay empty for long"

**Where:** `33-now.md:31`.

**Problem:** After The Empty House, this sentence reads as Jesus's warning applied to the reader: fill the room, or the seven come back. That is the substitute logic that Don't Swap It for Something Else rules out ("nothing is missing", "The habit made the void. It never filled it"). It also contradicts The Empty House's own answer, "there is no vacancy". The rest of the section, God first and the people in front of you, is right.

**Fix:**
> Something has changed. You have your space back: the hours, the energy and the attention that the habit used to eat. The room was never his, and it isn't empty. It's yours. Spend it on whatever you like.

Optional, for the Christian strand in The Empty House (`01b:31`): add "He doesn't come in as a substitute. He was the owner all along." Then nobody reads God as the thing that fills the gap.

### M1. When does the fifth mouth stop: today, or at the final step?

**Where:** `00-introduction.md:7` ("If lust has you in an affair, or using a woman ... stop that today"); `01b:49` ("this book asks you not to throw anything out yet"); `14f:25` ("You start from today, like every other man in these pages").

**Problem:** With "take" now a mouth, a reader sleeping with his girlfriend can't tell which rule is his. "Every other man" starts at the final step, not today.

**Fix:** In the Introduction, make the exception explicit:
> If lust has you in an affair, paying for sex, pressing a woman who has said no, or doing anything against the law, stop that today. Someone else is paying for it, and you don't need the last page for that. Everything else ends at the final step, with no campaign before it.

At 14f:25, use "You start from the same place as every other man in these pages."

### M2. "Unbearable" in the chapter that says it isn't

**Where:** `12b-do-not-negotiate.md:38`.

**Problem:** "It will feel unbearable for a few days, and it is only the itch." Three paragraphs later the same chapter says "Nobody ever went back because the itch was unbearable. It isn't."

**Fix:** Change it to "It will feel hard for a few days. That is only the itch, and it passes." Optionally add, to tie it to the Introduction's exception: "This is the one thing in the book that can't wait for the last page, because she is the one paying for it."

### M3. "Take it prisoner" and "say No to the first thought"

**Where:** `15b-you-would-if-you-could.md:51` and `:59`.

**Problem:** The gloss on 2 Corinthians 10:5 is a soldier's effort: "You take it prisoner. You decide where it goes." Then the testimony makes it a No said to every first thought. The section's own conclusion is the Easy Way one: "Let the bird fly over. Don't build it a nest ... the bird comes, finds nowhere to land, and flies on." The start of the section should match that ending.

**Fix:** Line 51, replace the last two sentences with: "A thought turns up at the gate. It doesn't get to wander where it likes. It is seen for what it is, and it goes no further." Line 59, after the quotation, add: "Notice that he isn't fighting the thought. He simply doesn't invite it in."

### M4. The Lies About Porn and About Women carries a sermon

**Where:** `04b-the-lies-about-porn-and-about-women.md:23` and `:27`.

**Problem:** Since the first check, this chapter has gained an abortion line ("if a child comes anyway, the child's life can be ended"), the Louise Perry paragraph, and "You helped to make the very woman you say you would never marry." They land before the reader has decided, which is the point where Chapter 1 says guilt "loads the gun". The abortion line answers a debate that isn't the reader's lie about lust. "You helped to make" accuses instead of exposing. The closing "None of this is here to add to your guilt" is now carrying too much weight.

**Fix:** Cut the abortion sentence from line 23. Cut the last two sentences of line 27 ("If you slept with her and left ... never marry."). The Perry paragraph can stay as evidence if the Author wants it, but it is the next thing to trim.

### M5. One look, and the whole of David's ruin

**Where:** `23-just-one-little-peek.md:20` ("The bill kept coming ...").

**Problem:** The method chapter on the little look now spends its longest paragraph on Amnon, Absalom and a lost kingdom, ending "One look cost him the peace of his kingdom for the rest of his life." That is a fear tactic, and the Warning chapter promises none. It is also the fourth David passage (In Bed with the Phone, A City Without Walls, this chapter, Fit for Service). The poisoned-cookie paragraph that follows is the real point.

**Fix:** Cut line 20. Line 18 already says what the look cost him.

### M6. The Customer: a list of risks

**Where:** `14e-the-collector-and-the-customer.md:31`; also `:27`, carried over from the first check.

**Problem:** "He can't know what he is carrying home ... In many places he is also committing a crime, and one arrest can cost him his job and his name." This is a list of risks, the kind Both Sides of the Rope says has never made a man stop. "He can't know whether she chose this" is the illusion-breaker, and it should stay. Line 27, "If you had lived as though the Christian rules were true ...", is still the apologetic aside the first check suggested cutting.

**Fix:** Keep the trafficking sentences. Cut "He can't know what he is carrying home to the woman he lives with" and the arrest sentence. The STI advice already sits in the right place, in Who Needs to Know. Cut line 27.

### L1. The dog and the grocery list

`01c-where-the-line-is.md:25`: "Nobody lets the dog write the grocery list." It pictures a beast in you that must be kept on a leash for life. The point of the paragraph is only *when* the line gets drawn. Suggested: "Nobody should write his grocery list while he's starving."

### L2. Instruction one still lists three faces

`21-the-easy-way-to-stop.md:9`: "the porn, the hand and the chase". Suggested: "Decide that you are finished with lust, all five mouths of it. Never again."

### L3. Seven and five

`01b-the-empty-house.md:33`: "Jesus said seven came back ... you will find five." Mixing the two numbers invites the question of what the other two are. Suggested: "Jesus's story ends with the house full. Count the ways your own tenant is fed, and you will find five."

### L4. His word as the restraint

`26-should-i-avoid-temptation.md:33`: "A man who'd have to smash his own word will usually wait, and while he waits the urge passes." Here his word does the holding back. Suggested: "A man with no hatch has nothing to bargain with, and the urge passes."

### Outside the method (for the pastor reviewer)

`14d:13` quotes Mark Driscoll, "If you tolerate, they will dominate." He is a contested figure, and out of context the line reads as a rule about women in general. It is not a method problem: the chapter's point, that the lever stops working, is sound. Flagged so the pastor rerun can weigh it.

The line (Where the Line Is) does not turn into a willpower vow. It is drawn as a map, and the chapter ends "don't try to drag yourself across the line tonight by force. That is sweeping the house again." The only leak is the "man who can wait" wording (H4) and the dog (L1).

The enemy stays small. "Pipsqueak", "mosquito bite" and "a tag scratching at the back of your collar" do that job, apart from H3, where a tenant who calls for forty years is not small.
