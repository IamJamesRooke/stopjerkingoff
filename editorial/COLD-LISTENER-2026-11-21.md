# Cold listener report 2

Source read: `book-swept.txt`, all 3,904 lines, in order. The Introduction is not in that bundle (index.Rmd only pulls it in as a child), so I also read `chapters/00-introduction.md` from the project to check what a listener has heard before chapter 1.

Chapter numbers below are the spoken numbers ("# 8. Crossing the Red Line"); FILE names are the markers.

## Overall verdict

The "porn or masturbation?" problem is largely fixed. From chapter 1 to chapter 30 the text nearly always says which one it means, and the Back Door chapter (33) handles the "I filed all that under porn" reader head-on. The room sequence 33 to 41 is the best-built part of the book.

What still makes a cold listener stumble is different now:

1. **Later-room material leaks into the first half.** Women, "the chase" and casual sex are argued in chapters 8, 11, 17 and 28, long before their rooms. This is now the main source of "what are we talking about?".
2. **An unnamed "her".** A woman the listener has never met keeps appearing in lists ("a message to her").
3. **Echoes.** Several arguments and stories are told three or four times, mostly in the back third (chapters 43 to 61), which was clearly edited less than the front.
4. **One leftover from the older, masturbation-only book**: chapter 61 asks "What about porn?" as if porn had not been the subject since page one.

Ch 43: "Lust is doing you no good in any room of the house, so the right time is now. But you have carried it a long time". Ch 53 point 2: "You don't need any of it." Ch 22 last line: "the advantages of the habit" (and retitle ch 23 to match, or change ch 22's "what the habit costs" to "what lust costs").

## 6. WHERE I WANTED TO STOP

- **Chapters 43 to 46 (Timing, Will I Miss It, Compartmentalize, Wrong Reasons).** After the rooms end at 41 and the Locked Door at 42, the listener is ready to decide. Instead there are four chapters that mostly replay earlier material (timing trap from ch 5, envy from ch 30, the private cast from ch 35, counters and superpowers from ch 10 and 25). This is the longest stretch where nothing new arrives. Chapter 43 in particular has almost no content that is not elsewhere.
- **Chapter 19 (the body).** Honest, but the hedging is said three times ("nobody has proved", "that shows a link and no more", "see a doctor" twice). One hedge paragraph would do.
- **Chapter 11, second half.** Reads as a sermon on the sexual revolution dropped into a book that has not yet earned the right to make it. (See 2.1.)
- **Chapter 38 (Ahab).** The Driscoll line "If you tolerate, they will dominate" and the "lever at home" section put the weight on wives withholding sex; a cold listener, and certainly a wife listening, will hear it as a different book. The pillow joke is good; the route to it is long.
- **Chapters 50 to 53.** Useful, but each restates edging, testing, the scroll and substitutes from earlier chapters. By 53 the listener has heard "it's cutting down" for the fifth time.
- **Chapter 56, middle.** Three scripture quotations and the AA rule in four paragraphs; the non-believer is asked to "change the flag" only after all of it.
- **Chapter 61.** See below.

### Five strongest
1. **Ch 28, Do Not Negotiate / Your own stalker** (`12b`): the freshest idea in the book, and the four instructions are reused correctly afterward. Would be the best chapter with the detour removed.
2. **Ch 35, You Would If You Could** (`15b`): the Friday-night story and "Faithful only because nobody offered" land harder than any argument; the bird and the nest is a usable instruction.
3. **Ch 22, A City Without Walls** (`09`): the Uriah story is told plainly and the "your own word" section turns it on the listener without preaching.
4. **Ch 62, If You Slip** (`29b`): firm and kind at once, and the bleach forum is the funniest thing in the book.
5. **Ch 42, The Locked Door** (`16`): the phone face down on the cushion is the best single image, and it pays off three times later.

Close behind: ch 16 (the itch and the cream), ch 33 (The Back Door), ch 7 (The Little Monster).

### Five weakest
1. **Ch 61, Porn, and Sex** (`29a`): opens on a question the book answered on page one, then repeats chapters 11, 40 and 53. Nothing new.
2. **Ch 43, Timing** (`17`): the answer is "now" and the rest is borrowed from earlier chapters.
3. **Ch 11, second half** (`04b`): right material, wrong place; arrives 28 chapters early and assumes things about the listener.
4. **Ch 38, the Ahab chapter** (`14d`): the story is not about lust until late, and the home section risks reading as blaming wives.
5. **Ch 31, The Ladder** (`14a`): a long list with a title that doesn't match, previews of five later rooms in bullet form, and a hand-off that points at men who aren't in it. In audio a 13-item list is hard to follow.

Also thin: ch 51 (duplicates ch 29), ch 45 (duplicates ch 35), ch 64 (rules 1 and 2).
