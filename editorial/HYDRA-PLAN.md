## ON HOLD: does the Hydra fit, or is there a better biblical image? (v3, 2026-11-20)

James is not sure the Hydra matches the rest of the book and asked for a biblical alternative. No further Hydra edits until he decides.

**Honest assessment of the Hydra.** It gave the book two things it needed: a named list of lust's forms, and a picture of why force fails. But the list does not need a Hydra, and the picture needed two repairs in one evening (what the fire means, and whether the sword makes willpower necessary). An image that needs that much patching is not carrying its own weight. It also adds a sixth world of imagery to a book that already has the Little Monster, the caller, the soldier, the trap and the ship, and it is the only one that is pagan and the only one that makes the enemy big.

**Biblical candidates**

| Image | Fit with the method | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Leviathan, whose heads God broke (Psalm 74:14) | Many heads, and God does the breaking. But it is a vast chaos monster, it is obscure, and nothing in it is about seeing through a lie | No. Bigger than the Hydra, and vaguer |
| The dragon and beast of Revelation | Seven heads, but apocalyptic and disputed | No |
| "Sin crouches at the door" (Genesis 4:7) | One enemy, and it is about mastering by effort | No. James was unconvinced by it once already |
| "Put to death your members" (Colossians 3:5) | A list of five, but "put to death" sounds like willpower | A supporting verse at most |
| The tree and its fruit (Matthew 7:17) | Pick the fruit and the tree bears again; deal with the root | Workable, small, but flat. Nobody remembers it |
| **"You will know the truth, and the truth will make you free" (John 8:32)** | This is the Easy Way in one sentence, and the book already quotes verses 34 and 36 of the same passage | **The method's verse** |
| **The swept and empty house (Matthew 12:43 to 45)** | A spirit is put out of a man, finds the house "empty, swept, and put in order", and comes back with seven worse. "The last state of that man becomes worse than the first." | **Recommended picture** |

**Recommendation: drop the Hydra. Use the house Jesus described.**

Why it fits better than anything else:
- **It is Jesus's own picture of exactly this failure.** A man throws the thing out by force, and it comes back worse. Every reader has lived that.
- **The book is already built in that house.** The Little Monster is already called a tenant who "pays no rent". The book already has the locked door, the back door, the caller at the door and the telephone, and its last chapter already says "an empty room doesn't stay empty for long".
- **Nothing needs patching.** There is no sword, so no question of whether willpower is needed. Throwing the tenant out by force and still believing the room needs him is the Willpower Method. Seeing that he never paid rent and the room was never short of him is the Easy Way. The evicted tenant who keeps telephoning is the caller, so "never answer" follows by itself. For the Christian, the house also gets its rightful owner.
- **The enemy stays small.** He is a tenant, not a dragon.

What stays from today's work whichever image wins: the five in order (look, think, touch, chase, take), the line chapter, the three new chapters and the new running order. In the house version the five are the Little Monster's **five mouths**, and one small belly.

What would change: "The Hydra" is rewritten as a chapter on the empty house and the five mouths, about twelve one-line callbacks are reworded, and "heads" becomes "mouths".

Care needed: the parable speaks of an unclean spirit. The book must use it as Jesus's picture of how a thing returns, and never tell the reader he is possessed. The pastor review checks this.

Runner-up: keep the Hydra as it now stands, in one chapter. It works. It is just the weaker fit.

## Is the Hydra the right image? (v2, 2026-11-20)

James asked whether a fearsome Hydra clashes with a Little Monster we made weak on purpose. It does, if the Hydra becomes the book's main picture. It does not, if the Hydra is used for the one thing it does better than any other image, and then punctured.

**Recommendation: keep the Hydra, in one role.** The Little Monster stays the truth about the enemy. The Hydra is how the enemy *looks* to a man who is fighting it with a sword.

That is honest to what the reader has lived. Lust has felt unbeatable to him. He has cut off a head a hundred times and watched two grow back. The Hydra gives that experience its picture. Then the torch comes, and by its light he sees what the heads grow out of: one small body. Five mouths and one belly. The legend made it huge because the only men who told the story were the ones who had been cutting.

So the rule for the whole book is one sentence: **the Hydra is what it looks like, and the Little Monster is what it is.**

Guards:
- One full telling (the Hydra chapter), one payoff (the last chapter), and about ten one-line callbacks. No more.
- Every time the Hydra appears, it is cut down to size in the same breath.
- The fire means one thing everywhere: the truth about what the head gave you. No "light", no "grace" attached to it (James, 2026-11-20). Burning takes no strength, so the "no willpower" promise holds.
- Hercules did not fetch the fire himself. The book is the reader's Iolaus, and later the reader is the next man's. Grace is taught in the book's own chapters on it, not hung on the myth.

**If James still doubts it, the fallback is the weed and the root.** Mow a weed and it comes back thicker. Pull the root and it is over. It is small, so there is no clash at all, and the Introduction already says the book "goes after the root". It is also less vivid, and nobody remembers a weed. The Hydra is the better hook as long as it keeps to its one role.

| In the myth | In the book |
|---|---|
| The Hydra | How lust looks from outside: one beast, many heads |
| The one small body | The Little Monster, feeding through every head |
| The sword with no fire: cut, and two grow back | The Willpower Method, cutting down, and substitutes |
| One stroke, then fire | The plain No, said once at the final step, after the belief is already dead. It never has to be repeated |
| Why they grow back | The Big Monster: the lie that the head had something to give |
| The fire on the stump | One thing only: the truth about what that head gave you, which is nothing. The belief dies, so the wanting dies, so nothing grows |
| Iolaus | This book, bringing the fire. At the end, the reader brings it to the next man |
| The immortal head under the rock | The voice that is left. See below |

## The immortal head

James's guess is "lust itself". That is right, with one sharpening.

The book promises a man freedom from lust's *hold*. It never promises that the old appetite will fall silent for life. The pastor's review made the same point: watchfulness is lifelong. So:

- The heads that burn are lust's hold on him: the habits and the lies under them.
- The immortal head is lust's *voice*: what the Bible calls the flesh, and what this book calls the caller.

It is buried alive. It can still talk from under the rock. It cannot bite, because it has no body left to feed it. That is "Let It Ring" in one picture. The rock is the man's settled No. For the Christian reader, one line can say whose rock it really is.

Desire for a woman is not a head at all. It was made good, it belongs to the man and not to the beast, and its home is marriage.

## The five heads

James asked for a list that is clean, complete and easy to remember. Seven nouns were none of those. The new list is five verbs, in the order lust climbs:

| 4 | **Chase** | Simping, the pedestal, obsession, flirting, sexting, paying for attention | The Man Led by a Woman, The King, Do Not Negotiate, Ladder bullets | Sexting is thin |
| 5 | **Take** | Sex outside marriage: the hookup, the affair, paid sex, and sleeping with the woman he means to marry | The collector and the married man, Ladder bullets, two sentences in Porn and Sex | Hookups and paid sex are bullets. The fiancee case is missing |

Why this list works:
- **Five, counted on one hand.** A book called "Stop Jerking Off" can use that.
- **It is complete.** Anything lust does is a look, a thought, a touch, a chase or a taking.
- **It is a ladder.** Each head leads to the next one up.
- **It is already in the book.** David on the roof looked, asked about her, sent for her and took her. "A City Without Walls" tells that story. Jesus names the eye, the heart and the hand in one passage (Matthew 5:28 to 30).

James's own case is no longer a head of its own. It is the hardest case under Take: taking before the promise. It still gets its own short chapter and an anonymous man: a woman he plans to marry, a photo she sent, and the thought "this one doesn't count". The chapter answers "shouldn't every man set his own line?" in the book's voice: the Little Monster would love to hold the pen.

## The line, stated once and early

- The biblical ideal: sex belongs inside marriage, and there it is a gift.
- For the reader who is not a Christian, a plain test: is this a gift given inside a promise already made, or a hit you take?
- "A promise already made" matters. Without those words the test lets the fiancee case through.
- Engaged is not married. The book says so kindly and without piling on guilt.

## Quality gate before James rereads (added 2026-11-20)

James asked what would make this version close to perfect before he starts reading. Nothing reaches him until every line below is done and reported.

1. **Quotes verified.** Every quotation checked against its source again, including the two still open: the Luther line about jeering at the devil, and the Dr. K quote near 6:49 of his video. Any quote that can't be confirmed becomes a paraphrase or goes.
2. **Scripture verified.** Every verse checked word for word against the World English Bible.
3. **Myth checked.** The Hydra telling checked against the ancient source (Apollodorus), with the book's own additions marked as its own.
4. **Method check, run again.** The Easy Way fidelity audit on the new version. Special watch: does the Hydra make the enemy feel big, and does "the line" turn into a willpower vow?
5. **Pastor review, run again.** Special watch: the line chapter, the fiancee chapter, grace getting the credit, and the pagan myth.
6. **Simulated readers, run again.** At least: a single man of twenty, a married man, a churchgoer, a non-believer, an engaged man, and a man who only knows one head. Each reports where he stopped believing or stopped reading.
7. **Five-head coverage check.** A table showing, for look, think, touch, chase and take: where it is named, where its lie is exposed, where it is burned, and where the ending pays it off. No blank cells.
8. **Consistency pass.** "It", the Little Monster, the Big Monster, the caller, the Hydra and the torch each mean one thing everywhere. The rationed phrases keep their counts.
9. **Hooks and lengths.** Every chapter between 500 and 1,500 words and ending on a hook that names no chapter. No pointers back or forward.
10. **Read-aloud pass.** The whole book read for the ear, including the four verses that run together in "The Rules in One Place".
11. **Evergreen and tone pass.** No brands, dated slang or rotting links. Women are people in every chapter, most of all in Chase and Take.
12. **Proofread.** Spelling (American), punctuation, no ellipses, and the editorial checks script clean.
13. **Build check.** Site, PDF and EPUB all build, the contents list and numbers are right, old page addresses still work, and the FAQ, About page and site description match the new version.
14. **Change report.** One list of every change to a chapter James has already approved, with before and after.

Items 4 to 6 are done by reviewers who have not seen the plan, so they read it cold.

## Risks

- **The monster gets big.** Answered by the tone guard above.
- **The metaphor gets overworked.** Limit: two full tellings (The Hydra, Now) and about ten one-line callbacks. The Little Monster, the caller and the soldier stay the main images.
- **A pagan myth in a Christian book.** It is told as a story, explained in place, in one paragraph. Scripture still carries the authority.
- **The fiancee chapter costs readers.** Some men will stop reading at "engaged is not married". The chapter must be warm, short and free of scolding, and the gift-or-hit test gives the non-Christian a way in.
- **Renumbering again.** Five new chapters move every number after chapter 1. Page addresses do not change.
- **Length.** Net growth of about 5 percent. Each new chapter must pass the 2+2=4 test.
