## 1. The book in one paragraph

An anonymous, Christian-scaffolded self-help book for men, in the spirit of EasyPeasy (itself built on Allen Carr). Thesis: lust gives a man nothing, so once he sees that, stopping is easy and needs no willpower. Lust has three faces: porn, the hand, and bad decisions with women ("thinking with the wrong head"). Frame: a man ruled by lust is a soldier who shows up drunk for duty. He is loved and he is unfit. The book gets him fit. Method: read in order, carry on as normal until the end, then pray the prayer in Ch 28. Free to read, CC BY-SA 4.0. About 69,000 prose words; Ch 8, 31, 5, 14, 11 and 29 are the longest.

## 2. Voice and style rules

- Second person, warm, confident, repetitive on purpose (EasyPeasy device). Short paragraphs. Slogans in CAPS. The odd joke. British spelling (colour, realise, grey, fortnight); the Bible and quoted people stay as written.
- Plain language: one idea per sentence, split anything over about 25 words (quotations and verses excepted), everyday words.
- "I" is for opinions and asides only. No invented autobiography. Stories about people are told about "a man", true on material facts; readable beats hyper-accurate.
- The book is ANONYMOUS. No author name, no pen name, no "I was caught in this". The only credit is CC BY-SA to EasyPeasy (its author is credited as "Hackauthor²" on the Attribution page and in Ch 34; that is EasyPeasy's credit, not ours).
- Scripture: World English Bible, verbatim. Never trim or reword a verse. Other quotes verbatim and short; anything unverifiable becomes a paraphrase. Sources go in each chapter's `<!-- SOURCES -->` comment.
- Named in the body: Bible figures, Augustine, Owen, Chalmers, Lewis, Pascal, Piper, Lambert, Challies, Dobson, Maltz, Bonhoeffer. Everyone else is "one coach", "a pastor", "a psychiatrist who treats porn addiction", "a man on the NoFap forum". Names go in SOURCES, the Appendix and Attribution.
- "Masturbation" is the normal word; "the habit" means masturbation with or without porn; say "lust" when the claim covers all three faces. "Jerking off" is rare and on purpose (title, Ch 10 joke, Intro, Ch 3, Ch 8, Ch 24).
- Respect for women: the fault is the man's thirst, never the woman. "Simping" appears once (Intro).
- No medical claims. Harm is conviction and testimony. No invented studies, stats or quotes. No superpowers, no Karezza.
- "If you're an atheist, play along" appears only in the Intro and the Ch 33 blessing. Elsewhere "if you're not a believer".
- "Carry on as normal" is about private habits. It never permits hurting someone (affair, using a woman, anything illegal).
- Guilt is not the engine. Say the truth about sin, then give grace in the same breath. "Unfit" never means "unloved".
- Never call this book a sequel to EasyPeasy or its "spiritual successor": it was "inspired by" it. Never call this book free.

A short line or fragment may stand as its own paragraph where it carries the point ("Not one." in Chapter 1). About once or twice a chapter, never stacked. ### Never send the reader back or forward (James, 2026-11-18)

Never tell the reader to go and find something: no "read the verse at the head of the chapter again", "see above", "below", "scroll up". Nobody scrolls back, and a listener can't. Restate the line in place, in a few words or in full. Naming a chapter in passing ("Chapter 8 described this") is fine when the sentence still works without looking it up. Advice to re-read the book or a chapter after a slip is part of the method and stays.

## 3. Key terms

- **Lust**: desire turned into taking. Desire is God's idea and good (noticing beauty, wanting a wife). Lust is the second look, the woman used as material.
- **Three faces / doors / outlets**: porn, the hand, the chase (bad decisions with women). The term is "faces" (Ch 3 says "three faces" and keeps "three doors" as an image only).
- **The habit**: masturbation, with or without porn. **MO / PMO**: defined in the Intro.
- **Little Monster**: the faint restless itch ("go on, just do it / look / text her"). It is small and starves fast. **Big Monster**: the lies in your head that make the itch seem important. Kill the Big Monster and the Little one starves. The Little Monster is an itch and can't talk; any "voice" is the Big Monster speaking for it (Appendix, fixed 2026-11-17).
- **The flesh** (Ch 3): the part of a man that wants what it wants, now, for itself. **The world's lies** (Ch 4): the second cause.
- **Willpower Method** (Ch 11, capitalised): any way of quitting that leaves you feeling you sacrificed something. Includes counters, streaks, rations, badges.
- **Ration / cutting down**: any "only on Saturdays / only no screen" deal. Always rejected.
- **Substitute**: anything pushed into the "gap". Always rejected. There is no gap.
- **Just once**: does not exist. It is a chain.
- **The thread**: "A man who can't control his lust can't be trusted." Self-control is a fruit, not a clenched fist.
- **Fit for service / drunk soldier**: loved, enlisted, unfit. Threaded 2026-11-17: Intro, Ch 3 (orders from the bottle), 8 (Nehemiah's armed builders), 10 (only dry), 12 (drunk on duty "just once"), 13 (reporting sick), 20 (sober on the wall), 21 (sobering up), 26 (bridge), 26b, 27 prayer, 28 (fit for duty), 28b (falls back in), 31 #11, 32 (closed).
- **Vow vs decision** (Ch 1, 21, 26, 32): the willpower vow made before understanding is out; the decision at the end with a prayer is in; plain sense (phone out of the bedroom) is not a gimmick and need not wait for the last page.
- **Lever**: lust gives others a handle on you (Ch 8).
- **Moment of revelation**: the day the weight lifts (Ch 27). **Flatline**: the temporary flat spell (Ch 22).
- **"YES! I'M FREE!"**: the book's cry. **"Isn't it marvellous?"**: the first-weeks attitude.
- **The final step**: a prayer of confession, not a last session (Ch 28).
