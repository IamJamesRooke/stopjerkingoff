# Opening rewrite, 2026-11-21

James: the first five chapters must be perfect, gripping and quick to reward, with the pill in the meat. This is what changed in the Introduction and chapters 1 to 5. The old versions are kept whole in editorial/PARKING.md.

## How it was tested

The new opening was read cold by three test readers: a 24-year-old who is not religious, a 38-year-old Christian husband listening on his commute, and a nonfiction editor. All three were hooked by the first page and all three named the same best line ("You are a champion at stopping. The real question is why you start again."). They scored the Introduction and chapter 1 at 4.5 to 5 out of 5. They also agreed on the weak spots, and those were fixed before publishing: chapter 2 turned into front matter halfway through, the cautions in the Introduction still read as small print, "don't quit yet" was said four times, a "he" in The Empty House read as God, and no "benefit" had actually been taken apart by the end of chapter 3.

## Introduction (1,256 words before, about 810 now)

- Before, it opened: "This book will help you conquer lust. You will stop masturbating, stop watching porn..."
- Now it opens on a scene: "It is two in the morning, and a man is lying in bed with his phone. He isn't enjoying himself. He stopped enjoying this years ago."
- The scene runs to "never again", widens to the other versions (the message rewritten five times, the money, the bed), and lands on "There is no dragon." The squatter now bangs on the floor when he is hungry.
- The promise paragraph, the grit paragraph and two testimonies follow under "What this book will do".
- "Two rules while you read": do not skip chapters, and don't try to quit while you read. The harm caveat ("stop that today") and the get-help note stay, in two short paragraphs. The honest line that the method has not been tested in a clinic stays.
- Cut and parked: the "Is this you?" questions, the bulleted safety list, "One appetite, fed five ways" (chapter 4 does this job), the staircase, the five-point promise list, one testimony.
- New ending: "Now go back to the man in the bed, and put one thing on his nightstand. A button."
- The homepage line under the cover is now the first sentence of the scene.

## Chapter 1, now called The Button (was A Warning; the web address is unchanged)

- Opens with the button, with jokes added: "He would press it hard enough to break the nightstand." "Nobody would press a button to be rid of fishing."
- "You never chose this" is kept, shortened.
- New section, "You already know how to stop". This is the first aha, moved up from chapter 3: you stop every time you finish, so stopping was never the problem. "You are a champion at stopping. The real question is why you start again." The nineteen-year-old's quote moved here with it.
- "What brings you back": FEAR, the list of lines the squatter wrote, no scare tactics, no superpowers.
- "Nothing to lose": question everything, YOU HAVE NOTHING TO LOSE, and a new line for the reader who wants to put the book down: "That flutter is only the squatter. He has noticed what you're reading." The prodigal son stays.
- Cut and parked: the "two ways to fail" frame (its content is in the sections above).

## Chapter 2, Can You Be Trusted? (899 words before, about 680 now)

- The monk, the drunk soldier and the trust sentence are unchanged. Added: "You don't need that proved to you. Ask the man who said 'never again' last night." And: "A grown man, trusted with a job and a family, can't keep his word against a squatter the size of a house cat. That ought to annoy you. Good. Be annoyed at the right party."
- The faith note is one section, and it is honest up front: "A Christian wrote this book, and it quotes the Bible, because the best pictures of this trap are in there. You don't have to believe a word of it to get out."
- Matthew 5:28, lust is not desire, and Romans 8:1 stay. The woman section is three sentences. The EasyPeasy and Allen Carr credit is one short paragraph.
- Cut and parked: "About this book" (length, read it twice, pass it on), the three definitions, the license sentence (it is on the Attribution page), John 8:36.
- New ending: "It begins with the strangest promise anyone has made you about this. It will be easy."

## Chapter 3, The Easy Method

- Opens on the mountain and the all-clear, where it used to open on "This book has one aim".
- The reasons the normal way is hard are now two (the third moved to chapter 1).
- New section, a sample proof so the reader isn't asked to take "no benefits" on trust: "It helps me sleep". "Ask who was keeping you awake. You slept like a stone when you were nine." "So the habit didn't give you sleep. It took your sleep, and every night it rents a little of it back to you. Then you thank it." It ends on the man who wears tight shoes for the pleasure of taking them off.
- "What to do while you read" says the new thing only (watch), and the plain No against the old vow.
- "A man who tries to quit out of desperation is doomed to fail" became "is working against himself", because the same page says a few men do get free the hard way.
- New ending, which now leads into The Empty House: "So start with the squatter. If he does nothing for you, why does he keep coming back? The best answer anyone has given is two thousand years old. It is a story about a house."

## Chapter 4, The Empty House

- "Now look at how he lives" became "Now look at how the squatter lives" (it followed the paragraph about the owner and read as God).
- "A tenant who pays nothing isn't a tenant. He is a squatter." became "You have been treating him as a tenant, and a tenant who pays nothing is only a squatter."
- One repeat of the carried plate and one "grubby" removed.

## Chapter 5, Where the Line Is

- "The chase is a man wanting something from a woman" became "a man working a woman for what he can get" (the old wording also condemned courting a wife).
- "He is the only one in this book who can move the line himself" became "who can cross the line honestly" (it sounded as though he moves God's line).
- The three closing caveats became one paragraph. The "one kind of man" paragraph is parked.

## Epigraphs

The verses at the top of chapters 3, 4 and 5 were removed, because each verse is quoted in the chapter itself and the readers heard them twice. All three verses are still in the text.

## What did not change

The four method lines, the marriage line, the harm caveat, the get-help note, the woman section, the EasyPeasy credit, and every quote and verse that stayed are word for word as before.

## Left for James

Two things the readers raised that are house rules, so nothing was changed: "no benefits, not one" is a hard claim this early (the sleep sample is there to earn it), and the reader who isn't religious felt the marriage line in chapter 5 arrive suddenly.
