# Voice rules (James, 2026-11-23)

James sent his own draft of part of the Introduction and said: "I'm not the best writer in the world... But notice some things I do." These are his rules. They apply to every chapter.

1. **Contractions.** It's, he's, don't, you've, that's. Leave a phrase uncontracted only when the stress falls on it ("There is no dragon." "It will be easy.").
2. **Write how we speak.** The book has a voice. It is a man talking to another man across a table, not an essay. If you wouldn't say the sentence out loud to a friend, rewrite it.
3. **Repetition on purpose.** Repeat a word or a shape for force ("he bangs and bangs and bangs"; "You don't because... And you don't because..."). Cut repetition that is only saying the same thing twice.
4. **Hyperbole and heat.** Exaggerate for effect and let him be angry at the habit ("Starve the little pest, then throw his ass out!"). Mock the habit and the Little Monster, never the reader and never women. Swearing stops at "ass" unless James says otherwise.
5. **Economy.** Take the shortest way to say it. No throat-clearing, no "this book will", no explaining a picture after showing it. "This isn't an academic piece."
6. **Lists get bullet points** (James: "Lists should have bullet points"). A run of three or more quoted lies, excuses or items goes in a bullet list, one per line, grouped under a short lead-in line if there are kinds. Don't leave them run together in a paragraph.
7. **Flow** (his earlier note). Every paragraph follows from the one before. Each section makes one move. Notes about the book never interrupt an argument. No "So", "And" or "But" opener unless the link is real.

## What a voice pass may and may not change

- May: contract, shorten, cut filler, turn written phrasing into spoken phrasing, sharpen a verb, add a beat of repetition.
- May not: change the argument, facts, figures, quotations, Bible verses (WEB, verbatim), names, studies, section headers, chapter order, image lines, or the last paragraph's hook. No new claims or people. Do not touch a chapter James has locked.
- Fixed picture: the Little Monster is the craving, cat-sized, in the back room upstairs. He only bangs and gets fed. He never speaks. He is starved and thrown out, never fought or killed. What holds the man is "the lies".
- Still in force: American English, no ellipses, no em-dashes, sentences of 25 words at most, dated facts carry their year, the narrator is not a Christian and is never named.
- James's own lines stay as he wrote them, even where they sound rough. If in doubt, leave it.
- Anything cut goes to editorial/PARKING.md with the chapter name.

## Before and after

Before (written):

> A while after the last time, a feeling shows up. It is a faint, fidgety itch, like a phone buzzing in another room. That itch is the craving. It is the whole dragon.
>
> Picture it this way. Your life is a house. In the back room upstairs lives a creature the size of a house cat. He smells like a gym bag left in a hot car. He can't get down the stairs. He eats only what you carry up to him. Every time you give in, to porn or to your own hand, that is a meal. When he gets hungry, he bangs on the floor. That banging is the urge.

After (spoken, built from James's draft):

> That overwhelming urge when temptation hits? That faint, fidgety itch, that craving? It's no dragon.
>
> It's a little monster living in the back room upstairs. A tiny pipsqueak, the size of a house cat, who smells like cheese left to rot in a car in midsummer. He doesn't pay rent. He doesn't even buy his own food, because you're the one who feeds him.
>
> When he's hungry he bangs and bangs and bangs on the floor until you carry his dinner up. Porn is a meal. Your hand is a meal. Then he's full, he settles down, and he leaves you alone.

What changed: the picture is no longer announced ("Picture it this way"), the sentences are things a man would say, the jokes do the describing, and the repetition carries the nagging.
