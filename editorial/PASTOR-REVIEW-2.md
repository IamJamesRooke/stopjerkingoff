# Pastor Review 2: the restructured book

Second clergy read, done cold on main after the restructure (63 chapter files, the Empty House and the five mouths). Read 2026-11-20 as a careful Catholic priest and a careful evangelical pastor would read it. Read only: no chapter was edited. All fixes go through the Author.

The first review's fixes are still in place: the grace framing, the Catholic lines on Confession, "the LORD", the Augustine story, Nathan and Psalm 51, the scrupulosity paragraph, and "if you're ready now, don't wait for my permission".

## Scripture check

Every Bible quotation was checked word for word against the World English Bible: the 43 epigraphs plus 29 quotations in the running text. **All match.** The only differences are "the LORD" for "Yahweh", which the Attribution page discloses.

One count is wrong (see M1). In Jesus's story it is the one spirit that comes back, bringing seven others. The book says "Jesus said seven came back."

## The three checks the coordinator asked for

**1. The unclean-spirit parable (Matthew 12:43-45). Passes, with one fix.** The disclaimer is clear and good: "the story isn't here to tell you that you have one." A Catholic or evangelical reader will accept it as an application. Two things will catch a pastor's eye. The book says Jesus described a man getting rid of evil "by force", and that isn't in the text. And insight is made the thing that fills the house ("there is no vacancy"), while God, the owner, is only an add-on for Christians. In Jesus's story, the house is lost because it is *empty*. Fix in M1.

**2. John 8:32 with "remain in my word". Passes.** The Empty House says "He was speaking of his own teaching, and a Christian will take it that way." That is honest. The fuller context is given where the verse is the epigraph. Optional: quote verse 31 once in the Empty House ("If you remain in my word, then you are truly my disciples").

**3. Sex belongs in marriage, including the engaged man. The teaching is right, but two places leak.** "Where the Line Is", "But I'm Going to Marry Her" and "Porn, and Sex" all teach it plainly and kindly. "If you're single, the answer is to wait" is clear. But the end of "Where the Line Is" (H1) and the Final Step's "If there is another person" (H2) can be read as letting a man carry on sleeping with someone until the book is finished. Some smaller phrases blur the line too (M4, M5, M6).

## HIGH

### H1. Where the Line Is: "don't drag yourself across the line tonight"

> "And don't try to drag yourself across the line tonight by force. That is sweeping the house again. For now it's enough to know where the line is. Keep reading."

This comes straight after the engaged man and the "take" mouth. It is right for the habit a man keeps alone. But a man sleeping with his girlfriend or his fiancee will read it as "carry on until the last page." Every pastor will stop here. The "Do I have to stop while I'm reading?" answer says "nobody is telling you to sin", but it is about willpower campaigns, not about another person.

**Suggested wording** (add after "Keep reading."):

> One exception. All of this is about the habit you keep by yourself. If another person is in it, that's different. You're not sweeping a house there. You're making a choice about someone you care about, and you can stop making it today.

### H2. The Final Step: "If there is another person" (James's standing rule, flagged only)

> "Make sure that nothing you are doing breaks the law, and that nothing you are doing is harming another person."

James decided the book won't tell a cheating man what to do, and this review respects that. Flagged only because clergy will notice that the prayer says "I am done with using a woman", while the instruction just before it asks only about the law and harm. An affair harms a wife, so the sentence already covers it, and the reader may not see that.

**Optional wording, keeping James's rule:** "...and that nothing you are doing is harming another person. A wife who trusts you counts." Or leave it as it stands.

## MEDIUM

### M1. The Empty House: "by force", "seven came back", and who fills the house

- "Jesus once described what happens when a man gets rid of something evil by force and stops there." Jesus doesn't say "by force". He told the story about "this evil generation", not about the method.
  - **Suggested:** "Jesus once told a story about a house that was cleaned out and then left empty."
- "Jesus said seven came back." **Suggested:** "In Jesus's story, the spirit came back with seven others. Count the ways your own tenant is fed, and you will find five."
- "Once a man has seen that, there is no vacancy." To a pastor, that makes the truth about the habit fill the house, when Jesus's point was that the house must not stand empty. The Christian line just after it carries the real answer, so give it full weight.
  - **Suggested** in place of "A Christian will add one thing...": "A Christian will say more. Jesus's story is about a house left empty, and a house isn't meant to stand empty. It has an owner, and he means to live in it. Knowing the truth puts the tenant out. The owner is what keeps the house full."
- Pentecostal and charismatic readers may believe the habit can involve a spirit. The disclaimer doesn't mock that, which is right. No change needed.

### M2. If You Slip and the FAQ: "The sin was never the noticing. It was the taking."

Jesus put the sin at the look (Matthew 5:28), and the book's own first mouth includes "the second glance... the one you take on purpose." "The taking" is also the name of the fifth mouth, so a reader may hear that only sex outside marriage counts.

**Suggested:** "The sin was never the noticing. It was the second look, the one you take on purpose." Make the same change in the FAQ answer "Is masturbation a sin?"

### M3. For the People Beside Him: "never a debt he can collect"

> "Paul tells husbands and wives not to deprive each other... But that is a gift between two people. It is never a debt he can collect"

Paul's own word is that each "owes" the other (1 Corinthians 7:3, "the affection owed her"). Catholic teaching even calls it the marriage debt. The pastoral point is right: no pressure, least of all after a betrayal. But a pastor will say the sentence contradicts the verse it cites.

**Suggested:** "Paul does say husband and wife owe each other this, and he says it to both of them alike. But he never gives a husband the right to take it, least of all after he has broken your trust."

### M4. "Partner" and "the woman he lives with"

"Partner" appears about 25 times (the Introduction, The Back Door, Fit for Service, Should I Avoid Temptation, The Men Still on the Ship, Now, Why This Book Exists) and "the woman he lives with" once (the man led by a woman). The book says sex belongs in marriage, and then keeps using the word the culture uses for living together. Clergy will read it as quiet approval of living together.

**Suggested:** use "wife" wherever the man is married, and "the woman in your life" or "the woman you'll marry" where he isn't. "Why This Book Exists" has "your partner or the woman you'll marry". Make that "your wife or the woman you'll marry".

### M5. "A bed that isn't yours" (Where the Line Is, and Rule 10)

An unmarried man may think his girlfriend's bed, or his own, is "his". **Suggested** for Rule 10: "and not to the chase or any bed outside marriage." In "Where the Line Is", "any bed outside marriage" also reads well.

### M6. Where the Line Is: three phrases a priest would query

- "what a husband and wife enjoy with each other is on the right side of the line." A Catholic will ask about porn watched together, and some evangelicals will too. **Suggested:** "and a husband and wife who give themselves to each other are on the right side of the line."
- "A promise has a date on it, and witnesses." An engagement has a date too. **Suggested:** "A promise is made, not planned. An engagement is a promise to make one."
- "it takes about twenty minutes and a ring." This sounds like a courthouse and skips the preparation the Catholic Church requires. **Suggested:** "it takes a ring and a wedding, and the wedding can be small."

### M7. Chapter 13: the devil "sends the Little Monster", and half of James 4:7

"Fit for Service" balances the devil with James 1:14 ("drawn away by his own lust"). Chapter 13 doesn't, and it quotes only "Resist the devil, and he will flee from you." The verse begins "Be subject therefore to God."

**Suggested:** quote the whole verse ("Be subject therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you."). Add one line: "He sends it, but it only works because the appetite is yours."

### M8. Sex therapist exercises (What It's Doing to You, and What Your Body Will Do)

"If a sex therapist gives you exercises, that is treatment, so follow it." Some standard exercises involve masturbation. A Catholic, and many evangelicals, will object to the book telling a man to follow them without saying anything about it.

**Suggested:** "If a sex therapist gives you exercises, tell him your convictions at the start. A good one will work within them."

### M9. The abortion line needs one line of mercy

> "She was told that if a child comes anyway, the child's life can be ended, as though that would cost her nothing."

This is true and stated plainly. But many male readers drove a girlfriend to that clinic or paid for it. Every pastor who has sat with those men will want one line of mercy here. **Suggested** (after "what she was worth"): "If you were part of a choice like that, it is forgiven the same way as everything else in this book. The grief is real, and there is help for it."

## LOW

- **If You Slip, the penance line.** "It's medicine for the soul, and it adds nothing to the bill." A Catholic theologian would say penance is a real satisfaction, though it never pays for forgiveness. **Suggested:** "It's medicine for the soul. It doesn't pay for your forgiveness. Christ did that."
- **The Back Door.** "All things are lawful for me" was a slogan from the people in Corinth, which Paul quotes in order to correct it. Say so in one clause, or a reader will think Paul taught it: "Some in Corinth had a slogan, 'All things are lawful for me.' Paul answered it: 'but I will not be brought under the power of anything.'"
- **The Final Step, the prodigal paragraph.** "The Father answers with two words. 'But God, I still smell like yesterday. Just come.'" Read aloud, the son's line and the Father's two words run together. In Luke the father runs to his son before the son has finished, and he never says "just come". Make it clear that this is the preacher's picture: "In his picture, the son protests, 'But God, I still smell like yesterday.' And the Father answers with two words: 'Just come.'"
- **Just One Little Look.** "One look cost him the peace of his kingdom." David's look led to adultery and murder. **Suggested:** "One look, and what he did next, cost him the peace of his kingdom."
- **Samson.** "The strongest man who ever lived" is said three times. **Suggested:** "the strongest man in the Bible."
- **The man led by a woman, Mark Driscoll.** James decided to keep him, so this is flagged only. Some clergy will react to the name. He is also missing from the list of names on the Attribution page.
- **The lever at home.** James loved this passage, so leave it. A pastor would welcome one sentence asking the husband to look at himself first ("husbands, love your wives, even as Christ also loved the assembly", Ephesians 5:25), and "wife" in place of "partner" (M4).
- **But I'm Going to Marry Her.** "Make it, and nothing in this chapter applies to you ever again" overstates a little. Optional: "and this chapter's question is answered."
- **The scrupulosity paragraph.** For Catholics, add: "If you're Catholic, tell your confessor. Priests know this well."
- **About page.** It says "about 35 short chapters". The book now has many more. This isn't a pastoral issue.

## Strengths a pastor will praise

- **The Collector and the Customer.** It treats the woman as a person with a story, and the child as a human life. The "would you say this under Christian rules?" test is excellent.
- **But I'm Going to Marry Her.** Firm on the line and gentle with the man who has already crossed it. This is the chapter clergy will most want to hand to engaged couples.
- **Where the Line Is.** "Nobody lets the dog write the grocery list" makes the case for a line outside yourself better than most sermons do.
- **The Final Step.** The prayer is sound on confession, grace and dependence ("I can't keep this by my own strength"). The Catholic note on Confession is accurate.
- **If You Slip, "Wanting it to hurt".** This is good teaching on grace against self-punishment, and "repentance is quick and it faces forward" is quotable.
- **What Your Body Will Do.** It says wet dreams are not sin. That agrees with Catholic moral teaching and with evangelical pastoral practice.
- **Who Needs to Know, and To wives.** The book calls the hidden habit a betrayal, gives her the pace, and sends both of them for testing after an affair. A pastor would preach the same.
- **Fit for Service.** Satan is not given the blame (James 1:14). Isaiah 6 is used faithfully, with forgiveness coming before the sending.
- **Ahab and Jezebel, Joseph, David and Uriah, Peter (Luke 22:32), and Job 31:1.** Each is told accurately and in context.
- **Now.** The suggestion to read Mark, and the prayer "God, if you're there, show me", are respectful invitations to non-Christians, not pressure.
