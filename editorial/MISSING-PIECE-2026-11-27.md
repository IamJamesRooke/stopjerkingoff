# What's missing after chapter 10 (reader check, 2026-11-27)

James, after reading through chapter 10: "can't help but feel something is missing." Three cold readers (Sonnet) read the Introduction through But It Feels Good and answered one question: what is still missing before a man could say No?

Readers: Tyler, 22, daily for 8 years, failed streaks; Mark, 41, divorced atheist who quit smoking with Carr's book; Dave, 38, married Christian, confessed to his wife twice and relapsed twice.

## Shared by all three
1. **No felt proof.** The book gives pictures (shoes, alarm, eggs) but never a small thing a man can do tonight to feel the monster is small. Carr builds momentum by letting the reader feel the illusion lift; here the reader takes notes. DONE (in a chapter ahead of James): Good News, Twice now has "Try it tonight".

## Shared by two
2. **The feared Friday night is never repainted** (Tyler, Mark). The Button names the fear; no chapter shows the free man's ordinary evening. DONE: "A Friday night, later" in Good News, Twice.
3. **Repetition and a slow middle** (Tyler, Mark). The house/rent/plate picture is re-explained in the Intro, Empty House, Difficult, Master and Little Monster; The Flesh's tour of the other six sins runs long before lust. PARKED for the end-of-book pass (The Flesh is read and was rebuilt by the Author on 11-27).
4. **The single man** (Tyler, Mark). "Sex with your wife is the meal" leaves the single or divorced man asking what his life is meanwhile, and the young man's real fear ("I'm broken for real women", the narrator's own "I couldn't finish with her") isn't answered by chapter 10. Check whether later chapters (Where the Line Is, If You Never Marry, the death-grip passage) answer it, and whether a promise should come earlier.

## One reader, worth weighing
5. **Pleasure conceded, then deferred** (Mark). "No benefits. Not one." (The Easy Method) vs "It feels incredible" (But It Feels Good), with "where that pleasure belongs is a question for later". He wants the rush shown to depend on the itch, in place.
6. **Grace after relapsing after a confession** (Dave). "God has given up on me" is his real lie; the prodigal son paragraph is short. Also: why accountability software, men's groups and prayer failed him (they aren't swords), and what a third confession to his wife should be. Check later chapters (If You Slip, To Wives, The Willpower Method).
7. **The narrator's story ends in failure** (Tyler). "Kept me clean for almost a year. I went back." A reader wonders why to trust him. (Constraint: the book must not claim he is free until James is.)
8. **Day-90 relapse autopsy and the NoFap benefits** (Tyler): walk one relapse through minute by minute naming each lie; say honestly why reported benefits aren't the habit giving anything.
9. **Master vs pipsqueak** (Mark): still reads as a clash in places; the fix line ("the lies made a pipsqueak look like a king") is in A Master, Not a Habit.

## Parked from the chapter 15-21 panel (2026-11-27)
- Why Are You Doing That?: readers felt the woman's selfie gets more scorn than the gym-mirror man, and "Find a nice guy. Have a family." / "A girl's modesty" (Why Pay Them?) read as lectures aimed at women. These are James's own lines, so they're left for him.
- Mud on Purpose: three readers found the celebrity list long; suggest cutting to three or four examples at the end-of-book pass.
- Ephesians 5:12 in Why Pay Them? is used to mean "expose" while Paul says even speaking of it is shameful; consider rewording.
