## The men who quit the pebble (from "If You Slip", replaced 2026-11-20 by Guillaco's original bleach forum at James's request)

If a slip has left you taking the whole business very solemnly, here's some medicine. Another writer once imagined a support forum for people trying to give up drinking bleach. The idea is borrowed here, with thanks, and the poison is changed. Meet the brave men of a made-up forum, who are trying to stop walking around with a sharp pebble in their shoe.

"Day 19 pebble-free. Walked past a gravel driveway this morning and nearly lost it. Stay strong, brothers."

"I allow myself the pebble on Sundays only. I think that's a healthy balance."

"Honestly, how is a man supposed to stop? I've always got feet. I've always got shoes. The temptation is with me every waking hour."

"I was doing so well. Then I had a stressful week at work and before I knew it I was in the yard, picking one out. A small one. I'm so ashamed. Resetting my counter."

"Does anyone else find the limp sort of comforting?"

"My accountability partner checks my shoes every Friday. So far so good, though I did think about the beach all through the meeting."

You're smiling because you can see what they can't. Nobody needs courage, a counter or a Friday inspection to keep a pebble out of his shoe. All he needs is to notice that it hurts and that it never did a thing for him. Once he's seen that, leaving it out isn't an achievement. It's just walking.

How many times this week were you tempted to put a stone in your shoe? None. That isn't self-control. You see a stone for what it is.

If you slipped, the lesson is not "try harder". Somewhere you started believing again that the pebble had something to offer. The world repeats its lies daily, so it does no harm to hear the truth twice.

### Old version: A Warning

#### A Warning

If you're hoping to be frightened into quitting, you'll be disappointed. There are no horror stories here about your hormones, your hairline, or what a weak and disgusting creature you are. Scare tactics have never freed anyone. If fear worked, you'd have stopped years ago.

Let's be straight about the medicine, too. Doctors will tell you that masturbation doesn't damage your body, and I'm not going to argue with them. **This book isn't about your body.** It's about your mind, your drive, your word and your soul.

I do say that lust is a sin and a thief. I say the habit robs you of drive, focus and self-respect. That is conviction, and it is the testimony of men who've lived it. You are the best witness. You know how you feel ten minutes afterward.

I won't sell you superpowers either. Some corners of the internet promise that quitting will give you a deeper voice, glowing skin and women turning their heads. I promise none of it. What you get is better than a superpower. You get yourself back.

This will sound strange: **you never chose this habit.**

Yes, you choose each time. You lock the door, you pick up the phone, you start. But did you ever sit down as a boy and decide to do this every night for the next fifteen years, whether you wanted to or not? Of course you didn't. Curiosity and puberty started it. Then the habit quietly took the wheel, and for most of us porn later poured fuel on it.

You've walked into a trap. It is one of the most subtle traps there is, because the bait is your own body. No man caught in it wants his son caught in it too. That tells you everything. Every man in the trap wishes he'd never started, and yet every man in the trap carries on. Nobody forces him. So why does he do it?

Imagine a button on your desk. Press it, and tomorrow you wake up as a man who simply never picked up this habit. No urges, no memories of it, no pull. Would you press it? Every man reading this would. Now ask why a man would press a button to be rid of something he supposedly enjoys. Nobody would press it to be rid of fishing.

This book can't press it for you. But it can show you that the button is real, and that it's closer than you think.

##### The only thing stopping you is fear

What keeps you in the trap isn't desire. It's **FEAR**. You fear that stopping means a long, gray stretch of frustration, craving and restless nights. That fear is built on beliefs you picked up along the way:

- "A man needs a release."
- "It's healthy. Doctors say so."
- "I'll go crazy if I don't."
- "It's the only thing that helps me sleep."
- "Everyone does it, and anyone who says he doesn't is lying."

Look at who gains from every line on that list. It isn't you. The squatter wrote it, and he would say that, wouldn't he? It's his dinner.

Believe those for long enough and the fear grows. You fear the nights alone, and the hard days without your little reward. Most of all, you fear you'll never be completely free. You fear that this is just how men are, and that even if you stop, you'll spend the rest of your life fighting it. If you've already tried and failed many times, as most of us have, you'll be sure of it. One man wrote that he had failed so often he sometimes wondered whether God's grace for him had run out.

It hasn't. And you are not a hopeless case. You've only been using a method that can't work.

You may feel nervous right now, as though this isn't the right time. The habit doesn't relieve that nervousness. It causes it. And when exactly were you planning to get out? Tomorrow? Once you're married? After this stressful season? That day never arrives by itself.

##### The good news

The greatest gain of all is losing that fear. It won't happen at once. The fear may even grow as you read. Some men put the book down halfway, because they sense that if they keep going they'll actually have to stop. Don't be one of them.

You may be desperate to stop, or you may dread the very thought. Either way: **YOU HAVE NOTHING TO LOSE.** If you reach the last page and decide you'd rather carry on, nothing stops you. There's no shock treatment. There is only good news.

Jesus told a story about a son who took his inheritance early, left home and wasted every coin of it. People call him the prodigal son. He ended up feeding pigs, and he was starving. In that pigsty he came to himself. He got up and walked home, and he found his father running down the road to meet him. That is how it feels to walk out of this trap. Before this book is finished, that will be you.

##### Two ways to fail

Every man can find it easy to walk away from lust, and that includes you. Your only job is to keep reading, chapter by chapter, with your mind open.

There are only two ways to fail with this method.

**The first is not following the instructions.** Some men find this book annoyingly bossy about certain things, such as not cutting down and not using substitutes. Yes, a few men have quit with those tricks. They managed it in spite of them. A man can also cross town walking backward, but nobody recommends it.

**The second is not understanding.** Don't take anything on trust. Question what you're told, including by this book. Question what you've always assumed about sex, manhood and "needs". If you think it's just a habit, ask why you can drop other habits in a week but not this one. If you think you enjoy it, ask why you can take or leave things you enjoy far more. Why do you *have* to have this? Why does a small panic rise at the words "never again"?

You're about to do what every man in the trap secretly longs to do. You're going to be free, and you have every reason to look forward to it.

One question is left before we start, and this whole book turns on it. Can you be trusted?

##### Why the normal way is so hard

The normal way of quitting goes like this. You write down everything the habit costs you: the guilt, the tiredness, the lost time, the distance from God. Then you tell yourself:

"If I can just hold out long enough, the urge will finally die, and then I'll be happy."

It sounds sensible. Men do get free this way. But it is the hard road, and most men who take it end up where one man on a Christian forum did. He wrote that everything that depended on his own effort failed him sooner or later.

Why is it so hard? There are three reasons.

**1. Stopping was never the problem.** You stop every single time you finish. In that flat, gray minute afterward, you are the most committed quitter on earth. "Never again. I mean it this time." Every man says it, and every man means it. The problem is not that minute. The problem is day three, or day thirty, or day three hundred, when you're tired or lonely and a quiet voice says "just this once". One becomes two, and you're back where you started, minus a little more self-respect. For some men it doesn't even take until day three. A nineteen-year-old, raised in a strict Christian home, wrote about his first time: "I vowed never to do that again, only to find myself searching for a secret place after a couple of hours."

**2. Guilt and fear make you reach for it.** What does a man do when he feels ashamed, anxious and worthless? He looks for comfort. And what has he trained himself to use for comfort? Tell a man the habit is ruining him and you've just handed him one more reason to escape into it tonight. A counselor on a Christian recovery blog saw it clearly: "Shame is the problem for so many, many Christians." This is why the scare videos and the thundering sermons have never freed you. They load the gun. A man who tries to quit out of desperation is doomed to fail, because desperation is the very thing that drives him back to it. That is true of porn. It is just as true of the hand, and of the next woman you chase to feel better about yourself.

**3. Every reason to stop makes stopping harder.** That sounds crazy, so take it slowly. For one thing, your reasons tell you that you *ought* to give something up. So part of you goes on believing there is something to give up: a comfort, a treat, an old friend. You feel like a man being marched away from his only pleasure. And for another, your reasons point your eyes at the wrong question. You don't do it because of the reasons you should stop. Nobody opens the site or shuts the bathroom door *because* it leaves him flat and guilty. The real question is the other one: why do you want to do it at all?

The man from the Christian forum, the one whose own effort failed him, spotted the trap inside all that effort. The harder he tried to stop thinking about porn, the more it became the center of his thoughts. Swap in "masturbation", or "her", and nothing changes.

##### Three questions

So for now we're going to put your reasons for stopping on a shelf. All of them, including the good ones, including the godly ones. We'll take them down again later, when they can no longer be used as a stick to beat you with.

In their place, look straight at what you do and ask:

1. **What is it actually doing for me?**
2. **Do I actually enjoy it?**
3. **Why am I doing this to myself?**

The rest of the book exists to prove one thing to you: lust does nothing for you. Nothing at all. The porn doesn't. The hand doesn't. The chase doesn't.

Be clear about what that means. I'm not saying the costs are bigger than the benefits. Every man already knows that, and it has never helped him. I'm saying there are no benefits.

Not one.

The relaxation, the sleep, the stress relief, the "release", the thrill of something new, the feeling of being wanted: each of them is an illusion. We'll take them apart one at a time.

Paul, the apostle who wrote much of the New Testament, told the Christians in Rome to "be transformed by the renewing of your mind". He didn't say to try harder. "Be transformed" is something done to you, by God, as your mind is made new. If you're not a believer, call it changing your mind. Either way, it starts with seeing clearly.

So start with a question nobody asks. If it does nothing for you, why is it so hard to stop?

**Chase.** He borrows your feet. This is lust doing your thinking around real women. The flirting you shouldn't be doing. The message you shouldn't be sending. The woman on a pedestal, and the money spent to make her notice you.

**Take.** He borrows your bed. Sex outside marriage. The one-night stand, the affair, the sex a man pays for. And one case is not like the others, and it gets a fair hearing in this book. It is the bed a man shares with the woman he loves and hasn't married yet.

Look, think, touch, chase, take. It is a ladder, and each rung leads to the next. It is also the oldest story there is. He saw her. He couldn't stop thinking about her. He went after her. He took her.

You may be feeding all five, or only two. It makes no difference. He is fed five ways, and there is one small belly behind them all. That is why you are not going to fight five battles. There is only one squatter, with whatever grubby friends he has brought home. They all eat from the same plate.

### From Where the Line Is (the next chapter, But I'm Going to Marry Her, covers this man)

#### The right woman and no promise yet

The hardest case is the man who has found the right woman. He loves her and means to marry her, and he asks a reasonable question. How can this be lust?

He is closer to the right side than he has ever been. He has the right woman. What he doesn't have yet is the promise. An engagement is a promise to make one.

There is good news for this man. He is the only man in this book who can cross the line honestly. It takes a ring and a wedding, and the wedding can be small.

Until then, what he keeps for the wedding isn't lost. It is kept. He will be bringing her a man who doesn't need to take, and every wife needs one of those.

### From Can You Be Trusted? (the faith note and credit now sit, shorter, in The Easy Method; the woman note is in the Introduction)

#### What I believe, and what you don't have to

A Christian wrote this book, and it quotes the Bible, because the best pictures of this trap are in there. You don't have to believe a word of it to get out. If you're an atheist, play along. If you hold another faith, bring it with you. When you hear God or sin mentioned, take it as your conscience talking. The trap works the same way on every man, and so does the way out.

The book says plainly what the Bible says: lust is sin. Jesus didn't set the bar at the act. He set it at the look. In Matthew 5:28 he says, "everyone who gazes at a woman to lust after her has committed adultery with her already in his heart".

But lust is not the same as desire. God made desire, and he made it strong. Lust is desire turned into taking: the look you go back for, the woman used as material. This book is against the taking. It has nothing against the desire.

If you are a Christian, hear this early. The word "sin" is here to tell the truth. It is not here to beat you. The same Bible also says, in Romans 8:1, "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus". Hold on to both. Nothing in this book saves anyone. Freedom is God's gift, and all the book can do is clear away the lies that keep a man from taking it.

If you're a woman, you are welcome here. The book says "he" all the way through, but the trap is built the same way for you, and so is the way out. And if you are reading for a son, a husband or a friend, read it yourself first. You can't lead a man out of a trap you don't understand.

Any mistakes in applying it here are mine.

I won't wish you luck, because luck doesn't come into it. Grace does.

Now for the method itself. It begins with the strangest promise anyone has made you about this.

It will be easy.
