| 2026-11-19 | The Man Led by a Woman | Samson's last scene in full. "At a great feast the Philistine rulers had him brought up from the prison to entertain them. A boy led him by the hand to the pillars that held the building up. Then he prayed. 'Lord GOD, remember me, please, and strengthen me, please, only this once'. God heard him. The temple came down on the rulers and on Samson himself. It isn't a tidy ending. He never got back his sight or the wasted years. But God heard a ruined man when he asked, and gave him back the work he was born for. If you have been at the mill for years, he still hears that prayer." (Judges 16:25-30; WEB quotation from 16:28. | 2026-11-20 | Fit for Service | The second full telling of David on the roof: The Bible tells this story about David, Israel's greatest king. It is in the second book of Samuel, and it begins like this. "At the return of the year, at the time when kings go out, David sent Joab, and his servants with him, and all Israel; and they destroyed the children of Ammon, and besieged Rabbah. But David stayed at Jerusalem. At evening, David arose from his bed and walked on the roof of the king's house. From the roof, he saw a woman bathing, and the woman was very beautiful to look at." / This was the man who, as a boy, killed the giant Goliath. No army ever beat him in the field. He was beaten on a rooftop on an idle evening, at the time when kings go out, because he hadn't gone. / The rest is quickly told. He took Bathsheba, the wife of his own soldier Uriah. When Uriah, even drunk, wouldn't go home and cover it up, David sent him back to the war to die. / Notice who was fit for service in that story. Uriah, drunk, still slept at the palace door like a soldier on duty. David, sober, was no use to anybody. Lust had taken the commander off the field without a fight. | Probably nothing: 'A City Without Walls' tells it whole. Kept in case the 2 Samuel 11:1-2 quotation is wanted. |
