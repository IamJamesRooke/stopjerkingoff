# Reader report 2: sixteen avatars, ranked by how many men each fix helps

Written 2026-11-19 from sixteen full reads of the current book (main at 10778fe, 55,618 prose words, Introduction to Attribution). Read only: no chapter was edited. This is input for the Author thread. It follows `READER-REPORT.md`, which dealt with length and tone; this one asks who the book is for and who it loses.

**How to use it.** James's rule: help as many men as possible, keep the focus, do not try to please everyone. So each fix below carries a rough *men helped* score and a *blur risk*. Fixes that help many men and cost the core message nothing come first. Section 5 lists suggestions I do NOT recommend because they would blur the book.

**Caveat.** The avatars are simulated. Their "how common are men like me" guesses are their own hunches (1 to 10), not data. I used them only as weights. Treat the ranking as an ordered hint to be checked against real feedback once the site feedback box is live.

## 2. What the research said (why these men)

- **A 34-country survey** (66,994 people) found the same mechanism in every gender, religion and culture: how often a person watches predicts the problem, and conflict between his values and his use makes it worse. Religiosity added little. Source: [Bőthe et al. 2026 summary](https://culturereframed.org/library-article/a-global-investigation-of-the-moral-incongruence-model-of-pornography-use-across-genders-religions-and-cultures/). Meaning for the book: its core (the loop, no shame, stop feeding it) fits men of any background, and its Christian frame is a *choice of language*, not what makes it work.
- **A 42-country study** (82,243 people) put problematic porn use at 3.2% to 16.6% depending on the test, highest in men, with no difference by sexual orientation; the highest country scores were Taiwan, China, Malaysia, Turkey and Brazil; only 4 to 10% of those affected had sought help and 20 to 40% wanted to. Source: [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/new-research-sheds-light-on-the-global-impact-of-problematic-pornography-use/). Meaning: there are many silent men outside the US/UK, many reading English as a second language, and a lot of men who want help but don't act.
- **Critics' point on NoFap-style forums:** a survey linked heavier forum involvement to stronger worthlessness and suicidal feelings after a relapse ([Public Health Post](https://publichealthpost.org/mental-behavioral-health/reboot-relapse-repeat-the-harm-of-anti-pornography-forums/); one survey, with a limited sample, so a caution rather than a finding). Meaning: the book's "no counter, no zero, you fell forward" approach is on the right side of this, and it is the part avatars liked most.
- Searches also showed active worlds the book only touches: Muslim recovery material (tawba, haram guilt), UK chemsex and compulsive-sex services for gay and bisexual men, and AI girlfriend companionship and loneliness. They informed the roster, not the verdicts.

Details and the quotes behind each:

### 1. Shrink Ch 9 and the repeat David story (helps ~41)
Quit point for eleven of sixteen: teen, redpill, married-secular, creator, ED, widower, virtual girlfriend, Hindu, Muslim, sex-positive and trauma. "Four screens of David, Samson and Ahab with no porn in it." The Muslim reader finds David and Bathsheba told as fact offensive to a prophet. The married man sees David three times (Ch 5, Ch 9, 27b), with Lewis four times and Augustine three times (he guesses about 8,000 words of repeats). The devout reader likes scripture, so do not remove it; keep one story whole, turn the rest into an optional, labelled box or move them to a "for those who want more Scripture" section. This does not blur the thesis (all of them say the book already works without faith).

### 2. A secular decision step the man can say (helps ~40)
The secular script has him say "I have looked at women as things to use. It was wrong." Flagged by teen, gay, married-secular, divorced, creator, escalation, sex-positive, widower, virtual girlfriend. It does not describe the creator-payer, the gay man, the AI user or the married man who believes it harmless, and it forces a confession some will not make. Fix: a step built on costs the man holds himself (sleep, money, marriage, honesty, the man he wants to be), with room for his own words and for his own faith's words (Muslim: a dua; Hindu: a sankalp; Pentecostal: a variant with the Holy Spirit). The Christian prayer stays for Christians. Also change "women" to "people" wherever the script is generic.

### 3. A safety box at the very front, plus real help (helps ~25, severity high)
The only crisis and trauma material sits in the Appendix, page 36 of 38. The trauma/ADHD reader (who has bad nights) and the man disturbed by his escalation would likely quit at Ch 4 or Ch 9, long before it. Needs, in one small box near the Introduction (and a pointer in Ch 14): if you use it to numb pain or after abuse, if urges frighten you or feel out of your control, or if you have dark thoughts, tell a doctor or therapist and do not wait for this book; therapists keep confidences within known limits; crisis help; and a non-US list (the lines are US-only now: 988 and RAINN; Hindu, Nigerian, widower, Muslim and virtual-girlfriend readers all noted it). The escalation avatar suggested an anonymous prevention helpline (Stop It Now); the numbers he gave are from memory and **must be checked before use**. Also say plainly, once, that the book is not a replacement for professional help. The "he's not a monster, he needs a trained person" line lands for him.

### 4. Alternatives to "tell one man before three days" (helps ~21)
Cannot or will not: the Muslim reader (concealing sin is a virtue), the Hindu reader (family shame), the widower (no one to tell), the teen (a minor), the virtual-girlfriend man (isolated), the trauma reader (his therapist is the person to tell), the cheater (the wrong first person). Fix: "tell one person you trust, or a counsellor, doctor, helpline or an online group, and if you are young, a school counsellor or doctor". Keep the principle; widen the door.

### 5. "Evenings and people" (helps ~21, medium blur risk)
The widower, virtual-girlfriend man, creator-payer, red-pill man ("what do I do with the energy"), divorced man and teen all say the book removes the habit and says little about the empty evening and finding people. Ch 25 says "no substitutes" and Ch 26 says "go to the party", which the isolated men can't do. Risk: it can look like a substitute. Fix: a short box that is clearly about *living*, not replacement: three or four plain ways an isolated man finds people and fills an evening (a local group, a class, volunteering, an older men's group, call someone), tied to the book's own line "a feeling is not an order, go and find people". Keep it to a page.

### 6. Plain English and idioms (helps ~14 plus every non-native reader)
Second-language readers named these as stumbling blocks: "thinking with the wrong head", "simping", "pipsqueak", "pilot light", "pebble in your shoe", "load the gun", "icing on the cake", "pew", "chatters", "mashed potatoes", "turn signal and wipers", "edging", "pile-up", "hit", the Screwtape and Chris Rock references, long sentences, and the Chalmers quote. This matches the book's own rule (short sentences, simple words), so it is an application of it. English is a second language for most of the world's searchers and the studies above put the highest problem scores outside the US/UK. Cheap, and no one loses.

### 7. For readers of other faiths and a non-missionary ending (helps ~22)
Muslim, Hindu, redpill, married-secular, gay and teen readers all flagged "Read the Gospel of Mark... pray once" (Ch 33) as a bait and switch and Rule 11 ("pray, read the Bible, be part of a church") as aimed at them. "Atheist, play along" does not cover a believer from another faith. Fix: a short box near the start ("if you hold another faith, bring it; this book will not ask you to convert"), reword Rule 11 to "whatever strengthens your resolve: your faith, your people, your community", keep the Mark invitation as an offer for those who are curious and move it out of the closing page. Add a short plain note on semen-loss myths (the Hindu reader: ojas and brahmacharya are never named).

### 8. "If there is another person" (helps ~8, high harm to others)
The cheater, and to a degree the divorced dater and the married man whose wife half-knows. "Carry on as normal", "already free", "congratulations" and "live the rest normally" never require him to end an affair, tell his wife, get tested or make amends; he could say the words in a hotel room and feel clean. The book's own bible says "carry on as normal never permits hurting someone", but the final step doesn't say so to him. Fix: a short section before the final step (end it today, tell the person you deceived, get tested, freedom is not trust), and name escorts, affair sites and travel in the "types" and "chase" chapters. The wife's side is already in Ch 31.

### 9. Fix rule 4 and soften "easy for every man" (helps ~20, safety)
Rule 4 "Ignore advice that contradicts this method" could lead the trauma, ED, escalation and sex-positive readers, and a devout man with a pastor, to ignore a therapist, doctor or pastor. Replace with "ignore advice to count, ration or test; never ignore your doctor or therapist." Lines flagged as dangerous or false for some: "there is nothing different about you, any man can find it easy" (Ch 13), "will it be harder for me? No." (Ch 24), "absurdly easy" (Ch 11), "free from the first day" (Ch 21), "short, a few hours" (the book is 55,000 words). Do not change the thesis; add "most men find it easier than they feared; if you are carrying trauma, depression or a compulsion, you may need extra help and that is not failure". The trauma reader also lists contradictions to reconcile on one page: no vow vs "never again" vs "decide"; "tell one man" vs "don't announce"; "don't cut down" vs "stop right away"; Ch 5 "that lift is real" vs "you get nothing"; "Don't mope. Rejoice" vs "you can't order yourself to feel glad".

### 10. An under-18 page (helps ~5.5, high stakes)
Teens are likely one of the larger groups. Missing for a minor: what to do if a parent finds out, who to tell (school counsellor, doctor), sextortion addressed to *him* (Ch 30 gives it to a father about "your son"; the appendix has no teen resources), school, grades and sleep named as the cost, and "first 72 hours, phone edition" up front. One page, kept clean.

### 11. "Paying for attention" (helps ~10)
Paying creators appear in one paragraph of Ch 14 and one bullet of Ch 25. Nothing on money, debt, cancel steps, the belief that "she likes me", or chatters, beyond the one fact. The creator-payer is a growing group and mixes this with porn. One short section: the monthly sum written out, how the chat works, cancel and block steps, and a pointer on debt. The two lines that landed ("tips her so that she will write back"; "he is a customer and he is being worked") are the model.

### 12. Do not lose the devout reader (helps ~6, and he is probably the biggest single group)
The devout Nigerian reader finishes and passes it on, but "easy" reads as cheap grace until Ch 21. He is lost by "no vows, no willpower campaign" (Ch 1, 23), the blank page in Ch 10 (he thinks it is a printing error), "business model" for the devil (27b), "Don't mope. Rejoice" and "the guilt dressed as discipline" lines that sound aimed at fasting and vigils, and Rule 4 against his pastor. Needs: an early line that "easy" means grace, not a low standard, and that prayer, fasting and fellowship are welcome as worship; a short scrupulosity box (re-confessing forever is not repentance) near Ch 21 or 29b; a Pentecostal-friendly prayer variant. **Do not cut more church content.** Two of the six biggest groups (the devout and the guilt-driven) want it, and the secular readers' real complaint is *length and placement*, not its existence (see #1 and #7).

### 13. ED, doctors and evidence (helps ~9)
The ED reader's likeliest cause at 29 is performance anxiety, which the book never names; the doctor path is one-liners; there is no named research for the "twice as likely" claim, the death-grip paragraph and the timelines. The red-pill and sex-positive readers also want sources. Fix: a short box "if your body isn't responding" (anxiety cycle, see a doctor first, other causes, honest timeline, talking to your partner) and an evidence appendix listing the sources behind the factual claims and labelling the rest as testimony. This also protects trust with the doubters. The "no medicine" line in the Appendix is not true of Ch 8, 22 and 29 and should be fixed.

### 14. State the single man's sexual ethic once, early (helps ~15, medium-high risk)
The divorced dater, red-pill reader, gay reader, teen and virtual-girlfriend man all reach Ch 29 "What about sex?" ("keep sex for marriage... if you're single, wait") two-thirds in and feel it is the first plain statement of the price. Fix with one short, honest paragraph in the Introduction that says where the book stands and that it defines "the chase" as taking, not wanting a partner. Do NOT add a separate chapter on dating or sexual ethics: it would blur the book's core and cost the core porn reader.

### 15. A note for gay men, and pronouns (helps ~2, almost free)
The book assumes a straight reader throughout; the Introduction's first paragraph, the scripts and Ch 31 shut him out; the Bible stories cast the woman as the lever. Doing #2 (people instead of women in the scripts) fixes most of it. Add one sentence ("this book is about what lust does, not about who you are attracted to") and a resource list that includes chemsex and substance support and sexual-health testing if the Author thinks it worth the space. Do not write a chapter.

## 5. Suggestions I do NOT recommend (they blur the book)

- **A moderation path** ("if you just want to cut down"), asked for by the sex-positive reader. The book's core is that cutting down fails (Ch 12). He himself said making room for moderation would blur it and did not push. Keep his useful points instead: drop the harshest language ("Porn is the crack", "That is prostitution", "IT'S A MASTER, NOT A HABIT") where it is not needed.
- **Stripping out the Christian frame.** The devout and guilt-driven readers are probably the biggest group, and secular readers said removing it was not necessary: they want it shorter, later and labelled.
- **A separate chapter for every type** (ED, gay, AI, widower, Muslim, Hindu, teen, married-secular). Boxes and paragraphs, yes; chapters, no. A book aimed at everyone helps no one.
- **Dropping "lust gives you nothing"** to accommodate men who use it to numb. Keep the claim; change the tone and add the safety box (#3, #9).
- **Kink, fetish or detailed "types of content" treatment.** Handle only through the safety box and the existing escalation line.
- **A full dating guide for single men.** One honest paragraph (#14); the rest is another book.
- **Pentecostal or other church-specific practice** (deliverance, laying on of hands) as chapters. A variant prayer is enough.

## 6. Other findings worth carrying

- **"NOT-ME" runs through nearly every chapter for most avatars.** "You" usually means a married or churchgoing man, a husband or a father (wife, kids, church, pastor, "your partner"). The cheap fix is to vary the "you" in examples and add one single man, one older man, one young man, one isolated man to the stories, not to add chapters.
- **Lowest engagement among avatars:** Ch 7 (for the already-converted), Ch 27b "Fit for Service" (the drunk-soldier frame and "somebody else always pays" read as shaming to the widower, escalation, trauma and Muslim readers), Ch 30 and Ch 31 (written for others; consider moving out of the numbered sequence, as READER-REPORT.md suggested), the Attribution page (use a spoken 40-word credit).
- **Lines flagged as hurting a specific man:** "A man who can't control his lust can't be trusted" (Introduction, Ch 9, Ch 21; hits the widower, trauma reader, creator and others); "Faithful only because nobody offered" (Ch 15); "harem of imaginary brides" (Ch 5); "a rehearsal for being alone" (Ch 6); "single, pimply and broke" (Ch 20); "At seventy-five, a grandfather waiting for the house to empty? Of course you aren't."; "Sex with your wife is the meal" (Ch 31); "Nobody bothers to distract a man who doesn't matter"; "smile and nod, don't sign" (Ch 4); "It is a betrayal, and you may call it that" (Ch 31) for the ED reader; the sextortion suicide statistic in Ch 30 with no warning; "Read the Gospel of Mark" (Ch 33).
- **Move earlier:** 29b "If You Slip" (best chapter for several men, easy to miss) and the 72-hour plan; both should be mentioned in the Introduction.
- **A one-screen "1am" summary** and larger print or short chapters for the widower; fewer idioms for all ESL readers (#6).
- **Truthfulness check:** the Introduction calls the book short ("a few hours") while it is about 55,000 words; the Appendix says the book "stays out of medicine" while Ch 8, 22 and 29 discuss the body.
