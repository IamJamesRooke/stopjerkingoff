# Reader Report 4: nine cold readers plus a non-native English reader

Read cold on main f2e3233 (after the 113-change pass), 69 files, about 64,200 words. Ten Sonnet readers each read the whole book in order, in character. Read only: no chapter was edited. Each reader's raw notes: `notes5/*.md` (churchgoer, nonchristian, skeptic20, relapser, porn-only, hookup, simp, married, engaged, esl). Chapters are cited by file name.

Caveat: one reader per type, so treat counts as signals, not statistics. Main has since moved (6862b14, read-aloud and plain-English passes), so check each item against the current text before fixing.

## Headline

1. **All ten finish. All ten stay past the first 1,500 words, most of them "barely".** The 113-change pass worked on the opening: "Nobody is going to shame you", the button question and "Has any of it worked?" hold every type. None quits in the first chapters.
2. **The one thing that makes men put the book down is the same for six of ten: the flat marriage rule at the moment of decision.** It is the single biggest unfixed issue.
3. **The unmarried partnered man is the book's biggest hole.** Four readers (porn-only, engaged, hookup, non-Christian) say it. No chapter speaks to him; 01c, 14f, 28 and rule 10 only draw a line across his bed.
4. **The new chapters split cleanly.** 00b (A Warning) and 15d (The Back Door) win nearly everyone. 00c (Can You Be Trusted?), 01c (Where the Line Is) and 14f lose or anger most. 14e is "not me" for everyone but the chaser.
5. **Repetition is still the top pace complaint**, even after the cuts: "nothing to give up", five mouths, David, the caller.

### Patterns by cost
1. Phrasal verbs. 2. Double-meaning words. 3. Long metaphor chains. 4. Archaic Bible English. 5. Church vocabulary. 6. Forum slang. 7. US and UK cultural references. 8. Short ironic fragments ("He is only dry."). 9. Long sentences (rare; the real ones are in 03d, 14c, 21).

### Easiest and hardest chapters
Easiest: Just Once, Let It Ring, Who Needs to Know, The Instructions, Will I Miss It, the bleach story in If You Slip.
Hardest: Good News Twice, The Flesh, the body chapters (08b, 22b), Two Wills, The Ladder, Samson, Ahab, the David retellings, The Easy Way to Stop.

### Three changes that would help most
1. Replace the top 15 phrasal verbs and idioms with plain verbs.
2. Define eight forum and church terms in one line each, and drop US names, sports and money words.
3. Shorten the in-text Bible quotes and the Samson, Ahab and Absalom retellings. Add a Brazilian crisis line to the appendix (per the EVERGREEN rule, a line that says to get medical help is fine; a rotting number is not: James's call).
