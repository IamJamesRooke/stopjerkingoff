# Pastor Review 3: after the 2026-11-21 editorial pass

This is the third clergy read, done on main after the 113-change editorial pass (commit f2e3233). I read it as a careful Catholic priest and a careful evangelical pastor would. I read every changed passage in context and re-checked every item from Pastor Review 2 against the current text. Read only: no chapter was edited. Every fix below has ready wording for the Author.

## Verdict

**The book is now theologically sound for both Catholic and Protestant clergy. Nothing is HIGH.** Both HIGH items from review 2 are fixed, and so are most of the mediums:

- The Introduction now tells a man in an affair, paying for sex or pressing a woman to "stop that today".
- "Where the Line Is" has its "One exception" paragraph for when another person is involved.
- The Final Step now restates the line: "the No you're about to say covers any bed outside it."

The new "bed" paragraph in "Where the Line Is" ("It ties two people together. It can make a child. It is the most one body can say to another: all of me, to you.") is the best thing added. A priest will recognize his own theology in it, and an evangelical pastor will preach it.

## Scripture check

I checked all 43 epigraphs and every Bible quotation in the new and changed text against the World English Bible, word for word. All of them match, including the new ones: Matthew 18:22, Psalm 4:8, Genesis 2:18, 2 Corinthians 10:5, Judges 16:5, Luke 18:13, the full James 4:7, and 1 Corinthians 7:3. The only differences are "the LORD" for "Yahweh", which the Attribution page discloses.

## MEDIUM

### M1. "Partner" (open from review 2): 31 uses in the text

The book says sex belongs inside marriage, and then calls the woman beside the reader his "partner" 31 times. To a priest or pastor, that word means a couple living together. It is the one theme that is still out of step with the line.

The general rule: where the context is a bed, a house or children, say **wife**. Elsewhere, say **the woman in your life**. Leave "accountability partner" (Chapter 11) and the sources comments alone.

- **02-why-is-it-difficult-to-stop:** "when his partner is asleep in the next room" → "when his wife is asleep in the next room"

### M2. Ahab chapter: the new opening line reads wrong aloud, and names living together

> "The next man is a quieter case, and more men are like him. This chapter is for the man who lives with a woman. He was a king named Ahab, and his wife was Jezebel."

There are two problems. Read aloud, "He" now points at "the man who lives with a woman", so the listener hears that he was a king named Ahab. And "the man who lives with a woman" (said twice in the chapter, with "the woman he lives with" in "The lever at home") names the living-together case as the audience.

**Ready wording:**

> "The next man is a quieter case, and more men are like him. He was a king named Ahab, and his wife was Jezebel. This chapter is for the married man, and for any man a woman can lead by what he wants."

In "The lever at home": "It matters far more with the woman he lives with." → "It matters far more at home, with his wife."

### M3. "But I'm Going to Marry Her": "It is her decision as much as his."

> "Read this chapter with her, if you like. It is her decision as much as his."

The kindness is right, because she should be part of it. But "it" has no clear subject, and a pastor will hear "whether they wait is up to the two of them." The chapter's own teaching is that the line isn't theirs to move.

**Ready wording:**

> "Read this chapter with her, if you like. Waiting is something the two of you give each other, and she should have a say in how you keep it."

## LOW

- **L1. Now, "What goes in the space":** "The room was never his, and it isn't empty. It's yours. Spend it well." This is a good callback, but the Empty House told Christians that the house "has an owner, and he means to live in it." A pastor will want the last word on the house to agree. **Ready wording:** "The room was never his, and it isn't empty. It's yours, and if you're a Christian, you share it with its owner. Spend it well."
- **L2. The Back Door epigraph** (open from review 2). "All things are lawful for me" was a Corinthian slogan that Paul quotes in order to correct it. As an epigraph with no explanation, it can read as Paul's own teaching. **Ready wording**, one line under the epigraph or early in the chapter: "Some in Corinth had a slogan: 'All things are lawful for me.' Paul let them have it, and then answered it: 'but I will not be brought under the power of anything.'"
- **L3. Driscoll** is still quoted in the Ahab chapter, as James decided, and he is still missing from the Attribution page's list of names. Add him there.
- **L4. The Ladder, C.S. Lewis:** "C.S. Lewis called the habit a harem of imaginary brides." Lewis said the habit sends a man back into the prison of himself to keep a harem of imaginary brides. The Thought Life chapter quotes him correctly. **Ready wording:** "C.S. Lewis said the habit gives a man a harem of imaginary brides."
- **L5. What Your Body Will Do, therapist care.** See M8 in the status table: add "and tell the therapist your convictions."

## New material a pastor will praise

- **The bed paragraph in "Where the Line Is".** Sex as "all of me, to you", and the flat feeling as the body saying something the man doesn't mean. This is the clearest case for chastity in the book, and it works for non-believers.
- **"Make it, and the bed is yours to share. What this chapter said about using her still holds, because lust doesn't stop at a wedding."** This is better than the old line, and true to both traditions.
- **The Empty House:** "He doesn't come as a substitute for the tenant. He was the owner all along."
- **The Final Step:** "A man who has failed fifty times takes this step exactly as a man does who has never tried." This is pure grace, with no works.
- **The Final Step, for non-believers:** "Where I have used anyone, on a screen or off it, that was wrong" now reads naturally.
- **Chalmers:** the line for non-believers ("An old want doesn't leave an empty heart. It leaves when there is something you love more") keeps the sermon's meaning and doesn't drop the believer's version.
- **The church guy and seventy times seven.** Accurate, and pastorally exact.
- **Martin Luther** is now identified where he appears, so a Catholic reader isn't left guessing.
