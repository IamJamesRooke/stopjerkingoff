# Frequently Asked Questions {#faq -}

Short, straight answers to the questions men ask most about quitting masturbation, quitting porn and conquering lust, with links to the chapters that go deeper. The whole book is on this site, and it takes a few evenings to read. If you want the longer story of why it was written, read [Why this book exists](why-this-book.html).

## What is this book about? {-}

Conquering lust. Lust is fed in five ways: a man looks, thinks, touches, chases and takes. So you stop watching porn, stop masturbating, and stop making bad decisions with women because you're thinking with the wrong head. One method deals with all five. See [The Empty House](the-empty-house.html). A man ruled by lust is like a soldier who shows up for duty drunk. He is still loved, and he is unfit for service. The book gets you fit. See the [Introduction](index.html) and [Fit for Service](fit-for-service.html).

## How do I stop masturbating? {-}

Change the way you think about it, and stopping becomes easy. Most men try to quit by forcing themselves to go without something they still believe they want. That is a fight you lose sooner or later. This book takes the opposite route, the one Allen Carr used for smoking and EasyPeasy uses for porn. It takes apart, one by one, the beliefs that keep you going back: that it relaxes you, that it helps you sleep, that it's harmless, that you'll miss it. Once you see that it gives you nothing, there's nothing left to resist. Start with [The Easy Method](the-easy-method.html) and read through to [The Rules in One Place](the-instructions.html).

## Can I stop without willpower? {-}

Yes, that's the aim of the whole method. Willpower means you still want it and you're holding yourself back, so every day feels like a loss and the urge wins in the end. When you no longer want it, you don't need willpower. [The Willpower Method](the-willpower-method.html) explains why gritting your teeth keeps failing, and [The Easy Way to Stop](the-easy-way-to-stop.html) explains what to do instead.

## Why is it so hard to stop masturbating? {-}

Because of what you believe about it, not because of any weakness in you. The habit feeds a small, real itch. The book calls it the Little Monster. Behind it sits a much bigger set of lies about what the habit does for you. That is the Big Monster. The lies make it feel like a comfort and a reward, so giving it up feels like a sacrifice. See [Why Is It Difficult to Stop?](why-is-it-difficult-to-stop.html) and [The Flesh](the-flesh.html).

## Is masturbation harmful? {-}

Doctors say masturbation doesn't damage your body, and this book doesn't argue with them. The book is about something else: what the habit does to your mind, your drive, your word and your soul. That part is conviction and the testimony of men who lived it, not a medical finding. Where doctors do have something to add, as with men who find they can't finish with a real partner, the book says so. See [What It's Doing to You](what-its-doing-to-you.html).

## Is masturbation a sin? {-}

Yes. The book says so plainly, because lust is sin, and for almost every man the two go together. Jesus said that looking at a woman to lust after her is adultery in the heart (Matthew 5:28). But it never leaves you there. "There is therefore now no condemnation to those who are in Christ Jesus" (Romans 8:1). Desire itself is not the sin. It's God's idea. Noticing that a woman is beautiful is not a fall. The book's final step is a prayer, with a plain statement beside it for the man who doesn't pray. See [The Flesh](the-flesh.html) and [The Final Step](the-final-step.html#the-prayer).

## I'm not religious. Will this book still work for me? {-}

Yes. You don't have to be a Christian to use it. When the book mentions God or sin, read it as your conscience talking. If you hold another faith, bring it with you. Nobody will ask you to convert, and the final step has a version for the man who doesn't pray. The trap works the same way on a believer and an unbeliever, and so does the way out. See [Will It Be Harder for Me?](will-it-be-harder-for-me.html)

## How is this different from NoFap? {-}

NoFap-style quitting usually means a streak counter, a day count, a reset after every slip, and a lot of willpower. This book has no counter and no streak. Counting days keeps the habit at the center of your life and turns every bad night into a disaster. It also skips the forum promises about superpowers and 90-day badges, because a reward you're waiting for keeps you thinking about what you gave up. See [The counter](the-counter.html), [The streak counter](the-ladder.html) and [Wrong Reasons to Stop](avoid-false-incentives.html).

## Do I have to stop while I'm reading? {-}

You don't have to start a willpower campaign. Don't make a vow you'll break by Friday, start a streak or cut down. Just keep reading until you've finished the whole book. Nobody is telling you to sin. A willpower campaign halfway through a book that's about to make willpower unnecessary only gets in the way. If you fall while you're reading, don't despair, and don't stop reading. If the wish to do it fades as you read, let it. And if you're ready to stop today, stop today. If you've already stopped, don't start again for the book's sake. And read the chapters in order without skipping, because each one builds on the last. See [The Easy Method](the-easy-method.html).

## Should I cut down gradually? {-}

No. Cutting down keeps the habit precious. You spend all day waiting for the time you've allowed yourself, and each one feels like a reward. It makes the trap tighter, not looser. See [Beware of Cutting Down](beware-of-cutting-down.html) and [Just Once](just-once.html).

## I've quit porn but I still masturbate. Is that enough? {-}

No. Porn and masturbation feed each other, and "porn-free but still doing it" usually means the fantasy has just moved into your head. The book deals with the thought life too. See ["Porn-free but still doing it"](the-ladder.html) and [Fantasy and the Thought Life](fantasy-and-the-thought-life.html).

## How do I stop watching porn? {-}

The same way. Porn is fully covered in this book. It's the same trap with a screen attached, and it goes with the rest, not afterward and not gradually. Every lie the book takes apart about masturbation has a twin about porn: that it's harmless, that it teaches you something, that nobody gets hurt. This book was inspired by [EasyPeasy](https://easypeasymethod.org/), which deals with porn alone. See [The lies about porn and about women](the-lies-about-porn-and-about-women.html) and [More questions](porn-and-sex.html)

## What does "thinking with the wrong head" mean? {-}

It means lust is making your decisions about women. You chase the wrong one, stay with the wrong one, spend money to impress one, or throw away a marriage for one. It doesn't feel like lust at the time. It feels like love, or luck. A man who has mastered lust can't be led around by it, and he can see a woman as a person again. See [Thinking with the wrong head](crossing-the-red-line.html#thinking-with-the-wrong-head) and [Fantasy and the Thought Life](fantasy-and-the-thought-life.html).

## What about semen retention? {-}

Once you've stopped, you're already doing it. The book only asks you to notice what happens. Men report more energy, confidence and focus, but those are reports, not proof. The book makes no claims about testosterone or superpowers, and there's no magic day when you level up. See [Try it and see](what-your-body-will-do.html#try-it-and-see).

## What happens in the first few weeks? {-}

You may feel an occasional urge. Some of it is the leftover itch, the Little Monster going hungry, which is milder than missing lunch and fades quickly. Some of it is old cues ringing the bell: the bed, the shower, the empty house, the phone at midnight. A cue is only a reminder, and it wears out fast if you don't treat it as a crisis. Don't mope and don't fight it. Name it instead: that's the Little Monster dying, and isn't it marvelous to be nearly rid of it? See [The First Weeks](the-first-weeks.html).

## Are wet dreams or morning erections a relapse? {-}

No. A wet dream involves no decision, so it is not a sin and not a slip. Change the sheets and carry on. Morning erections are just plumbing. Ordinary desire isn't a relapse either, and neither is a flat spell when your desire seems to switch off for a while. Just don't "check that everything still works". See [Things that are not a relapse](what-your-body-will-do.html#four-things-that-are-not-a-slip).

## What if I slip? {-}

Confess, get up, and find the lie you started believing again. One slip is not a life sentence, and you don't reset anything because there's no counter. The dangerous thought is "I've ruined it, so I may as well carry on." The book doesn't use the word "relapse" because it turns one stupid act into an identity. See [If You Slip](if-you-slip.html).

## I'm married. Does this apply to me? {-}

Yes. Many men thought the wedding would end it and found it came with them on the honeymoon. Sex within marriage is a gift, and this book is not against sex. It's against a counterfeit of it that takes from your wife what you promised her. See [The married man](at-least-im-not-him.html#the-married-man).

## Will I miss it? {-}

No. You can't miss something that never gave you anything. What you'll notice is the time and the clear head you get back. Hard days get easier to handle, and good days are properly good. See [Will I Miss It?](will-i-miss-it.html), [What Am I Giving Up?](what-am-i-giving-up.html) and [Getting Your Time Back](getting-your-time-back.html).

## Should I block my phone and avoid temptation? {-}

There's no single rule, because it depends on your life. Avoid two opposite mistakes. One is the fortress, where you ban the internet and look at your shoes, which only tells you you've given up something great. The other is the dare, like lying in bed at midnight with the phone on your chest to prove you're strong. Charge the phone in the kitchen because bed is a stupid place for it, not because you're in danger. Flee what's stupid and live the rest normally. See [Should I Avoid Temptation?](should-i-avoid-temptation.html) and [The phone in the bedroom](should-i-avoid-temptation.html#the-phone-in-the-bedroom).

## Who wrote it? {-}

It's published anonymously, because the book is about you, not the person who wrote it. It was inspired by EasyPeasy and Allen Carr's *Easy Way to Stop Smoking*, and it quotes real testimonies from men who stopped. See the [Attribution](attribution.html) page for every source.

## How can I help spread it? {-}

Send the link to one man. That's the whole job. Lust has been made normal, and most men have never been told there's an easy way out. If the book helped you, you now know something he needs. You can also copy it, print it, translate it or post it anywhere, because the license allows all of that. See [Pass it on](now.html#pass-it-on).

## How can I help my son, husband or friend stop? {-}

Don't lecture him or corner him. He already knows. Show him it can be left, mention the book once, plainly and kindly, and leave it where he can find it. There are sections written for wives, parents and pastors. See [For the People Beside Him](advice-to-non-users.html), especially [To wives](advice-to-non-users.html#to-wives) and [To parents](advice-to-non-users.html#to-parents).

## I'm struggling with more than a habit. Where can I get help? {-}

If you're in crisis or thinking about harming yourself, please reach out now. The appendix says where to turn and who to ask. See [If you are in crisis](appendix-resources.html#if-you-are-in-crisis).
